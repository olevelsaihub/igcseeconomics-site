Economics / Draw: show the shift and label the new equilibrium (3 marks)

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Economics · 4EC1

Draw: show the shift and label the new equilibrium (3 marks)

Pearson's meaning: draw a diagram with two or three separate points that earn marks. In 4EC1 you are given a supply and demand diagram (a product, labour or currency market) and asked to draw the effect of a change, labelling the new curve, the new equilibrium price (or wage or exchange rate) and the new quantity.

Command word: DrawUsually 3 marksAbout 3 minutes

What Pearson asks

Pearson's meaning: draw a diagram with two or three separate points that earn marks. In 4EC1 you are given a supply and demand diagram (a product, labour or currency market) and asked to draw the effect of a change, labelling the new curve, the new equilibrium price (or wage or exchange rate) and the new quantity.

How the marks are earned

Three AO2 marks: 1 for shifting the correct curve in the correct direction and labelling it, 1 for the new equilibrium price (or wage or exchange rate) shown and labelled on the vertical axis, and 1 for the new equilibrium quantity shown and labelled on the horizontal axis. An unlabelled shift or point does not earn its mark.

Method

  1. Decide which curve moves: does the change affect buyers (demand) or sellers (supply)?

    Why: Shifting the wrong curve loses all the marks that follow.

  2. Decide the direction: more supplied or demanded at every price = right; less = left.

    Why: The direction decides whether price rises or falls.

  3. Draw the new curve parallel to the old one and label it, e.g. S1 or D1.

    Why: Mark 1 needs a labelled new curve.

  4. Mark where the new curve crosses the unchanged curve. Draw dashed lines to both axes.

    Why: This shows the new equilibrium clearly.

  5. Label the new price (P1, W1 or ER1) on the vertical axis and the new quantity (Q1) on the horizontal axis.

    Why: Marks 2 and 3 need these labels.

Answer frame

Shift: (curve) shifts (left/right) from (S) to (S1). New equilibrium: price falls/rises from Pe to P1; quantity rises/falls from Qe to Q1. All new items labelled.

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Shifting demand when the change affects producers' costs (such as a subsidy or tariff), or the other way round.
  • Shifting the curve in the wrong direction; a subsidy shifts supply right, a tariff shifts supply left.
  • Drawing the new curve but not labelling it.
  • Not marking or labelling the new price and quantity on the axes.
  • Moving along the curve instead of shifting it.

Worked illustration

November 2025 · Paper 2 · Q3(c)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (c).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A subsidy is money from the government to producers, which lowers their costs, so they supply more childcare at every price. On Figure 3 I draw a new supply curve to the right of S, parallel to it, and label it S1. Where S1 crosses the original demand curve D is the new equilibrium. From this point I draw dashed lines across to the price axis and down to the quantity axis. The new price, labelled P1, is below Pe. The new quantity, labelled Q1, is to the right of Qe. So the subsidy lowers the price of childcare and increases the quantity bought.

Why it earns the marks

1 mark for supply shifted right and labelled S1, 1 mark for the lower price labelled P1, and 1 mark for the higher quantity labelled Q1.
