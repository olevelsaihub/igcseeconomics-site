Economics / Explain: one point, two linked expansions, in context (3 marks)

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Economics · 4EC1

Explain: one point, two linked expansions, in context (3 marks)

Pearson's meaning: give a statement of fact, then two further expansion points. The expansions may build on each other or both come from the same fact. The question places the answer in context (a country, industry or group), and your answer must use it.

Command word: ExplainUsually 3 marksAbout 3 minutes

What Pearson asks

Pearson's meaning: give a statement of fact, then two further expansion points. The expansions may build on each other or both come from the same fact. The question places the answer in context (a country, industry or group), and your answer must use it.

How the marks are earned

Three AO2 marks: 1 for naming a relevant point, 1 for developing it, and 1 for making the answer fit the context named in the question (the country, firm or group). A general answer that ignores the context usually loses that mark.

Method

  1. Name ONE point: 'One advantage / impact / way is ...'.

    Why: Mark 1.

  2. Expand it with 'This means ...' explaining how or why.

    Why: Mark 2.

  3. Expand again with '... so ...' that reaches the result for the person, firm or country named in the question.

    Why: Mark 3 is for fitting the context; name it and link to it.

  4. Keep to one point and about three sentences.

    Why: Extra points are not rewarded.

Answer frame

One (advantage / impact / way) is ... . This means ... , so (named business / country / group) ... .

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Naming the point but not expanding it (1 mark only).
  • Never mentioning the context (country, industry or group) named in the question.
  • Giving two separate points instead of one point with two linked expansions.
  • Expansions that repeat the point in different words instead of moving the reasoning on.

Worked illustration

November 2025 · Paper 2 · Q1(g)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (g).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One advantage of free trade is access to a much larger market. With no tariffs or quotas, an Icelandic business's goods are not made dearer when they enter other countries, so it can sell to millions of customers abroad rather than only Iceland's small home population. This lets the business increase its sales and revenue and grow.

Why it earns the marks

1 mark for the point (a larger market), 1 mark for the expansion (no tariffs or quotas, so its goods can be sold abroad without extra cost), and 1 mark for the context (an Icelandic business with a small home market increases its sales).
