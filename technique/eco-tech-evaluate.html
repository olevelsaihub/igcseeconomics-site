Economics / Evaluate: balanced argument and a supported conclusion (12 marks)

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Economics · 4EC1

Evaluate: balanced argument and a supported conclusion (12 marks)

Pearson's meaning: write a longer answer that applies what you know from the specification and ends with a conclusion backed by reasons about the economic situation in the case. It is the last part of each 4EC1 paper and always uses the case data. You must argue both sides and finish with a clear, reasoned judgement.

Command word: EvaluateUsually 12 marksAbout 14 minutes

What Pearson asks

Pearson's meaning: write a longer answer that applies what you know from the specification and ends with a conclusion backed by reasons about the economic situation in the case. It is the last part of each 4EC1 paper and always uses the case data. You must argue both sides and finish with a clear, reasoned judgement.

How the marks are earned

Twelve marks in three equal parts: 4 for AO2 (economic terms and theory), 4 for AO3 (using the data in logical chains of reasoning) and 4 for AO4 (evaluation). Marked on levels 1-4, 5-8 and 9-12. One viewpoint only stays in Level 1. The top level needs accurate knowledge, coherent chains using the data, a balanced argument and a conclusion that is supported by reasons.

Method

  1. Write one sentence defining the key term and one sentence picking out the key data.

    Why: This sets up AO2 and AO3 from the start.

  2. Write two developed chains for the claim, each linked to the data (see the Analyse card).

    Why: Depth on the 'for' side earns the AO2 and AO3 marks.

  3. Write two developed chains against, each starting 'However, ...' and using the data.

    Why: A balanced argument is needed for Level 3.

  4. Add at least one 'it depends on ...' point (time, size of the change, how the money is used).

    Why: Shows weighing, which is what evaluation means.

  5. Write a conclusion: answer the question directly, give the main reason, and say what it depends on.

    Why: A supported judgement is required for the top level.

  6. Plan your time: about 15 minutes, roughly a page and a half.

    Why: It is the biggest single part on the paper.

Answer frame

Term means ... . The data shows ... . Benefit 1: ... so ... which leads to ... . Benefit 2: ... so ... . However, ... because ..., so ... . Also, ... . It depends on ... . Conclusion: overall ... because ... ; this is most likely if ... .

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Writing only benefits, with no 'however' side.
  • Ending without a conclusion, or with a conclusion that just repeats earlier points.
  • A judgement that is not backed by a reason from the case.
  • General points that never use the data, which limits AO3.
  • Many short undeveloped points instead of fewer well-linked chains.

Worked illustration

November 2025 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

The complete original question is shown; the illustration answers part (c).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Government expenditure is money the government spends on public services such as healthcare. The data says the UK Government has promised more funding and higher pay for doctors over five years because the public health system is struggling and under heavy strain.

Benefit 1: more spending on healthcare raises demand for medical staff, medicines and equipment. Hospitals can recruit more doctors and nurses, and higher pay makes it easier to keep them, so staff shortages ease. More jobs means lower unemployment, and these workers spend their wages, which raises spending and output across the economy.

Benefit 2: with more staff, hospitals can treat patients sooner, so waiting lists fall. People who are ill can recover faster and return to work, and fewer days are lost to sickness. A healthier workforce is more productive, so each worker produces more, which supports long-term economic growth.

However, the extra spending has to be paid for. Because UK healthcare is funded through taxes, the government may raise taxes or borrow more. Higher taxes leave households with less money to spend, which can slow growth, and higher borrowing adds to government debt. There is also an opportunity cost: money spent on hospitals cannot be spent on education or transport, which also support growth.

The benefits also depend on how well the money is used. If much of it goes on pay without improving how hospitals are run, waiting times may not fall much. Training new doctors takes years, so some benefits will only arrive in the long run.

Conclusion: increasing healthcare spending is likely to bring real economic benefits for the UK, mainly through a healthier and more productive workforce, because the system is currently under strain and staff shortages are holding back treatment. However, the size of the benefit depends on how efficiently the money is spent and how it is funded; if it is funded by large tax rises, the gains will be smaller.

Why it earns the marks

Level 3 (9-12). AO2: government expenditure, productivity, opportunity cost and taxation used correctly. AO3: chains use the extra funding, doctors' pay and the strained system. AO4: two costs, a 'depends on' point and a supported conclusion.
