Idea. Imagine you want a new phone on a loan. If the interest on the loan goes up, you might wait. And if your savings account pays more, you might save instead of spending. The central bank is the country's monetary authority. It sets interest rates, manages the money supply, regulates the banks, aims to control inflation, and lends to banks as a last resort. Monetary policy means the central bank changing interest rates, or the amount of money, to influence total demand in the economy. In a boom, demand races ahead and prices rise fast, so the central bank raises rates to cool it. In a recession, it cuts rates to lift spending and jobs.

Method. Step one. Define monetary policy and name the central bank. Step two. State the direction: raise rates to cut inflation, cut rates to raise output and jobs. Step three. Build the chain. Step four. Add the exchange rate route. Higher rates attract money from abroad, so the currency rises. Imports get cheaper, which eases inflation.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2023. In December 2022 the U S Federal Reserve raised rates for the seventh time that year, to four point five per cent, the highest in fifteen years. Analyse how monetary policy can help reduce inflation. Six marks. Use the data. Rates reached four point five per cent, and were forecast to rise to five point two five per cent by the end of 2023. Chain one. Borrowing becomes dearer. Households take fewer loans, and firms delay investment. Total demand falls, easing demand-pull inflation. Chain two. With rates at a fifteen-year high, saving is more rewarding. Americans save more and spend less, cutting demand further. Chain three. Higher U S rates attract savings from abroad, so the dollar rises. Imports become cheaper, easing cost-push inflation. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying the government sets interest rates. It is the central bank. Another is getting the direction wrong. To reduce inflation, rates go up, not down.

Your turn. Now you try. Unemployment is high in Country V, so its central bank cuts the interest rate from six per cent to three per cent. These are example figures. Explain how this could reduce unemployment. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The rate halves, from six per cent to three. Borrowing becomes much cheaper, and saving is less rewarding. So households borrow and spend more, and firms invest more. Total demand rises. Firms produce more output and hire more workers, so unemployment falls.
