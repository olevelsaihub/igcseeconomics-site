Imagine a law that lets only one bus company run in your town. Fares are high and there is no choice. If the government removes that rule, new bus firms can enter, and fares may fall. Deregulation means removing or reducing government rules and controls on firms and markets. It is a supply-side policy: it aims to raise competition and the productive capacity of the economy. Letting more firms, including foreign firms, into a market is deregulation. So is relaxing employment rules. Adding a minimum wage, licences or safety laws is the opposite.

Step one, define it. Step two, chain an advantage. Fewer rules mean lower costs and easier entry, so there is more competition and investment, then lower prices, more jobs and growth. On a diagram, lower costs and new firms increase supply. Supply shifts right, from S to S one. Price falls and quantity rises. Step three, chain a disadvantage. Rules often protect people. Remove them, and workers may lose job security, conditions may become unsafe, and pollution may rise. Step four, apply it to the sector named in the case. Step five, for Assess, weigh it up. Will firms really hire? What protections are lost? Would other policies work better? Then judge.

Here is a real question from November 2020. South Korea's unemployment for people aged fifteen to twenty-nine rose to eleven point six per cent. Experts advised cutting employment rules in finance, transport and tourism. Assess the effectiveness of deregulation in reducing unemployment. Nine marks. Pick out the data: eleven point six per cent of young people unemployed, reports of too many rules, and three named service sectors. Employment rules add to the cost and risk of hiring. Relax them, and tourism and transport firms find it cheaper to hire, so they take on more staff, including young people. Removing rules that limit entry into finance lets new firms in. They need staff, so jobs are created, and more competition may lower prices. But firms may keep the savings as profit instead of hiring. Lost rules can mean less job security or unsafe work. And youth unemployment may have other causes, like a skills mismatch, so training could work better. Judge. Deregulation could cut youth unemployment in these sectors if rules really are the main barrier to hiring. But the effect is uncertain, so it works best alongside other supply-side policies. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is confusing deregulation with privatisation. Privatisation sells state-owned firms. Deregulation removes rules. Another is picking a new rule, such as a minimum wage, as an example. That adds a rule, so it is the opposite.

Now you try. An example country removes the rule that allowed only two taxi firms in its capital. It also relaxes rules on drivers' working hours. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage. Pause here and work it out.

Advantage: new taxi firms can enter, so supply shifts right. More competition means lower fares and more rides, and the new firms create jobs for drivers. Disadvantage: with no limit on working hours, drivers may work very long shifts. Tired drivers are less safe, so workers and passengers lose protection. Remember: define deregulation, chain an advantage through costs and competition, chain a disadvantage through lost protection, and apply it to the case. Now try the practice questions.
