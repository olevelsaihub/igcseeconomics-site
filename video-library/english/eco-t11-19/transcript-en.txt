Imagine the government owns your city's bus company. One day it sells the company to private investors. Now the buses are run by a private firm that wants to make a profit. Privatisation is the sale or transfer of a government-owned firm to private investors. It moves from the public sector to the private sector. The opposite is nationalisation. Privatisation affects many stakeholders: the government, the firm, consumers, workers and rival firms. Each is affected differently.

Step one, define privatisation. Step two, pick the stakeholder named in the question. The government gets sale revenue but loses control. The firm gets investment and freedom. Consumers may get lower prices, or face a private monopoly. Workers may lose jobs. Step three, explain the effect through the profit motive. New owners want profit, so they cut waste and invest, so efficiency rises. Step four, use the case. For nine- and twelve-mark answers, weigh the gains against risks such as a private monopoly, job cuts or price rises, then judge.

Here is a real question from June 2024. In 2023, Portugal's government-owned airline, TAP Air, looked for investors so it could be privatised. Analyse the possible advantages of privatisation for TAP Air. Six marks. Define it. Privatisation means selling a government-owned firm to private investors. TAP Air would move from the Portuguese Government to private owners. Advantage one, investment. Investors put in new money, so TAP Air can buy newer aircraft or open new routes. It sells more flights and its revenue rises. Advantage two, the profit motive. The owners cut waste and plan schedules better, so the cost of each flight falls. TAP Air can then earn more profit or charge lower fares. Advantage three, freedom. With no government owner, decisions are not slowed by bureaucracy or changed for political reasons, so managers react quickly. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is defining privatisation as a firm becoming a private limited company. It means moving from government to private ownership. Another is claiming prices must fall. A private monopoly could raise them.

Now you try. A government sells its state-owned electricity company to private investors. Explain one possible effect on the company's workers. Pause here and work it out.

The new owners want profit, so they look for ways to cut costs. Some jobs may be cut, or pay held down, so some workers lose income or job security. Remember: define privatisation, pick the stakeholder, explain through the profit motive, and use the case. Now try the practice questions.
