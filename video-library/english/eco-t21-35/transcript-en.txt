Idea. Drive a car faster and you get there sooner, but you burn more fuel. You cannot have top speed and the lowest fuel use at the same time. Governments face choices like this. A government has six macroeconomic objectives: low unemployment, economic growth, low and stable inflation, a balance or surplus on the current account, protecting the environment, and redistributing income. A trade-off is a choice between two objectives, where getting more of one means getting less of the other. For example, unemployment falls from six to four per cent, but inflation rises from two to four per cent. There are four main trade-offs. Lower unemployment against low inflation. Faster growth against low inflation. Growth against the environment. And inflation against the current account, because higher prices make exports less competitive.

Method. Step one, name the two objectives correctly. Step two, explain the chain by which gaining one harms the other. Step three, give a second route if you can, and apply it to the country's data. Route one, demand-pull. When unemployment falls, more people earn wages and spend more. If demand grows faster than output, firms raise prices. Route two, cost-push. With fewer spare workers, firms must offer higher wages to hire. Wages are a cost, so firms pass them on as higher prices.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2019. In Vietnam, unemployment fell between 2015 and 2016, but inflation rose. Analyse how the fall in unemployment may have caused the rise in inflation. Six marks. Route one. As unemployment fell, more people in Vietnam earned a regular wage. Households spent more. When demand grew faster than output, firms raised prices: demand-pull inflation. Route two. With fewer spare workers, Vietnamese firms competed for staff with higher wages. Higher costs were passed on as higher prices: cost-push inflation. Conclude. Both routes push Vietnam's inflation up as unemployment falls. This shows the trade-off between low unemployment and low inflation. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is getting the direction wrong. Lower unemployment tends to raise inflation, not reduce it. Another is listing two objectives with no link. Explain why gaining one harms the other.

Your turn. Now you try. A country's economy is growing at seven per cent a year, and its factories and power stations are expanding fast. Explain one trade-off between economic growth and protecting the environment. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Growing at seven per cent means much more output. More factories and power stations use more resources and cause more pollution. So faster growth harms the environment. Remember: name both objectives, explain the chain that links them, add a second route, and apply it to the country. Now try the practice questions.
