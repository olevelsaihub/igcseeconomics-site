Think of a family that sells homemade cakes to neighbours and buys groceries from a shop. Money comes in, and money goes out. A country keeps the same kind of record with the rest of the world. Exports are goods or services sold to other countries, so money flows in. Imports are goods or services bought from other countries, so money flows out. The current account is the part of the balance of payments that records trade in goods and services with other countries. It also includes income, such as profits earned abroad, and transfers, such as money sent home by workers. Trade in goods, things you can touch, is visible trade. Trade in services is invisible trade. Tourism is a service, so it is invisible. If exports are worth more than imports, the current account is in surplus. If imports are worth more than exports, it is in deficit. Here, with example figures, exports are fifty billion and imports forty billion: a surplus.

Step one. Is it a good or a service? That gives visible or invisible. Step two. Follow the money from the named country's point of view. In is an export. Out is an import. Step three. Put them together. Try it. Kenyans go on holiday in South Africa. Holidays are a service, so invisible. Kenyan money goes out to South Africa. So, for Kenya, it is an invisible import. Step four, for a definition. Give two ideas: what is recorded, and who it is with.

Here is a real question from June 2024. What is meant by the current account on the balance of payments? Two marks. Idea one: it is a record of the value of a country's exports and imports of goods and services. Idea two: these are transactions with other countries. Put together: the current account is the part of the balance of payments that records the value of a country's exports and imports of goods and services with other countries. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is calling tourism visible trade. Tourism is a service, so it is invisible. Another is confusing the current account with the government's budget. It records trade with other countries, not taxes and spending.

Now you try. For Malaysia, classify two items. A: Malaysia sells palm oil to India. B: Malaysians stay in hotels in Japan. Then C: exports are eighty billion and imports ninety-five billion, as example figures. Surplus or deficit? Pause here and work it out.

A: palm oil is a good, and money comes into Malaysia, so it is a visible export. B: a hotel stay is a service, and money leaves Malaysia, so it is an invisible import. C: imports, at ninety-five billion, are worth more than exports, at eighty billion. So the current account is in deficit, by fifteen billion. Remember: good or service, money in or out of the named country, then compare exports with imports. Now try the practice questions.
