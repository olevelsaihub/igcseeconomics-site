Read the narration

Imagine three neighbouring countries agree to stop charging tariffs on goods they sell to each other. A phone made in one can now be sold in the others more cheaply. So what is a trading bloc? It is a group of countries that cut or remove the trade barriers between them, such as tariffs and quotas. Members do not need the same currency, tax rates, language or borders, and they can still trade with other countries. Past papers use real blocs: A f C F T A in Africa, A S E A N, Mercosur, and R C E P, C P T P P and A P T A in the Asia-Pacific. When a member stops charging tariffs on imports from other members, those imports become cheaper to supply. Supply shifts right, so the price falls and more is bought.

Step one, define a trading bloc. Step two, check whose view the question wants, the country, its firms or its consumers, and whether it asks for benefits or drawbacks. Step three, develop each effect as a chain. No tariffs make exports cheaper in member countries, so firms sell more, bringing growth and jobs. Drawbacks chain too. Firms from lower-cost members compete freely, so domestic firms may lose profits, close, and cut jobs. Step four, use the bloc data: members, partner countries, market size. Step five, for Assess or Evaluate, set benefits against drawbacks, and judge what the outcome depends on.

Here is a real question from November 2021. India is a member of the Asia-Pacific Trade Agreement, A P T A, which has five members including Bangladesh, China, South Korea and Sri Lanka. Analyse the benefits for India of being in a trading bloc. Six marks. The text above part b says A P T A has five members. Figure 4, on the page before this part, shows foreign direct investment, or F D I, into India rising from forty-five point one four billion dollars in 2016 to sixty-four point three seven billion in 2019. Benefit one. With lower tariffs, Indian goods are cheaper in Bangladesh, China, South Korea and Sri Lanka. So Indian firms export more, revenue rises, and jobs are created in India. Benefit two. Selling to a bigger market lets Indian firms produce more. They gain economies of scale, so average costs fall, and India can specialise in what it does best. Benefit three. Imports from members such as China and South Korea face lower tariffs. So prices in India fall and consumers get more choice. Benefit four. Firms based in India can export easily to other members, so multinationals want to invest there. This may help explain the rise in F D I, of about nineteen billion dollars. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is saying members must share a currency, or can only trade with each other. Neither is true. Another is giving general free-trade points. Link each point to the named bloc and its members.

Now you try. An example country joins a trading bloc of twelve countries. Its car makers have higher costs than car makers in other members. Analyse one benefit and one drawback for the country's firms. Pause and decide.

Benefit: firms can sell to the eleven other members with no tariffs, so exports and sales rise. Drawback: lower-cost car makers from members can now sell freely here, so the country's car makers lose sales and profits, and may cut jobs. Remember: define the bloc, check whose view and which side, chain each effect, and use the bloc's data. Now try the practice questions.
