Think of a strawberry farm. A sunny summer gives a huge crop, so the farm can sell more at every price. A storm ruins the crop, and it can sell less. A shift in supply means producers offer more, or less, at every price. It is caused by something other than the good's own price. An increase moves supply right. A decrease moves it left. Lower costs, better technology, subsidies and good weather shift supply right. Higher costs, indirect taxes, fewer subsidies and bad weather shift it left. With demand unchanged, supply right means price falls and quantity rises. Supply left means price rises and quantity falls.

Step one, identify the supply factor. Step two, explain how it changes costs or output. Step three, state the direction, then the new price and quantity, with figures from the case.

Here is a real question from November 2025. After years of heatwaves and drought, Greece, Spain and Tunisia expect better olive harvests. Analyse how favourable weather may affect the market for olive oil. Six marks. The supply curve shows how much producers are willing and able to sell at each price. Better weather means more olives from each tree, so each tonne of oil costs less to make. Use the data. Greece expects two hundred and thirty thousand tonnes, up from one hundred and thirty thousand. That is one hundred thousand tonnes more. Producers offer more olive oil at every price, so supply shifts right, from S to S one. Price falls from P e to P one, and quantity rises from Q e to Q one. The data agrees. Price is expected to fall from ten thousand euros a tonne to between four and five thousand: a fall of at least half. A common mistake is saying advertising shifts supply. Advertising works on buyers, so it shifts demand. Another is saying a rightward supply shift raises the price. More supply means a lower price.

Now you try. The government adds a new tax of fifty cents on every bottle of fizzy drink. Explain the effect on supply, and on the equilibrium price and quantity. Pause here and work it out.

The tax raises the cost of selling each bottle. Firms supply fewer bottles at every price, so supply shifts left. S one crosses D higher up. Price rises from P e to P one, and quantity falls from Q e to Q one. Remember: identify the factor, link it to costs, state the direction, then the new price and quantity.
