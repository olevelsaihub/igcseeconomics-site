Draw a minimum wage on a labour market diagram

Idea. Think of a supermarket hiring shelf stackers. Workers offer their time. The supermarket wants workers. The wage is the price paid for that labour. On a labour market diagram, the wage rate goes up the side and the quantity of labour along the bottom. Demand for labour slopes down: firms hire more when wages are low. Supply slopes up: more people want to work when wages are high. They cross at the equilibrium wage, W e, and quantity, Q e. A minimum wage is the lowest wage an employer is legally allowed to pay. It only has an effect if it is set above W e. On the diagram it is a flat, horizontal line.

Method. Step one. Draw a horizontal line at a wage clearly above W e, right across both curves. Label it W one, the minimum wage. Step two. Where W one meets the demand curve, draw a dotted line down. Label it Q d. Step three. Where W one meets the supply curve, draw a dotted line down. Label it Q s. Step four. Check. Q d must be left of Q e, and Q s must be right of Q e. The gap between them is a surplus of labour: people who want work but cannot find it.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2021. Using the diagram, draw the effects of a minimum wage, W one, set above the equilibrium wage, W e. Label the new quantity of labour demanded and the new quantity supplied. Three marks. First, find W e and Q e on the diagram you are given, where demand for labour crosses supply of labour. Draw W one as a horizontal line above W e. Where it cuts demand, drop a dotted line to Q d. Where it cuts supply, drop a dotted line to Q s. Why? At the higher wage, firms want fewer workers, so Q d is less than Q e. More people want to work, so Q s is more than Q e. The gap is a surplus of labour. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is drawing the minimum wage below W e. A minimum wage that changes anything must be above equilibrium. Another is shifting a curve, or swapping Q d and Q s. No curve moves. Demand gives the smaller quantity, supply the larger.

Your turn. Now you try. An example country sets a minimum wage for fruit pickers above the equilibrium wage. Show the effect on a labour market diagram. Label W one, the quantity of labour demanded and the quantity supplied, and say what the gap shows. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Draw W one above W e. Read down from demand to Q d, left of Q e. Read down from supply to Q s, right of Q e. Farms want fewer pickers at the higher wage, but more people want the job. The gap is a surplus of labour, so some pickers are unemployed. Remember: a horizontal line above W e, read down to Q d from demand and Q s from supply, then label everything.
