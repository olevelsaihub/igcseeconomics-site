When your favourite chocolate bar is on offer, you might buy two instead of one. When its price goes up, you buy fewer. Price changes how much we buy. Demand is the quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at a given price, over a period of time. The demand curve slopes down: the lower the price, the more people buy. Supply is the quantity producers are willing and able to sell at a given price. The supply curve slopes up: a higher price makes selling more profitable. A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the curve, never a shift. Price falls from P to P one, and quantity demanded extends from Q to Q one. Learn the four movements. Price rises: demand contracts, supply extends. Price falls: demand extends, supply contracts.

Step one, for a definition, say willing and able, and at a given price. Step two, read the old and new price and quantity from the graph. Step three, say it is a movement along the curve, and give the reason.

Here is a real question from June 2022. The graph shows the supply of wheat. Explain one effect on the supply curve of wheat following a change in the price of wheat. Three marks. Read the graph. At four dollars, one hundred units. At four dollars fifty, one hundred and fifty units. At five dollars, two hundred units. Choose a rise from four dollars fifty to five dollars. Quantity supplied rises from one hundred and fifty to two hundred units: fifty more. Growing wheat is now more profitable, so farmers offer more. This is an extension along the same supply curve, not a shift. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is saying a change in the good's own price shifts the curve. It only moves along it. Another is defining demand as what people want. Say willing and able to buy, at a given price.

Now you try. A cinema cuts its ticket price from ten pounds to eight pounds. Tickets sold each week rise from four hundred to five hundred and fifty. Explain the effect on the quantity demanded. Pause here and work it out.

The price falls from ten pounds to eight pounds. Quantity demanded extends along the demand curve, from four hundred to five hundred and fifty tickets. That is one hundred and fifty more tickets. Cinema is now cheaper compared with other outings, so more people go. Remember: define with willing and able, read the figures, and call it a movement along the curve, with a reason.
