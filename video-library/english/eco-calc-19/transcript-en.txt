Idea. Say you save one hundred pounds, and the bank pays two per cent interest a year. How much do you earn? You need two per cent of one hundred pounds. The rule: divide the rate by one hundred, then multiply by the amount. Two divided by one hundred, times one hundred pounds, is two pounds. You use this in three ways. Interest is a percentage of savings, added on. Income tax is a percentage of the taxed part of income, taken off. A tariff, a tax on imports, is a percentage of the import price, added on.

Method. Step one, find the base the rate applies to: the savings, only the taxed part of income, or the import price. Step two, work out the amount: rate over one hundred, times the base. For example, a ten per cent tariff on a two hundred dollar import is twenty dollars. Step three, if a final figure is wanted, add or subtract. Step four, write the working, and give the answer with its currency.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2020. George earns twenty-one thousand euros as a manager in Cyprus, but only pays tax on one thousand five hundred euros. Calculate his net pay. Two marks. Find the base. The first nineteen thousand five hundred euros is taxed at zero. Only the last one thousand five hundred is taxed, at twenty per cent. Tax is twenty over one hundred, times one thousand five hundred euros, which is three hundred euros. Net pay is twenty-one thousand minus three hundred: twenty thousand seven hundred euros. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is taxing the whole salary. That gives four thousand two hundred euros of tax, which is wrong. Only the taxed band counts. Another is stopping at the tax, when the question asks for net pay. Three hundred euros is the tax, not the answer.

Your turn. Now you try. Amira imports a bicycle for two hundred and forty dollars, and a fifteen per cent tariff is charged on the import price. Calculate the price including the tariff. Then, Ben saves eight hundred dollars at three per cent a year. Calculate his interest for one year. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a. The tariff is fifteen over one hundred, times two hundred and forty dollars: thirty-six dollars. Add it on: two hundred and seventy-six dollars. Part b. Three over one hundred, times eight hundred dollars, is twenty-four dollars. See the bicycle price grow, from two hundred and forty dollars to two hundred and seventy-six dollars, once the tariff is added. Remember: find the base, work out rate over one hundred times the base, then add or subtract, and show the currency. Now try the practice questions.
