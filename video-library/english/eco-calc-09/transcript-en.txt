A school club bakes one hundred cookies. Everything together costs fifty dollars. How much did each cookie cost to make? Average cost is the cost of making one unit. Divide total cost by the quantity produced. Fifty dollars divided by one hundred is fifty cents. Total cost is fixed cost plus variable cost. Say fixed costs are twenty dollars and each cookie costs thirty cents. Bake two hundred, and total cost is eighty dollars, so average cost falls to forty cents. Making more spreads the fixed cost over more cookies, so here the average cost falls.

The method has three steps. Add fixed and variable cost to get total cost. Divide by the number of units. Give the answer in money to two decimal places.

Here is a real question from January 2023. A firm makes three thousand units a month. Total fixed costs are six thousand two hundred dollars, and total variable costs are three thousand five hundred and fifty dollars. Calculate average cost per unit. Two marks. Read the table: three thousand units, six thousand two hundred dollars fixed, three thousand five hundred and fifty dollars variable. Add them. Six thousand two hundred plus three thousand five hundred and fifty is nine thousand seven hundred and fifty dollars. Divide by three thousand. Nine thousand seven hundred and fifty divided by three thousand is three dollars twenty-five per unit. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is dividing only the fixed cost, or only the variable cost, by output. Another is stopping at total cost. The question wants the cost of one unit.

Now you try. A phone-case maker produces four thousand cases a month. Total fixed costs are five thousand dollars, and total variable costs are seven thousand six hundred dollars. Calculate average cost per case. Pause here and work it out.

Add the costs. Five thousand plus seven thousand six hundred is twelve thousand six hundred dollars. Divide by four thousand. Twelve thousand six hundred divided by four thousand is three dollars fifteen per case. Remember: add fixed and variable cost, divide by output, and give money per unit.
