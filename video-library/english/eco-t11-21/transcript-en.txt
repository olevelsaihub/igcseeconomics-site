Idea. A factory pays for wages and materials. Those are its own costs. But its smoke also harms people living nearby, and they pay nothing to the factory for that harm. Private costs are paid by the producer or consumer making the decision. External costs fall on third parties. Add them together, and you get social costs: the total cost to the whole of society. Benefits work the same way. A student who stays in education earns higher wages: a private benefit. Society gains a more skilled workforce: an external benefit. Together they make social benefits.

Method. Step one, decide whether the question asks about costs or benefits. Step two, write the private part. Step three, add the external part of the same kind. So there are just two formulas to learn. For a two-mark What is meant by question, name both parts and say it is the total for society.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. What is meant by the term social costs? Two marks. The question asks about costs. The private part is private costs, paid by the producer or consumer. The external part is external costs, which fall on third parties. Social costs are the total cost to society: private costs plus external costs. That earns both marks. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing costs and benefits in one formula. Another is writing that social costs are just external costs. The private part counts too.

Answer. The private costs are three million. The external costs, paid by residents, are nought point five million. Social costs are three point five million dollars. The bars show it. Private plus external makes the whole social cost. Remember: decide costs or benefits, write the private part, and add the external part. Now try the practice questions.
