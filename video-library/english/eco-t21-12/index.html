Effects of unemployment on the economy, government and confidence

Idea. Imagine the biggest factory in a town closes. Hundreds of workers lose their jobs. With less money coming in, they spend less in local shops, so the shops suffer too. Unemployment is when people who want to work, and are able to, cannot get a job. Rising unemployment affects output, the government's budget, confidence and society. Unemployed people produce nothing. So the economy produces inside its production possibility curve, at a point like U, instead of on it. Output is lower than it could be: resources are wasted.

Method. Step one, check the direction. Is unemployment rising or falling? Every effect reverses when the direction changes. Step two, name the effect. Step three, chain it through incomes and spending. Jobs are lost, incomes fall, spending falls. So the government collects less income tax and less V A T, a tax on spending. Confidence works the same way. Consumers fear for their jobs, so they spend less. Firms expect fewer sales, so they delay investment. Always say what people then do. Step four, use the rate given. Step five, for Assess, add the other side. Firms find it easier to recruit, and face less pressure for pay rises. Then judge what the impact depends on.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2019. South Africa's unemployment reached twenty-seven point seven per cent, its highest in thirteen years. Assess the impact of high unemployment on a country such as South Africa. Nine marks. Pick out the data. Twenty-seven point seven per cent unemployed. Thirty-nine per cent of unemployed people have never worked, and for young people it is sixty point three per cent. Cost one: lost output. With more than a quarter of the workforce out of work, South Africa produces inside its P P C, so G D P is lower and growth is slower. Cost two: the budget. More people need benefits, but fewer pay income tax, and lower spending means less V A T. So there is less for schools and hospitals, or more borrowing. Cost three: poverty. Losing a job means losing most of a household's income. Long spells without work can bring stress, poor health and crime. Other side: firms recruit more easily and face less pressure to raise pay. But it depends on type and length. South Africa's unemployment is high and long-lasting, so the costs outweigh the gains. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying tax revenue rises when unemployment rises. It falls: less income tax, and less V A T as spending falls. Another is writing 'confidence falls' and stopping there. Say what people and firms then do.

Your turn. Now you try. In an example country, unemployment falls from six per cent to four per cent. Explain one effect on the government's finances. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The direction is falling, from six to four per cent. So more people are in work, and every chain runs the other way. More people earn incomes, so they pay more income tax, and spend more, so V A T revenue rises. Fewer people need benefits. So the government has more to spend on services. Remember: check the direction, name the effect, chain it through incomes and spending, and use the data. Now try the practice questions.
