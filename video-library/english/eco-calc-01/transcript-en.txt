A farmer has one field. She can grow carrots or potatoes. If she plants more carrots, there is less room for potatoes. Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up. On a P P C, moving from A to B gives more carrots, but potatoes fall from eighty to sixty tonnes. Twenty tonnes of potatoes are given up. So the rule is: old amount of the other good, minus the new amount. Give the answer in units of that good.

Step one, find the start point and read the other good. Step two, add the extra units, and read the other good again. Step three, subtract the new amount from the old amount, and write it out. Step four, name the good given up, with its units.

Here is a real question from January 2023. Output is two hundred and fifty units of consumer goods. Calculate the opportunity cost of making an extra forty units of consumer goods. Two marks. Find the start. At two hundred and fifty consumer goods, the curve gives five hundred units of capital goods. Add forty: two hundred and fifty plus forty is two hundred and ninety. At two hundred and ninety consumer goods, the curve gives four hundred capital goods. Capital goods fall from five hundred to four hundred. Five hundred minus four hundred is one hundred. The opportunity cost is one hundred units of capital goods. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is subtracting the good that rises. The extra forty consumer goods are what you gain, not what you give up. Another is giving a bare number. Say which good, and use units.

Now you try. On its P P C, a country making sixty cars can make ninety tractors. Making eighty cars, it can make seventy tractors. Calculate the opportunity cost of the extra twenty cars. Pause here and work it out.

Moving from sixty to eighty cars, tractors fall from ninety to seventy. Ninety minus seventy is twenty. The opportunity cost is twenty tractors. Remember: read the start, read the new point, subtract the good given up, and name it with units. Now try the practice questions.
