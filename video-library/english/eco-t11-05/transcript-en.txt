Idea. Imagine you get a small amount of pocket money each week. You want to spend it on the things that make you happiest. Economists assume every consumer tries to do this. This is the assumption that consumers maximise their benefit. It means getting the most satisfaction they can from their limited income. Real people often fall short, for three reasons. They misjudge the benefit, perhaps from too little information or persuasive adverts. Habits are hard to break. And people copy friends or family to fit in.

Method. Step one, state the assumption. Step two, pick the reason that fits the case. Adverts or missing information point to poor judgement. Still buying after a price rise points to habit. Friends point to copying. Step three, develop it. Show how the reason leads to a choice that gives less benefit. Step four, for a six-mark Analyse, develop two or three reasons with the data, then conclude.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2023. Obesity is rising in Poland. A sugar tax adds nought point five zero zloty per litre to sugary drinks, and adverts have increased. Yet people still buy them. Analyse why consumers may not maximise their benefit. Six marks. Start with the assumption: consumers try to spend their limited income for the greatest benefit. Reason one, poor judgement. Each drink tastes good now, but the harm builds slowly. More adverts give an attractive image and little health information. Reason two, habit. The tax shifts supply to the left and raises the price. But people used to the sweet taste find it hard to stop, so demand is steep and quantity bought falls only a little. The extra cost is small next to most incomes. Reason three, copying others. If friends and family drink these products, a person may buy them to fit in, instead of a healthier drink that would give more benefit. Conclude. Because of poor judgement, habit and copying, consumers keep spending on drinks that harm their health. They do not get the maximum benefit from their income. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying consumers aim to maximise profit. Profit is the assumption about firms. Consumers aim to maximise benefit. Another is naming a reason without developing it. Show how habit leads to a worse choice, using the case.

Your turn. Now you try. Many teenagers in a town spend two hours every night on a gaming app. They say it hurts their sleep and schoolwork. Most of their friends use the same app. Analyse two reasons why they may not maximise their benefit. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Reason one, habit. Opening the app every night becomes hard to stop, even though they know it costs them sleep. So their time gives less benefit than rest or study would. Reason two, copying others. Their friends all use the app, so they join in to fit in, rather than choosing what is best for them. Remember: state the assumption, pick a reason from the case, and develop it to a choice with less benefit. Now try the practice questions.
