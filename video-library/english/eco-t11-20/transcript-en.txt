Idea. Your neighbours hold a loud party until three in the morning. They chose to have it. You did not, but you are the one who cannot sleep. An externality is an effect of producing or consuming something that falls on a third party: someone who is not part of the decision or the deal. An external cost is a harm to a third party, such as pollution, congestion, litter or noise. An external benefit is a gain to a third party, such as from education, healthcare or vaccinations. Business can damage the environment in four main ways: air, water, noise and visual pollution.

Method. Step one, define the externality. Step two, name it from the case. Step three, say who the third party is and how they are affected. Step four, add wider effects on health, output or government spending. Separate private effects from external ones. Rent paid by a factory is a private cost. Smoke that harms its neighbours is an external cost.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2022. In 2019 the NHS in England checked children's records and invited those who had missed the M M R vaccination. Analyse why vaccinations may be an external benefit. Six marks. An external benefit is a gain to a third party. First, the private benefit: the vaccinated child is protected from measles, mumps and rubella. Now the spillover. A vaccinated child is unlikely to catch the diseases, so cannot pass them on. So classmates, brothers, sisters and babies too young for the injection are protected too. They took no part in the decision. Wider effects. Fewer children fall ill, so parents miss less work, and the NHS spends less treating outbreaks. So the benefit to society is greater than the benefit to each child. That is why the NHS chased up missed vaccinations. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is calling private costs, like rent or wages, external costs. Another is naming pollution without saying who is harmed, and how.

Your turn. Now you try. A new airport opens next to a village. Explain one external cost of the airport. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Noise pollution. Planes take off and land all day and night. Villagers, who are third parties, lose sleep. Tired people may work less well and need more healthcare. Remember: define the externality, name it from the case, say who the third party is and how they are affected. Now try the practice questions.
