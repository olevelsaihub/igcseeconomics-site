In many places, only one company runs the trains. If you want to travel by train, you buy from them. You cannot shop around for a cheaper ticket. A monopoly is a market with one dominant seller. One firm supplies all, or nearly all, of the product. In this example market, firm A has ninety-two per cent of all sales. Its features: one dominant firm, a unique product with no close substitute, high barriers to entry, and the firm is a price-maker. It can set the market price.

Barriers to entry are obstacles that make it hard for new firms to enter. They include high start-up costs, patents, expensive technology and strong branding. To explain one, say what a new firm would need, and why it cannot get it. For effects, trace the chain. No competition, so the firm can raise prices or let quality slip, so consumers pay more and have less choice. Then balance it. A monopoly may gain economies of scale, use big profits for research, and avoid wasteful duplication, like two rail networks side by side.

Here is a real question from January 2023. Describe one way that high start-up costs can be a barrier to entry. Two marks. Mark one, the point. Entering the industry needs huge spending on factories, equipment or networks, before a single sale is made. Mark two, the development. Most new firms cannot save or borrow that much, and lenders see it as too risky. So they cannot enter, and the existing firm stays dominant. Point plus development: two marks. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is naming a barrier without saying why it stops new firms. Another is saying a monopoly is always bad. Mention the possible benefits too.

Now you try. A drug company holds a patent on a new asthma inhaler. Describe one way the patent can be a barrier to entry. Pause here and work it out.

A patent is the legal right to be the only producer of the inhaler for a period. Rivals cannot legally copy it, so they cannot enter, and the company stays the only seller and can set the price. Remember: define the idea, explain how the barrier keeps rivals out, trace the effect, and balance it. Now try the practice questions.
