Your family pays tax on wages and on shopping. The government uses that money to build roads, run schools and pay nurses. Fiscal policy means government decisions about spending, taxation and borrowing, used to change total demand in the economy. A direct tax is charged on income, wealth or profit, like income tax. An indirect tax is charged on spending, like V A T and excise duties. To cut unemployment, use expansionary policy: lower taxes or more spending. To cut demand-pull inflation, use contractionary policy: higher taxes or less spending.

Step one, define the policy or tax. Step two, pick the direction. Step three, build the chain: tax cut, more disposable income, more spending, firms produce more and hire. Step four, use the data. Expansionary policy fits a recession, when spending is weak. Step five, for Assess, weigh the limits, then judge.

Here is a real question from January 2023. Unemployment in Spain reached four million, after five monthly rises. Tourism and commerce were worst hit. Assess the likely benefits of lowering taxes to reduce unemployment. Nine marks. Cut a direct tax. Lower income tax means workers take home more pay. They spend more, including in tourism and commerce. Cut an indirect tax. Lower V A T makes shop prices fall, so people buy more. Firms produce more and hire workers, who spend their wages too. But tax cuts may widen the fiscal deficit. Worried households may save the money, or spend it on imports. And if Spain's unemployment is structural or seasonal, a demand boost does little. So it should cut cyclical unemployment, but the benefit depends on confidence and the type of unemployment. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is calling interest rates fiscal policy. Interest rates are monetary policy. Another is stopping the chain too early. Go on to demand, output and jobs.

Now you try. Inflation in Country M is nine per cent, caused by too much spending. Should the government raise or cut income tax? Explain the chain. Pause and decide.

Raise income tax. This is contractionary policy. People have less disposable income, so they spend less, demand falls, and firms raise prices less. Demand-pull inflation falls. Remember: define it, pick the direction, chain through demand to the objective, then weigh the limits. Now try the practice questions.
