Last year a bus ticket, a loaf of bread and a phone top-up all cost a little less than they do today. When most prices rise together like this, life costs more. Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level over time. The general price level is the average price of goods and services. Deflation is the opposite: a fall in the general price level. We measure it with the consumer price index, or C P I. It tracks the price of a typical basket of goods that households buy. If the index goes from one hundred to one hundred and four, prices rose four per cent. That is the inflation rate.

Step one, define with the words general price level. Step two, ask how the cause works: through spending, or through costs. Demand-pull inflation comes from spending. Cost-push inflation comes from costs. Wages, raw materials or energy cost more, and firms pass these higher costs on as higher prices. Step three, for multiple choice, test each option. Higher wages: cost-push. More consumer spending: demand-pull. Higher productivity or higher interest rates tend to reduce inflation.

Here is a real question from January 2023. Mexico raised its minimum wage by fifteen per cent, and nearly eleven million workers are paid it. Analyse the impact on inflation. Six marks. Chain one. Wages are a cost to firms. A fifteen per cent rise pushes up their costs, so they raise prices to protect profits. This is cost-push inflation. Show the scale. With eleven million workers affected, many firms raise prices at the same time, so the general price level rises. Chain two. Those workers now have fifteen per cent more income, and they spend it. If firms cannot supply enough, prices are pulled up. This is demand-pull inflation. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is a vague definition. Prices go up is not enough. Say a sustained rise in the general price level. Another is thinking higher productivity causes inflation. It lowers costs, so it reduces inflation.

Now you try. A country's oil import prices double, so fuel and transport cost firms much more. Which type of inflation is likely, and how does it raise the general price level? Pause here and work it out.

This works through costs, so it is cost-push inflation. Fuel and transport cost firms more, they pass this on as higher prices, and because so many firms use fuel, the general price level rises. Remember: define with the general price level, ask spending or costs, then chain the cause to higher prices. Now try the practice questions.
