Think about a family budget. If a family earns more than it spends, it has money left to save. If it spends more than it earns, it must borrow. Governments are the same. Government revenue is the money the government receives, mostly from taxes. Government expenditure is what it spends, for example on health, schools and benefits. A fiscal surplus is when government revenue is greater than government expenditure in a year. A fiscal deficit is when expenditure is greater than revenue, so the government must borrow the difference. For example, revenue forty billion dollars and spending forty-five billion. Forty minus forty-five is minus five billion. That is a deficit, so the government borrows five billion dollars.

Step one, define the term by comparing revenue and expenditure. Step two, choose a consequence. A surplus can repay debt, cut taxes, or pay for services. A deficit means borrowing and interest, maybe higher taxes later, but a short-run boost to demand. Step three, chain it through to who is affected. Repaying debt means less interest each year, so more money is left for services that households use. Step four, use the figures given. Step five, in an Assess answer, weigh the other side. A surplus may mean taxes are too high, or services were cut. Then judge.

Here is a real question from June 2022. Nauru, a tiny island with ten thousand, eight hundred and twenty-four people, had a fiscal surplus of sixteen point one per cent of G D P in 2019. Analyse the benefits of a fiscal surplus for Nauru. Six marks. Define it: revenue, mostly from taxes, is greater than spending over a year. A surplus of sixteen point one per cent of G D P is very large, so the government has a lot of spare money. Benefit one. The government can repay past borrowing. It then pays less interest each year, so more revenue can go on services, and its finances are safer. Benefit two. The surplus could go back to the ten thousand, eight hundred and twenty-four residents as lower taxes. Their disposable income rises, so they spend more, and local firms see more demand. Benefit three. Spend it on healthcare or education. Healthier, better-trained workers are more productive, raising output and growth in the long run. A common mistake is mixing up the fiscal balance with the current account. Exports and imports belong to the current account. The fiscal position compares government revenue and spending. Another is saying a surplus helps the economy without saying how it is used. Name the use, then the effect.

Now you try. An example country collects thirty billion dollars in revenue and spends thirty-six billion. Is this a fiscal surplus or a deficit? Then explain one consequence. Pause and decide.

Revenue minus expenditure: thirty minus thirty-six is minus six billion dollars. Spending is bigger, so it is a fiscal deficit of six billion dollars. The government must borrow six billion dollars. National debt rises and interest must be paid, which may mean higher taxes or spending cuts later.
