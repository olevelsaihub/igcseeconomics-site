Idea. A T-shirt printer pays rent for its workshop, and buys a blank shirt for every T-shirt it prints. Rent is five hundred dollars at any output. Blank shirts cost four dollars each. Add them for total cost. Even at zero output, the rent must be paid. So total cost rises as output rises, but never falls below the fixed cost.

Method. Steps one and two. Work out total variable cost: cost per unit times units. Then add the fixed cost. For one hundred and fifty shirts: six hundred dollars plus five hundred is one thousand one hundred dollars. If total cost is given, subtract instead. Fixed cost is total cost minus variable cost. Steps three and four. Write it in one line, fixed cost plus cost per unit times quantity, and give the answer with currency.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2021. A firm has fixed costs of fifty thousand dollars a month and variable costs of two hundred and fifty dollars per unit. It makes seven hundred and fifty units a month. Calculate total costs per month. Two marks. Pick out the three figures: the fixed cost, the cost per unit, and the output. Total variable cost is two hundred and fifty times seven hundred and fifty, which is one hundred and eighty-seven thousand five hundred dollars. Add the fixed cost. Fifty thousand plus one hundred and eighty-seven thousand five hundred is two hundred and thirty-seven thousand five hundred dollars. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme. The variable part is much bigger here. Together they make the total.

Common mistake. A common mistake is adding the cost per unit to the fixed cost without multiplying by output. Another is dividing by output. That gives average cost, but the question asks for the total.

Your turn. Now you try. A food van has fixed costs of one thousand two hundred dollars a month. Ingredients cost two dollars fifty per meal, and it sells nine hundred meals a month. Calculate total costs per month. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Variable cost is two dollars fifty times nine hundred, which is two thousand two hundred and fifty dollars. Add one thousand two hundred: total cost is three thousand four hundred and fifty dollars. Check the picture: a small fixed cost and a larger variable cost add up to the total.
