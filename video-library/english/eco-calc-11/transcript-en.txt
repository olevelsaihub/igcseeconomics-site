Juice bar A has three workers who make one hundred and fifty smoothies a day. Juice bar B has five workers who make two hundred. B makes more in total, but each of its workers makes less. Labour productivity is the output made per worker in a period of time. Divide total output by the number of workers. One hundred and fifty divided by three is fifty. Two hundred divided by five is forty. The answer is in units of output per worker, such as smoothies or cars. It is never in dollars.

Step one, take the total output for the period. Step two, divide it by the number of workers. Step three, give the answer in units per worker. Why does it matter? If each worker makes more, the wage cost of each unit falls, so the firm's cost per unit can fall.

Here is a real question from November 2023. A firm employs one hundred and thirty-five workers and makes thirty-three thousand seven hundred and fifty units a month. What is the monthly labour productivity? One mark. Divide output by workers. Thirty-three thousand seven hundred and fifty divided by one hundred and thirty-five is two hundred and fifty. Check: one hundred and thirty-five times two hundred and fifty gives the output back. Now the options. A has the right number but in dollars, so it is wrong. B and D come from multiplying. C, two hundred and fifty units, is correct. The answer is C: two hundred and fifty units per worker each month. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is dividing the wrong way round. Workers divided by output gives a tiny number that makes no sense. Another is choosing an answer in dollars. Productivity counts units, not money.

Now you try. A bakery has twelve workers who make four thousand eight hundred loaves a week. Calculate labour productivity. Next year fifteen workers make five thousand four hundred loaves. Has productivity risen or fallen? Pause here and work it out.

Four thousand eight hundred divided by twelve is four hundred loaves per worker. Next year, five thousand four hundred divided by fifteen is three hundred and sixty. So productivity has fallen, from four hundred to three hundred and sixty, even though total output rose. The number of workers rose faster than output. Remember: take total output, divide by the number of workers, and answer in units per worker. Now try the practice questions.
