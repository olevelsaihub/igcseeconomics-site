You have one hundred pounds for a school trip to France. At the exchange desk, one pound buys one euro twenty. How many euros do you get? An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another. Each pound buys one euro twenty, so one hundred pounds buys one hundred times one point two. That is one hundred and twenty euros. So the rule is this. Going from the first currency to the second, multiply by the rate. Going from the second back to the first, divide by the rate.

Step one. Write the rate as one unit of A equals x units of B. With one pound equals one euro twenty, A is pounds and B is euros. Step two. A to B, multiply. B to A, divide. Sixty euros back to pounds is B to A, so divide: sixty divided by one point two is fifty pounds. Step three, a size check. One pound buys more than one euro, so the euro number should always be bigger. Sixty euros and fifty pounds: that makes sense. Step four. Round as asked, and write the new currency's symbol. Fifty euros divided by one point two is forty-one point six six six, which is forty-one pounds sixty-seven to two decimal places.

Here is a real question from June 2025. A UK-made smartphone costs seven hundred and fifty pounds. The rate is one pound equals one euro eighteen. Calculate the price in euros. Two marks. The rate is one pound equals one euro eighteen, so A is pounds and B is euros. The price is in pounds, and we want euros. That is A to B, so multiply. Seven hundred and fifty times one point one eight is eight hundred and eighty-five. The answer is eight hundred and eighty-five euros. Check the size. Each pound buys more than one euro, so the euro price should be bigger. Eight hundred and eighty-five is bigger than seven hundred and fifty. A common mistake is dividing when you should multiply. Seven hundred and fifty divided by one point one eight gives about six hundred and thirty-six, which is smaller. The size check shows it is wrong. Another is writing the answer in the old currency, or adding the rate to the amount. The answer needs the new symbol.

Now you try. An American tourist pays fifty-one euros for dinner in Rome. The rate is one dollar equals nought point eight five euros. Calculate the price in dollars. Pause here and work it out.

One dollar equals nought point eight five euros, so A is dollars and B is euros. We go from euros back to dollars, B to A, so divide. Fifty-one divided by nought point eight five is sixty dollars. Check the size. One dollar buys less than one euro, so the dollar number should be bigger. Sixty dollars is more than fifty-one euros. Correct. Remember: write the rate as one unit of A, multiply for A to B and divide for B to A, check the size, then add the new symbol. Now try the practice questions.
