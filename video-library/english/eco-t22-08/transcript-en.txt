Idea. Think of a concert hall with two hundred seats. However many people queue, and however much they offer, only two hundred get in. The number is fixed. Before any quota, demand and supply cross at the equilibrium price, P e, and quantity, Q e. A quota is a legal limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported. Whatever the price, no more can be supplied. So supply becomes a vertical line at the quota. This is perfectly inelastic supply.

Method. Step one. Find where the quota lies. A new quota below Q e, or a tighter one, goes to the left. A bigger quota moves the line right. Step two. Draw the vertical line and label it S one, quota. Step three. Where it meets D, draw a dashed line across to P one. Step four. Label its foot on the quantity axis Q one.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. China introduced a quota on imports of crude oil. Draw the effect on the equilibrium price and quantity, and label the new curve, price and quantity. Three marks. The quota limits oil imports to less than the amount bought before. So the vertical line goes to the left of Q e. Draw the vertical line and label it S one, quota. It meets D above P e. Draw across to P one, higher than P e. Its foot is Q one, lower than Q e. The price of crude oil rises and less is bought. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is drawing a parallel shift of the sloping supply curve. A quota fixes quantity, so the line must be vertical. Another is putting the line on the wrong side when a quota is increased. More is allowed in, so the line moves right and the price falls.

Answer. The quota is below Q e, so draw a vertical line to the left of Q e and label it S one, quota. Where the line meets D, read across to P one, above P e. Q one is at its foot, left of Q e. The price of sugar rises, and the quantity falls. Remember: find the side, draw a vertical line and label it, then mark P one where it meets D and Q one at its foot. Now try the practice questions.
