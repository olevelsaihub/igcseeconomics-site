Read the narration

You have ten pounds of pocket money. A cinema trip costs ten pounds, and so does a new phone case. You want both, but you can only afford one. Countries face the same problem. People's wants never run out, but resources, land, labour, capital and enterprise, are limited. This is scarcity. On this curve, point Z is wanted but cannot be made with the resources available. Needs are things we must have to survive: food, water and shelter. Wants are the extras we would like, such as holidays, phones and education. When you choose, you give something up. Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up. Choose the cinema, and the opportunity cost is the phone case. Three economic agents make choices. A consumer buys for their own use. A producer makes and supplies goods and services. A government runs the country.

Step one, start from scarcity: the agent has limited money. Step two, say who chose and what they chose, using names from the question. Step three, name one realistic alternative. Step four, explain that the budget cannot pay for both, so it is given up. A family, a firm or a government all work the same way.

Here is a real question from June 2024. Casper has decided to buy a new television. Explain one possible opportunity cost for Casper of this decision. Three marks. First, name one realistic alternative. Casper might also have wanted a new laptop. Then explain. Casper's money is limited, so he cannot afford both. The laptop was his next best choice, so he sacrifices it. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is saying the opportunity cost is the money paid. It is the next best thing you could have had instead. Another is listing lots of things. Name one single next best alternative.

Now you try. A government has enough money to build a new hospital or a new road, but not both. It builds the hospital. Explain one possible opportunity cost. Pause here and work it out.

The government chose the hospital, so the alternative given up is the new road. Its budget is limited, so it cannot pay for both. The road was the next best choice, so drivers lose the faster journeys it would have given. Remember: who chose what, one next best alternative, and why both cannot be had.
