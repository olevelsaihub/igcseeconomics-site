Idea. A pie chart is a circle cut into slices, like a pizza. Here the whole circle is all the workers in a country. Each slice is the share working in one sector. In a developed country, such as Japan, T is the biggest slice, well over half. S comes second, about a quarter. P is only a thin slice. These sizes are an example, not real figures. In a developing country, such as Nepal, P is the biggest slice, and it must be at least half of the circle. S and T share the rest.

Method. Step one, decide if the country is developed or developing. Step two, draw a line through the centre, straight up and down, to split the circle in half. Step three, size the slices. Developed: T more than half, S about a quarter, P under a tenth. Developing: P more than half, then split the rest between S and T. Step four, write P, S and T clearly inside each slice.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2021. On a blank pie chart, draw and label the sizes of the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors for a developing economy such as Haiti. Three marks. Haiti is a developing economy, so most people work in farming and other primary jobs. P must be the biggest slice, and more than half. Draw the half-way line. Give P the whole of one half plus a bit more, about sixty per cent. Split the rest: T about thirty per cent, S about ten. Label each slice. Sixty plus thirty plus ten makes one hundred per cent, the whole circle. With a protractor, each one per cent is three point six degrees, so P is two hundred and sixteen degrees, but rough sizes are fine. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is making P the biggest slice, but less than half. For a developing country, P must reach the half-way line. Another is drawing three equal slices, or forgetting the labels. Make the size order clear, and label every slice.

Your turn. Now you try. On a blank pie chart, draw and label the sizes of the three sectors for a developed economy such as Canada. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Canada is developed, so T is the biggest slice, about seventy-five per cent. S is about twenty, and P is a thin slice of about five. That makes one hundred. Remember: decide developed or developing, halve the circle, size the slices, and label P, S and T. Now try the practice questions.
