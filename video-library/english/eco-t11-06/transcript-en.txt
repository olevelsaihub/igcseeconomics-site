Idea. Picture a small café. The owner could charge as much as people will pay. Instead she keeps prices low and gives regular customers a free cake. Why would a business give money away? Profit is total revenue minus total costs. If the café takes five hundred dollars and its costs are three hundred and fifty, profit is one hundred and fifty dollars. Economists assume firms aim to make this as big as possible. That is profit maximisation. But some firms choose other aims: maximising sales or revenue, caring for customers, charitable work, survival or growth. A manager paid by sales may also prefer more sales to the highest profit.

Method. Step one, state the assumption that firms aim to maximise profit. Step two, find the firm's real aim in the case. Step three, show how this aim cuts profit now, through lower prices or extra costs. Step four, show the possible gain later: loyal customers, a good reputation, publicity or motivated staff. Step five, for a nine-mark Assess, give the benefits, then the counter-arguments, then a judgement that says what it depends on.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2023. Simona runs a hairdresser in Romania. She puts customer care before profit. She may raise prices and charge a fee for lateness. Assess the benefits. Nine marks. Her aim is customer care: low prices and no charge for lateness. So slots are wasted and customers wait weeks. Some leave, revenue falls, and she struggles to repay her loan. The benefits. Each appointment earns more, and the fee pays her for lost time. The fee also makes people arrive on time, so fewer slots are wasted, waits shorten and revenue rises to repay the loan. The other side. Some customers come because she is cheap and flexible. If rivals are nearby, demand may be price elastic, so revenue could fall. The fee could also hurt her friendly reputation. Judge. The changes are likely to help, because the fee tackles wasted slots directly. It depends on the size of the price rise. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying the firm maximises profit when the case shows a different aim. Another is giving one side only, or no judgement, in an Assess answer.

Your turn. Now you try. Ravi owns a bakery. Each evening he gives his unsold bread to a local charity instead of selling it off cheaply. Explain one reason why Ravi may choose not to maximise profit. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Ravi's aim is charitable work. He gives up the revenue from selling the bread cheaply, so profit is lower. But local people hear about it, which gives good publicity, so more customers may choose his bakery. Remember: state the profit assumption, find the real aim and its cost, then show the gain and judge. Now try the practice questions.
