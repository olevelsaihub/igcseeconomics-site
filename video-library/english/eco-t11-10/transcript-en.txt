Think of ice cream on a hot day. More people want it, so shops can charge more and still sell more. Economists show this on a market diagram. Price goes up the side and quantity along the bottom. Demand, D, slopes down: people buy more when it is cheap. Supply, S, slopes up. Where they cross is the equilibrium, with price P e and quantity Q e. When something other than price changes, a whole curve moves. This is a shift. An increase moves the curve to the right. A decrease moves it to the left. Changes that affect buyers move demand: income, tastes, population, advertising, and the prices of substitutes and complements. Changes that affect producers move supply: costs, technology, weather, taxes and subsidies.

Step one. Decide which curve moves, demand or supply, and whether it is an increase or a decrease. Step two. Draw the new curve parallel to the old one, on the correct side. Label it D one or S one. Here, supply has fallen, so S one is to the left. Step three. Find where the new curve crosses the curve that did not move. That is the new equilibrium. Draw a line across to the price axis, P one, and down to the quantity axis, Q one. Learn the four results. Demand right: price and quantity both rise. Demand left: both fall. Supply right: price falls, quantity rises. Supply left: price rises, quantity falls.

Here is a real question from January 2020. Incomes rise. Show the effect on the market for wireless headphones, and label the new curve, price and quantity. Three marks. Income affects buyers, so demand moves. With more income, people can afford more headphones at every price. So demand increases, and shifts to the right. Draw D one parallel to D, to its right. Leave S where it is. D one crosses S at a higher point. Draw across to P one, above P e, and down to Q one, to the right of Q e. Price and quantity both rise. A common mistake is moving supply when the change affects buyers. Income is about buyers, so only demand moves. Another is marking P one and Q one at the old crossing, or forgetting the labels. Use the new crossing, and label all three.

Now you try. A new machine cuts the cost of making bicycles. Show the effect on the market for bicycles, and label the new curve, price and quantity. Pause here and work it out.

Costs affect producers, so supply moves. Lower costs mean firms can supply more at every price. Supply increases, and shifts to the right. Draw S one to the right of S. It crosses D lower down. So P one is below P e, and Q one is to the right of Q e. Price falls and quantity rises. Remember: which curve and which way, draw and label it, then mark P one and Q one at the new crossing.
