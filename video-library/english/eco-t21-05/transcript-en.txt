Shops are packed in good years and quiet in bad years. A country's output rises and falls like a wave, again and again. On the diagram, real G D P goes up the side and time along the bottom. The wavy line is actual G D P. The straight dashed line is the trend, the long-run average growth path. Boom sits at the top of the wave, the peak, where output is highest. Downturn comes just after the peak. Growth slows: G D P is still growing, but more slowly. On the diagram it is the stretch where the line turns down after the peak. Recession is at the bottom of the wave, the trough. Output has fallen for two quarters in a row and is at its lowest. Slump or depression are also accepted. Recovery is the rising stretch after the trough, where output grows again.

Step one, find the label already given. Step two, follow the order left to right: boom, downturn, recession, recovery. Step three, anchor boom at a peak and recession at a trough. Step four, fill the in-between stretches: downturn on the way down, recovery on the way up. Step five, check nothing is repeated or missing.

Here is a real question from January 2023. The diagram shows real G D P against time, with Boom already labelled at the peak. Label the other three stages. Three marks. Move right from the peak. The line starts to fall, so that box is downturn. The lowest point of the wave is the trough. Label it recession. After the trough the line climbs again. Label it recovery. Check the order: boom, downturn, recession, recovery. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is swapping downturn and recovery. Downturn is on the way down. Recovery is on the way up. Another is calling the trough a downturn, or writing the given label again.
