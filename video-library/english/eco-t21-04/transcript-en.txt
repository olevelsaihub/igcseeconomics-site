Some years the shops in your town are busy and hiring. Other years they are quiet and cutting staff. The whole economy moves like this. The economic cycle is the up-and-down pattern of real G D P over time, around its long-run trend. It has four phases. Boom is the peak. Output, spending and jobs are at their highest. Downturn comes next. Just after the peak, growth slows down. G D P is still growing, but more slowly. Recession: output falls. Growth is negative for two quarters in a row, down to the low point. A quarter is three months. Recovery: confidence returns and G D P starts to rise again.

Step one, name the phase and what G D P is doing. Step two, say what happens to total spending. Step three, follow it through firms to the outcome. Learn the results. In a boom, unemployment is low and inflation rises. In a recession, unemployment rises and inflation slows. In a recovery, unemployment starts to fall and inflation may pick up. For a nine-mark Assess, add limits. Cost-push inflation may not fall in a recession, and low interest rates may keep spending up. Then judge.

Here is a real question from June 2024. Germany's G D P fell zero point four per cent in a quarter. Inflation hit a record ten point four per cent, with high gas and oil prices. Assess how a recession may affect inflation. Nine marks. First, define recession: real G D P falls for two quarters in a row. Chain one. In a recession, spending falls. The data shows German consumer spending and investment falling. Firms have unsold stock, so they hold or cut prices. Demand-pull inflation slows. Chain two. Firms cut output, so they need fewer workers and materials. Wages and input prices rise more slowly, so firms' costs, and prices, rise more slowly too. But much of Germany's inflation is cost-push, from energy. A recession does little to cut world gas and oil prices. Low interest rates may also keep spending up. So inflation should fall somewhat, depending on energy prices. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is defining a recession as when the economy is bad. Give the fall and the time: real G D P falls for two quarters in a row. Another is jumping from recession straight to unemployment. Show the link through spending and firms.

Now you try. Country Q enters a recession, and household spending falls by three per cent. Explain the likely effect on unemployment. Pause here and work it out.

With spending down three per cent, firms sell less, so they cut output. They need fewer workers, so unemployment rises. This is cyclical unemployment. Remember: name the phase, go through spending and firms, then reach jobs or prices. Now try the practice questions.
