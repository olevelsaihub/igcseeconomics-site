Idea. A small bakery has two ovens and three bakers. It can bake cakes or bread. If it bakes more cakes, it must bake less bread, because the ovens and bakers are limited. A production possibility curve, or P P C, shows the most of two goods an economy can make when it uses all its resources fully and efficiently. A point on the curve, like A, means resources are fully employed. A point inside, like U, means some resources are unemployed. A point outside, like Z, cannot be reached today. Moving along the curve from A to B means more of one good and less of the other. What is given up is the opportunity cost. An outward shift is economic growth: more of both goods. Causes include better technology, more investment, and more or better trained workers. An inward shift is negative growth, from resources running out, emigration or disasters.

Method. Step one, say what the P P C shows. Step two, classify what you see: a point, a movement or a shift. Step three, quote the figures, then give the cause or effect.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2023. The economy moves from A to B on its P P C. Analyse the impact on the economy. Six marks. Read the points. At A: three hundred and fifty million capital goods and one hundred and sixty million consumer goods. At B: four hundred and fifty million capital goods and one hundred and thirty million consumer goods. Capital goods rise by one hundred million. Consumer goods fall by thirty million. Those thirty million consumer goods are the opportunity cost. Resources are scarce, so workers and machines move from consumer goods to capital goods. Households have fewer goods now, so living standards may dip. But capital goods are machines and tools that make other goods. With one hundred million more, capacity rises, so the P P C can shift outwards in future. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying a point inside the curve is impossible. It can be reached, but some resources are unemployed. Another is calling a shift a movement. A movement stays on the same curve. A shift draws a new curve.

Your turn. Now you try. An economy moves from point C, two hundred food and eighty clothing, to point D, one hundred and fifty food and one hundred and twenty clothing. Find the opportunity cost, and give one cause of an outward shift. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Clothing rises by forty units. Food falls from two hundred to one hundred and fifty, so the opportunity cost is fifty units of food. Better technology lets the same resources make more of both goods. So the P P C shifts outwards.
