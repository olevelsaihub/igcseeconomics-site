Pick up a phone. It may be designed in one country, built from parts made in several others, and shipped across an ocean to a shop near you. The whole world helped make it. Globalisation means the world's economies becoming more connected, or integrated, and more dependent on each other, or interdependent. You see it as more trade, more investment across borders, and more firms working in many countries. Why has it grown? Four drivers. Fewer tariffs and quotas. Cheaper transport. Cheaper communication. And multinational corporations, or M N Cs, becoming more important.

Step one. Pick out the driver named in the question. Step two. Show how it makes trading across borders cheaper or easier. Step three. Carry the chain on. Cheaper trade means more goods cross borders, so economies become more connected. That is greater globalisation. Step four. Use a figure from the case. Be careful with direction. Anything that makes trade dearer or harder slows globalisation down.

Here is a real question from November 2021. Most goods trade goes by sea in containers. The largest ship carries over twenty thousand containers, and shipping a t-shirt from Vietnam costs five cents. Explain one reason why cheaper transport has increased globalisation. Three marks. The reason. One huge ship carries a vast number of goods in one journey, so the cost of moving each item is tiny. Just five cents for a t-shirt. Develop it. Transport now adds almost nothing to the price. So a European shop can buy clothes from a low-cost producer on the other side of the world, and still sell them cheaply. Finish the chain. Many more goods are traded between countries, so economies become more connected and dependent on each other. Globalisation increases. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is choosing higher tariffs or higher transport costs as a cause of globalisation. They make trade harder, so they slow it down. Another is defining globalisation as just more trade. Include the idea of economies becoming connected and dependent on each other.

Now you try. A small leather-goods firm in Morocco uses video calls and an online marketplace to find buyers in Canada. Explain one reason why cheaper communication has increased globalisation. Pause here and work it out.

The reason. The internet lets the Moroccan firm reach Canadian buyers for almost nothing, with no costly trips or offices abroad. So the firm exports more. Small firms everywhere can do the same, so trade links grow and economies become more connected. Globalisation increases.
