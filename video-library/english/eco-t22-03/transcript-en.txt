Idea. Think of a big burger chain. You find its restaurants in dozens of countries, run by one company. That company is a multinational. A multinational corporation, or M N C, is a firm that produces, sells or operates in more than one country. Foreign direct investment, or F D I, is a firm putting money into productive assets in another country, such as building a factory there. Simply exporting goods is not F D I. Why do firms invest abroad? To reach new customers, to use cheaper labour or materials, to save transport costs by producing near buyers, to gain economies of scale, and to get round tariffs. The host country, the one receiving the investment, can gain jobs, skills, technology and tax. But there are drawbacks: low wages, profits sent home, pollution, and local firms pushed out.

Method. Step one. Decide whose view the question takes. Is it the firm, asking why invest? Or the host country, asking about gains and costs? Step two. For a motive, name it and show how it raises the firm's profit, through lower costs or more revenue. Step three. For an effect, follow it through the host economy. Then use the case.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2020. Mondelez, a food and drink M N C, opened its first factories in Bangladesh in 2018, after fifteen years of exporting there. Analyse why it might want to invest in Bangladesh. Six marks. Motive one. Fifteen years of exports prove there is demand. Making the products inside Bangladesh removes long-distance shipping, so transport costs fall. Motive two. Wages in Bangladesh are likely to be lower. Lower labour costs cut the cost of each product. Mondelez can cut prices to win customers, or keep prices and earn more profit. Motive three. Goods made in Bangladesh are not imports, so they avoid any tariffs on imported food. All three motives make investing more profitable than exporting. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is calling exporting F D I. F D I means owning or building productive assets abroad. Another is giving benefits to the host country when the question asks why the firm wants to invest.

Your turn. Now you try. A Japanese car maker builds a new factory in Mexico, employing three thousand workers. The figure is just an example. Explain one benefit for Mexico. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The view is Mexico's, so give an effect. The factory creates three thousand jobs. Those workers earn wages and spend them in local shops. So unemployment falls, local firms sell more, and the Mexican government collects more tax. Remember: decide whose view, name the motive or effect, develop it to profit or to the host economy, and use the case. Now try the practice questions.
