When cakes sell for a higher price, a bakery bakes more of them. Economists ask: how strongly does supply react to price? Price elasticity of supply, or P E S, is the percentage change in quantity supplied divided by the percentage change in price. Price up ten per cent, quantity supplied up fifteen per cent: P E S is one point five. Along a supply curve, price and quantity supplied move the same way. When demand rises, price rises and so does quantity supplied. So P E S is positive. Read the size. Below one, supply is inelastic. Exactly one, unitary. Above one, elastic.

Step one. Write both changes with signs. Say price falls eight per cent and quantity supplied falls four per cent. Steps two and three. Quantity supplied on top, price underneath. Minus four divided by minus eight is plus nought point five. A fall divided by a fall is positive. If P E S is given, multiply it by the price change to find the change in quantity supplied. P E S of two and a three per cent price rise give a six per cent rise in quantity supplied.

Here is a real question from June 2022. Greg makes pottery. The price rose by five point four per cent and quantity supplied rose by four point seven per cent. Calculate P E S to two decimal places. Two marks. Both are rises, so both are plus. Price plus five point four, quantity supplied plus four point seven. Quantity on top. Four point seven divided by five point four is nought point eight seven zero three. To two decimal places, nought point eight seven. Nought point eight seven is below one, so Greg's supply is price inelastic: output rose by a smaller percentage than price. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is putting price on top. Five point four over four point seven gives the wrong answer. Another is giving a negative P E S when both changes are falls, or adding a per cent sign.

Now you try. A toy factory sees the price of its scooters rise by six per cent. It raises quantity supplied by nine point five per cent. Calculate P E S to two decimal places, and say if supply is elastic or inelastic. Pause here and work it out.

Nine point five divided by six is one point five eight three three. To two decimal places, one point five eight. One point five eight is above one, so supply is elastic. Quantity supplied rose by more than the price. Remember: quantity supplied over price, divide, round as asked, and give a positive number with no per cent sign. Now try the practice questions.
