Idea. In most countries, just a few big companies sell mobile phone contracts. When one cuts its price or launches a new deal, the others soon react. An oligopoly is a market where a few large firms control most of the sales. In this example market, four firms have ninety per cent of all sales. Its features: a few large firms dominate, products are differentiated, there are barriers to entry, and firms are interdependent. Each watches and reacts to its rivals. Firms often use non-price competition: winning customers with branding, service, loyalty cards or quality, instead of lower prices.

Method. Step one, define oligopoly. Step two, match each feature to evidence in the data: the number of firms or shares, branding, and barriers such as high set-up costs. Step three, for collusion, chain it. Firms secretly agree to fix prices or limit supply. A group that does this is a cartel. Competition falls, so consumers pay more. Step four, for rivalry, chain the other way. Firms fight for market share, through price wars or better service, so consumers gain.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025. Uganda's petrol market has five main companies, each with its own brand. Analyse why it could be described as an oligopoly. Six marks. Define it, then feature one. An oligopoly is a market where a few large firms control most sales. Uganda's petrol market is led by just five main companies. Feature two, differentiation. Each company has its own logo and colours, and stands out differently: service, the environment, modern facilities, a loyalty card, motivated staff. Petrol is very similar, so this non-price competition keeps drivers coming back. Feature three, barriers to entry. Set-up costs are very high, so few new firms can afford to join, and the same five companies stay dominant. Few dominant firms, branding and high barriers: it is an oligopoly. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying an oligopoly has one dominant firm. That is a monopoly. Another is describing collusion as just firms working together, without the result.

Your turn. Now you try. Three cinema chains have eighty-five per cent of ticket sales. In the same week, they all raise prices by the same amount. If they are colluding, explain one likely effect on consumers. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The chains have agreed to fix prices, so there is no cheaper rival to switch to. Cinema-goers pay more for each ticket, and some may go less often. Remember: define oligopoly, prove each feature with the data, and chain collusion or rivalry through to consumers. Now try the practice questions.
