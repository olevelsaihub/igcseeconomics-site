One worker asking the boss for a pay rise is easy to ignore. Two hundred workers asking together are much harder to ignore. A trade union is an organisation of workers that acts to protect and improve its members' interests. Unions aim for higher pay, better conditions, protected jobs and rights, and they give legal help. How do unions get results? Through collective bargaining: the union negotiates with the employer for all members at once. It can also threaten strikes, or lobby the government for laws that help workers. There is a downside. If the union wins a wage above the old level, firms' costs rise. At the higher wage, firms want fewer workers than before.

Step one, define a trade union. Step two, explain the mechanism: bargaining or a strike threat wins pay or conditions. Step three, use the case: the union, its members and the pay rise. Step four, give the other side. Higher wages raise costs, so the firm may cut jobs, raise prices, or replace workers with machines. Step five, judge. Say what the outcome depends on: membership, productivity gains, and whether the firm can pass costs on to customers.

Here is a real question from January 2023. After negotiations with the union Usdaw, Morrisons raised pay from nine pounds twenty to at least ten pounds an hour, for one hundred and ten thousand workers. Most are union members. Assess the impact on Morrisons and its employees. Nine marks. Use the data. The rise is eighty pence an hour, about eight point seven per cent. Because most workers are members, Usdaw had strong bargaining power. For employees, this is a clear gain. Their income and living standards rise, and they may feel more valued and motivated. For Morrisons, the wage bill rises a lot, because the rise covers so many workers. To protect profit, it might cut staff or hours, or use self-service tills. But the cost may be smaller than it looks. Better-paid staff may work harder and stay longer, raising productivity. As a leading supermarket, Morrisons may pass some cost on in prices. Judge. Employees clearly gain. The effect on Morrisons depends on whether productivity rises, and whether it can raise prices without losing customers to rivals. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is assuming unions always win higher pay at no cost. Higher wages raise costs, which can mean fewer jobs or higher prices. Another is defining a union as a group that helps firms or the economy. A union protects its workers.

Now you try. In a city, nine in ten bus drivers belong to a union. After a two-day strike, the bus firm raises pay from fifteen dollars to sixteen dollars fifty an hour. Calculate the percentage rise, then assess the impact on the firm and its drivers. Pause here and work it out.

The rise is one dollar fifty. One fifty divided by fifteen, times one hundred, is ten per cent. Drivers gain higher pay, won because most belong to the union. The firm's costs rise, so it may raise fares or cut routes. If passengers keep using buses, the firm copes; if not, jobs may go. Remember: define a trade union, explain how it wins, use the case figures, give the costs, and judge with it depends. Now try the practice questions.
