Imagine a new lemonade stall at your school fair. A big drinks company sets up next to it and sells drinks below what they cost to make, just to drive the stall away. Countries face threats like this from imports. Protection means government measures that restrict imports, to shield domestic producers or consumers. A tariff, a tax on imports, shifts import supply left: price rises and fewer imports are bought. Dumping is a firm or country selling exports abroad at a price below its cost of production. Firms dump to get rid of surplus output, or to drive foreign rivals out and win market share. Infant industries are new industries that have not yet grown big enough to compete. Small firms have high average costs. Protection gives them time to grow and gain economies of scale. Other reasons are to protect domestic jobs, keep out unsafe goods, raise tax revenue from tariffs, reduce a current account deficit, and retaliate against another country's barriers.

Step one, name the motive. Step two, explain the threat the imports pose. Step three, show what happens if nothing is done, or how restricting imports removes the threat. Step four, use the case: the product, the country, the event. Step five, for a definition, give both parts.

Here is a real question from November 2020. In December 2018, UK consumers were warned to avoid some imported plastic toys containing banned chemicals. Many were sold online. Explain one reason why UK consumers need protecting from some imported goods. Three marks. The reason is safety. Some imports are made in countries with weaker safety rules, so they may not meet UK standards. Here, toys contained banned chemicals and were sold online. Develop it. Parents cannot tell the toys are unsafe. Children play with them and could become seriously ill. So the government restricts these imports to protect UK consumers. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is defining dumping as just selling cheaply. It must be abroad, and below the cost of production. Another is describing how a tariff works when the question asks why a country wants protection.

Now you try. A country has just started making solar panels. Its firms are small, and cheaper panels arrive from large foreign firms. Explain one reason why the government may protect its solar panel industry. Pause here and work it out.

This is an infant industry. The firms are new and small, so they cannot yet match the low prices of large foreign rivals. Protection gives them time to grow. As output rises, they gain economies of scale and average cost falls, until they can compete without help. Remember: name the motive, explain the threat from imports, show the consequence, and use the case. Now try the practice questions.
