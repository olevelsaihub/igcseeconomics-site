Idea. Imagine you get twenty pounds pocket money a month, but you spend twenty-five. You are five pounds short, so you must borrow. These are example figures. A government faces the same sum, only much bigger. Government revenue is the money the government receives, mostly from taxes. Government expenditure is what it spends, for example on health, schools and benefits. The fiscal balance is revenue minus expenditure. If revenue is greater than spending, the answer is positive: a fiscal surplus. If spending is greater than revenue, the answer is negative: a fiscal deficit.

Method. Step one, take total revenue. Step two, subtract total expenditure, and write the subtraction. For example, revenue fifty billion dollars, spending sixty billion. Fifty minus sixty is minus ten billion dollars. Step three, keep the sign. Here the spending bar is taller than the revenue bar, so the balance is negative. That is a deficit of ten billion dollars. Step four, copy the currency and units from the data, such as euros million, or rupees crore. Then say whether it is a deficit or a surplus.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2021. Figure 1 gives India's government revenue and expenditure for 2019 to 2020, in rupees crore. Calculate the fiscal surplus or deficit. Two marks. Revenue is two million, eighty thousand, two hundred and one crore. Expenditure is two million, seven hundred and eighty-four thousand, two hundred crore. Spending is bigger, so expect a deficit. Revenue minus expenditure gives minus seven hundred and three thousand, nine hundred and ninety-nine rupees crore. It is negative, so India had a fiscal deficit. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is subtracting the wrong way round. Spending minus revenue flips the sign, and turns a deficit into a surplus. Another is adding the two figures, or leaving out the currency and units. Subtract, then write the units.

Your turn. Now you try. In an example country, government revenue is eighty-four million euros, and government expenditure is seventy-one million euros. Calculate the fiscal balance and say whether it is a surplus or a deficit. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Revenue minus expenditure: eighty-four minus seventy-one is plus thirteen million euros. Revenue is the taller bar, so the balance is positive. This time it is a fiscal surplus of thirteen million euros. Remember: revenue minus expenditure, keep the sign, then add the units and name it. Negative is a deficit, positive is a surplus.
