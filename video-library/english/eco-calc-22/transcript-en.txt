Idea. Say you earn twenty pounds from chores this month and spend fifteen. Twenty minus fifteen: you are five pounds ahead. Countries do the same sum with their trade. Exports bring money in. Imports send money out. Trade in goods is visible. Trade in services is invisible. So there are three balances. Visible balance: exports of goods minus imports of goods. Invisible balance: exports of services minus imports of services. Add the two to get the current account balance. Read the sign. A positive balance is a surplus. A negative balance is a deficit.

Method. Step one. Choose the right rows. Visible means goods only. Invisible means services only. The current account uses them all. Step two. Write exports minus imports. Step three. Keep the sign. Step four. Give the currency and units.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025. Figure one shows a country's exports and imports in billions of euros. Calculate the visible trade balance. Two marks. Visible means goods, so use only the goods rows. Exports of goods are one hundred and fifty billion. Imports of goods are one hundred and twenty billion. Ignore the services rows. Goods exports are bigger than goods imports, so we expect a surplus. One hundred and fifty minus one hundred and twenty is thirty. The visible trade balance is thirty billion euros, a surplus. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is using every row when the question asks for visible trade. That gives forty billion, which is the current account, not the visible balance. Another is subtracting the wrong way. Imports minus exports flips the sign and turns a surplus into a deficit.

Answer. A: goods only. Sixty minus seventy-five is minus fifteen. The visible balance is minus fifteen billion dollars, a deficit. B: services are twenty-five minus fifteen, which is plus ten. Add the visible balance: minus fifteen plus ten is minus five. The current account is minus five billion dollars, a deficit. The check agrees. Total exports are eighty-five billion, total imports ninety billion. Imports are bigger, so it is a deficit. Remember: choose the right rows, subtract exports minus imports, keep the sign, and give the units. Now try the practice questions.
