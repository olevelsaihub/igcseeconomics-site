Idea. Your local cinema puts up its ticket price. Some people stop going. Economists ask a simple question: how strongly did buyers react? Price elasticity of demand, or P E D, compares two percentage changes. The percentage change in quantity demanded goes on top. The percentage change in price goes underneath. Each change gets a sign. A rise is plus. A fall is minus. This matters, because the sign carries into your answer. On a demand curve, a higher price means a lower quantity demanded. One change is plus and the other is minus, so P E D is usually negative.

Method. Step one. Write both changes with signs. Say price rises ten per cent and quantity demanded falls fifteen per cent. Steps two and three. Quantity on top, price underneath, then divide. Minus fifteen divided by ten is minus one point five. Step four. Round as the question asks and keep the minus sign. P E D has no per cent sign. Then ignore the sign to classify: one point five is above one, so demand is elastic. Sometimes you are given P E D and must find the quantity change. Multiply P E D by the price change. P E D is minus nought point five and price rises eight per cent, so quantity demanded falls four per cent.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2022. Inny cleans windows. He raised his prices by one point six per cent and quantity demanded fell by two point one per cent. Calculate P E D to two decimal places. Two marks. Write the signs. Price rose, so plus one point six. Quantity demanded fell, so minus two point one. Quantity on top. Minus two point one divided by one point six is minus one point three one two five. To two decimal places, that is minus one point three one. Ignore the sign: one point three one is above one, so demand for Inny's service is elastic. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is putting the formula upside down. Price over quantity gives the wrong number. Another is dropping the minus sign, or adding a per cent sign. Both can cost the answer mark.

Your turn. Now you try. A gym raises its monthly fee by six per cent, and the number of members falls by five per cent. Calculate P E D to two decimal places, then say if demand is elastic or inelastic. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Price is plus six, quantity is minus five. Minus five divided by six is minus nought point eight three three three. To two decimal places, minus nought point eight three. Ignore the sign: nought point eight three is below one, so demand is inelastic. Members fell by a smaller percentage than the fee rose. Remember: write the signs, put quantity over price, divide, and round with the minus sign kept. Now try the practice questions.
