Small and large firms, and why firms stay small

Read the narration

Think of the corner café that knows your name and your usual order, and a huge coffee chain with thousands of shops. Both can do well, in different ways. Small firms are flexible, so they change quickly. They give personal service, the owner keeps control, and they can serve niche markets. Large firms get economies of scale, more finance, spread risk and have more market power. A niche market is a small, specialised part of a market for a particular product, such as handmade candles or made-to-order signs. It is not just a small market. The product is specialised. So why do firms stay small? Four reasons: the market is small, the product is niche, the firm cannot get finance, or the owner simply chooses not to grow.

Step one, pick one reason. Step two, develop it with because. For example, banks see small firms as risky, so loans are hard to get, and the firm cannot afford to expand. Owner's aims need a because too. The owner may want to keep control, have more free time, or already earn enough. So they choose not to grow. Staying small has costs too. A large firm can move down its average cost curve through economies of scale. A small firm stays further up, with higher average costs, fewer ways to raise money and more risk. Step three, apply it to the owner and business in the case. Step four, for Assess, weigh the benefits of staying small against the gains from growth, then judge.

Here is a real question from January 2023. Heather runs a successful restaurant in a small town in Portugal. She decided not to open another one. Explain one reason why she may have kept her business small. Three marks. Spot the key fact: a small town. That points to the size of the market. Only a limited number of local people eat out. Develop it. A second restaurant in the same town would mostly share the same customers, not find new ones. Its sales might not cover the extra costs of premises and staff. Conclude in context. So Heather may stay with one successful restaurant. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is giving a large firm's advantage when the question is about small firms. Spreading risk and many sources of finance belong to large firms. Another is saying the owner does not want to grow, with no reason why. Say what the owner gains by staying small.

Now you try. Amira makes made-to-order wedding cakes in her kitchen. Her bank has refused her a loan for a bigger bakery. Explain one reason why her business may stay small. Pause here and work it out.

The reason is lack of finance. Banks see small businesses like Amira's as risky. Without the loan, she cannot pay for a bigger bakery, so her business stays small. Remember: pick one reason, develop it with because, and apply it to the owner in the case. Now try the practice questions.
