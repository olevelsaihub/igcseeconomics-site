Read the narration

Think about your day. You walk on a road the government built, maybe to a state school. Then you buy a snack from a shop owned by a family. Both sides of the economy serve you. The public sector is owned and controlled by the government. It aims to serve the public interest and is funded mainly by taxes. The private sector is owned by individuals or groups, and usually aims for profit. A mixed economy is one where both sectors provide goods and services. What, how and for whom to produce is decided partly by markets and partly by the government.

Step one, define the sector. Step two, explain the benefits of public provision using the data. Step three, weigh the costs, then judge. The benefits of public provision: access for everyone, external benefits, jobs, and lower costs in future. The costs: tax money has an opportunity cost, funding may run short, and without a profit motive, provision can be less efficient.

Here is a real question from June 2023. Some governments make outdoor areas and parks a priority, to reduce health problems like obesity. Assess the benefits of the public sector providing them. Nine marks. The public sector is run by the government to serve the public interest. A public park is free to enter, so everyone can exercise, even people who could not afford a gym. That reduces obesity and stress. Healthier people need less treatment, so government spends less on healthcare. More people are fit to work, so output rises. And a private firm could not easily charge for an open park, so it would supply too few. But the data says funding is not always available. Money spent on parks cannot also fund schools or hospitals. And crowded parks mean fewer people benefit. Judge. Public parks are likely to bring real benefits, but it depends on funding enough parks so they do not get crowded. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is saying the public sector aims to make a profit. Its aim is the public interest. Another is thinking public limited companies are in the public sector. They are owned by shareholders, so they are private.

Now you try. A government plans free public libraries in every town, paid for by taxes. Give one benefit and one cost of the public sector providing them. Pause here and work it out.

Benefit: libraries are free, so children from low-income homes can read and study. Their skills improve, which helps the whole economy. Cost: the tax money has an opportunity cost. It cannot also pay for hospitals. Remember: define the sector, build benefit chains from the data, weigh the costs, and judge. Now try the practice questions.
