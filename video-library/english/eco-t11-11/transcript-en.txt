Idea. At the end of a market day, a fruit stall still has twenty mangoes left. What does the seller do? Cuts the price, so more people buy them. Equilibrium price is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. The market clears, with nothing left over. If the price is above equilibrium, quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded. Goods go unsold. This is excess supply, a surplus. If the price is below equilibrium, quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied. This is excess demand, a shortage.

Method. Step one, at the given price, read quantity demanded and quantity supplied. Step two, name the surplus or shortage, and its size. Step three, say which way the price moves. Step four, explain the movements. A surplus: price falls, demand extends and supply contracts. A shortage: price rises, demand contracts and supply extends. Step five, end at equilibrium.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2021. In Vientiane, in Laos, people hire taxis through the LOCA app. At ten thousand Kip a journey, there is excess supply. Analyse how market forces can remove it. Six marks. At ten thousand Kip, eight journeys are supplied, but only four are demanded. Eight minus four: an excess supply of four journeys. Waiting drivers earn nothing, so they lower their fares. As the price falls, journeys are better value, so demand extends. Some drivers stop working, so supply contracts. The price keeps falling until eight thousand Kip, where six journeys are demanded and six supplied. The surplus is gone. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is calling unsold goods excess demand. Unsold goods mean excess supply. Another is saying a curve shifts to remove the surplus. Only the price changes, causing movements along both curves.

Your turn. Now you try. A bakery sells cupcakes at one pound. At this price, three hundred are demanded each day, but only two hundred are supplied. Name the problem, give its size, and explain how market forces remove it. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Demand is bigger than supply, so there is excess demand. Three hundred minus two hundred is a shortage of one hundred cupcakes. Cupcakes sell out, so the bakery raises the price. Demand contracts and supply extends, until they are equal at the equilibrium. Remember: read the gap, decide if price falls or rises, explain the movements, and end at equilibrium.
