Look at a phone. It may be designed in one country, built in another, from parts made in many more, then sold all over the world. Globalisation is the world's economies becoming more connected and dependent on one another, through trade, investment, and firms working across borders. Different groups gain and lose. Consumers get choice and lower prices. Producers find new markets. Workers in export industries gain jobs. But traditional industries can close, pollution can rise, and a country relying on one export suffers if world prices fall. Why do prices fall? Foreign rivals add competition, and firms selling to the world produce on a huge scale, so cost per unit falls. Supply increases, and the price falls.

Step one, underline the group in the question. Step two, name a specific effect on that group. Step three, explain how globalisation causes it, step by step. Step four, use the case. For a six-mark Analyse, build two or three chains on the named group only. For a twelve-mark Evaluate, add real counter-points, then say what the outcome depends on.

Here is a real question from June 2024. Global trade reached a record twenty-nine trillion dollars in 2022, and is still growing, though more slowly since the global health crisis. Analyse the possible benefits for consumers of globalisation. Six marks. Chain one, choice. With world trade at a record twenty-nine trillion dollars, shops can stock goods not made at home, such as tropical fruit or foreign cars. Consumers find products that suit them better. Chain two, prices. Home firms face foreign competition, and world-scale firms gain economies of scale. Costs fall, and some of this is passed on as lower prices, for items like clothes and electronics. Chain three, living standards. Lower prices mean each dollar buys more, so consumers can afford more and their standard of living rises. Chain four: technology spreads, so new products arrive sooner. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is writing about the wrong group, such as new markets for firms, when the question asks about consumers. Another is listing benefits in single sentences without developing any of them.

Now you try. Most of Country Y's exports are cocoa beans, sold to large foreign buyers. Analyse one benefit and one cost of globalisation for cocoa farmers in Country Y. Pause here and work it out.

Benefit: farmers can sell to the whole world market. More buyers mean more sales, so farm incomes rise. Cost: relying on one export is risky. If the world cocoa price falls, say by thirty per cent, farm incomes fall with it. And large buyers may keep most of the value. Remember: underline the group, name an effect, build the chain, and use the case. Now try the practice questions.
