Baking one cake at home is expensive for each slice. A big bakery making thousands of cakes buys flour in bulk and uses big ovens, so each cake costs much less to make. Economies of scale: average cost, the cost of each unit, falls as a firm grows and produces more. On the long run average cost curve, this is the falling part. Grow too big, and average cost starts to rise. These are diseconomies of scale: the rising part of the curve. The lowest point of the U is the most efficient scale, where average cost is at its lowest.

Decide the type. Internal economies come from one firm growing, like bulk buying. External economies come from the whole industry or area growing, like skilled labour or better roads. The curve you just saw shows internal economies: one firm growing. Diseconomies come from bureaucracy, poor communication, weak control and low worker motivation. Then tie the economy to a fact in the case, and chain it to unit cost: what becomes cheaper, so average cost falls, so prices can fall or profit rise.

Here is a real question from June 2024. India is expanding its railways, with more freight trains carrying cement, iron ore and steel. Analyse the impact of external economies of scale on construction firms in India. Six marks. Define. External economies are falls in a firm's average cost that come from the growth of the industry or area around it. Better railways are improved infrastructure. Chain one. More freight trains move cement, iron ore and steel in larger loads, more cheaply. Transport is a big cost, so the average cost of each building project falls. Chain two. Reliable deliveries mean fewer days when workers and machines wait idle. Firms finish more projects with the same staff, spreading fixed costs over more output. So construction firms could charge lower prices to win contracts, or earn more profit. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is calling infrastructure or skilled labour an internal economy. They come from the area, so they are external. Another is saying just lower costs. Say average cost, or cost per unit, and link it to growth.

Now you try. A supermarket chain grows from twenty stores to two hundred. Explain one internal economy of scale it may gain, and state one diseconomy it may face. Pause here and work it out.

Purchasing economies. Buying for two hundred stores, it orders in bulk, so suppliers give discounts. The cost of each item falls, so it can cut prices or raise profit. A diseconomy: poor communication. Head office may lose touch with two hundred stores, so decisions slow down and average cost may rise. Remember: decide internal or external, name the economy, tie it to the case, and chain it to average cost. Now try the practice questions.
