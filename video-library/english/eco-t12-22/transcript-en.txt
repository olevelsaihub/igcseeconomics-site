Imagine your town had only one phone shop. It could charge what it liked, and you would have nowhere else to go. So governments regulate competition. They aim to promote competition, limit monopoly power, protect consumers' interests, and control mergers and takeovers. Here is an example. Firm A has forty per cent of a market and Firm B has thirty. If A takes over B, one firm holds seventy per cent. A firm with a very large market share faces little pressure from rivals. It may charge higher prices, offer less choice, give poorer service, and innovate less. A competition regulator, such as the C M A in the UK, can block a merger or takeover. Governments can also lower barriers to entry for new firms, and use laws to cap charges or ban unfair practices.

Step one, state the aim. Step two, explain the harm being avoided: a dominant firm can raise prices, cut quality or innovate less. Step three, tie it to the case: the market share, the takeover, or the charge cap. Step four, for a six-mark Analyse, build more than one chain from the data.

Here is a real question from June 2025. In April 2023, the UK's Competition and Markets Authority, the C M A, stopped Microsoft buying Activision. Microsoft already controlled about sixty to seventy per cent of online video gaming. Explain one reason for the C M A's decision. Three marks. Identify the reason: to stop Microsoft gaining even more monopoly power. Develop it: Microsoft already had about sixty to seventy per cent, so buying Activision would raise its share further, leaving rivals very little. Now the effect, in context. With few rivals to fear, Microsoft would have less pressure to keep prices low or develop new games. UK gamers could pay more and see less innovation. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme. Check the three parts of your answer: the reason, how it develops, and the case.

A common mistake is saying the government does not want firms to grow. Explain the harm to consumers instead. Another is confusing competition rules with rules on pollution. Pollution is a separate issue, about externalities.

Now you try. Two taxi apps in a city together have eighty-five per cent of all rides. One plans to buy the other, but the competition regulator blocks it. Explain one reason why. Pause here and work it out.

After the takeover, one app would have eighty-five per cent of rides. Only fifteen per cent would be left for every other taxi firm. The reason: to limit monopoly power. With little competition left, the app could raise fares and let service slip, so riders would lose out. Remember: state the aim, explain the harm a dominant firm could cause, and use the market share in the case. Now try the practice questions.
