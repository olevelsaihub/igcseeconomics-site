Idea. Imagine you sell lemonade at school. The money you take from sales is your revenue. What you spend on lemons and cups is your cost. What is left over is your profit. Total revenue is all the money a firm receives from selling its products. It equals price times quantity sold. Fifty cups at one dollar each is fifty dollars. Costs come in two kinds. Fixed costs stay the same whatever you make, like rent and insurance. Variable costs change with output, like raw materials. Total cost is fixed cost plus variable cost. Twenty dollars plus ten dollars is thirty dollars. Average cost is total cost divided by quantity: thirty dollars over fifty cups is sixty cents each. Profit is total revenue minus total cost. Fifty dollars minus thirty dollars is twenty dollars. If costs are bigger than revenue, the firm makes a loss.

Method. Step one. Write the formula first. These four come up again and again. Step two. For a two-mark meaning, give two parts: what it is, and where it comes from or what it is made of. Step three. For profit questions, trace the change. Say lemons cost ten dollars more and the price stays the same. Total cost rises to forty dollars, so profit falls from twenty to ten.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2024. What is meant by the term total revenue? Two marks. Start from the formula: price times quantity sold. It reminds you that revenue is money from sales. Part one: it is the total money a firm receives. Part two: from selling all of its goods or services. Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing up revenue and profit. Revenue is all the money from sales. Profit is what is left after costs. Another is dividing only fixed cost by output for average cost. Average cost uses total cost.

Your turn. Now you try. A bike repair shop pays eight hundred dollars rent a month, and spends fifteen dollars on parts for each repair. Which cost is fixed and which is variable? And what is meant by profit? Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Rent is fixed: it is eight hundred dollars however many repairs are done. Parts are variable: more repairs mean more parts. Profit is total revenue minus total costs. It is what the shop keeps after paying all its costs. Remember: write the formula, give both parts of a meaning, and trace any change through to profit. Now try the practice questions.
