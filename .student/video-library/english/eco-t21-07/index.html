Benefits and costs of economic growth

Read the narration

When a new factory opens in a town, more people find jobs. They have more to spend, so nearby shops get busier too. Economic growth is a rise in a country's output, its real G D P, over time. It is one of the government's main macroeconomic objectives: a big goal for the whole economy. Growth brings benefits. Firms earn more profit and invest in machines and buildings. That raises productive potential, the most the economy can produce. The P P C shifts out. Other benefits: unemployment falls, incomes and living standards rise, poverty can fall, and the government collects more tax. The costs: overheating and inflation, pollution, using up finite resources, and wider inequality.

Step one, check whether the question wants a benefit, a cost, or both. Step two, pick one clear effect. Step three, explain the link from more output to that effect. Step four, tie it to the country and its growth figure. Step five, for a nine-mark Assess: develop two benefits with the data, then the costs, then judge.

Here is a real question from June 2021. The text above Figure four says Turkmenistan grows by six point two per cent a year. Oil and gas are more than sixty per cent of G D P. Agriculture is only ten per cent of G D P, but employs half the workers. Assess the likely benefits of growth. Nine marks. Benefit one, jobs. To produce more, firms need more workers. As oil and gas expand, they create jobs directly and in firms that supply them, so unemployment falls. Benefit two, incomes. Figure four shows G D P per capita rising from about four thousand one hundred dollars in 2009 to about seven thousand five hundred in 2018. People can buy more, so living standards rise. Growth can also cut poverty. Half the workers are in farming, which makes only ten per cent of G D P, so many have low incomes. New jobs and more tax for public services could help them. Now the costs. If output grows faster than the economy can cope with, demand runs ahead of supply. The economy overheats, and prices rise: inflation. Growth based on oil and gas causes pollution and uses up finite resources. And profits may go to a few, while farm workers gain little, so inequality widens. Judge. Growth of six point two per cent is likely to bring more jobs and higher incomes. But the benefit depends on whether gains reach farm workers, and whether inflation and reliance on oil and gas are managed. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is writing a general benefit with no because. Show how more output leads to the effect. Another is saying growth causes inflation, without explaining how. Explain overheating.

Now you try. Country Z's real G D P grows by eight per cent a year, led by new car factories. Most of the new jobs are in two big cities. Assess the likely benefits and costs of this growth. Pause here and work it out.

Benefits: car factories create jobs, incomes rise, and the government gets more tax. Costs: factories cause pollution, eight per cent may overheat the economy, and jobs in two cities widen inequality. Judge: benefits likely win, if inflation is controlled and gains spread beyond the cities. Remember: pick the effect, link it to more output, use the country's data, and in an Assess, weigh the costs and judge. Now try the practice questions.
