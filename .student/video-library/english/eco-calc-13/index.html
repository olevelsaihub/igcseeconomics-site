Your pocket money rises from twenty dollars to twenty-five. That is five dollars more, compared with the twenty you started with. Five out of twenty is twenty-five per cent. So percentage change is new minus old, divided by old, times one hundred. Always divide by the old, earlier value. Inflation is measured with the consumer price index. If the base year index is one hundred and it is now one hundred and twelve, prices have risen twelve per cent since the base year.

Step one, pick the two values the question asks for. From 2021 to 2023, use four hundred and four hundred and sixty. Skip 2022. Step two, find the change: sixty. Step three, divide by the old value and times by one hundred: fifteen per cent. If the old index is not one hundred, use the full formula. From one hundred and twenty to one hundred and twenty-six is six, divided by one hundred and twenty: five per cent. A fall gives a minus sign. Fifty down to forty-five is minus ten per cent. Step five, round as asked, and always add the per cent sign.

Here is a real question from November 2021. The table shows a firm's profit from 2017 to 2019. Calculate the percentage change in profit between 2017 and 2019, to two decimal places. Two marks. The old value is 2017: eighty thousand seven hundred and fifty dollars. The new value is 2019: eighty-seven thousand one hundred dollars. Ignore 2018. The change is six thousand three hundred and fifty. Divide by eighty thousand seven hundred and fifty and times by one hundred: seven point eight six three. To two decimal places, seven point eight six per cent. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is dividing by the new value. That gives seven point two nine per cent, which is wrong. Another is using index minus one hundred when the old index is not one hundred.
