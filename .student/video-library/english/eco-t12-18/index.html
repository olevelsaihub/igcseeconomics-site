Supply of labour: what changes the workforce

Idea. Think about the workers in your town. Each year some students leave school and start work. Some grandparents retire. New families move in from elsewhere. The number of workers keeps changing. The supply of labour is how many people offer to work at each wage rate. On a labour market diagram, it slopes up, with the wage rate up the side. Supply rises with a bigger population, immigration, a higher retirement age, a lower school-leaving age, and more women in work. It falls with the opposite changes, and with emigration. Skills matter too, and so does mobility. Geographical mobility is moving to another place for work. Occupational mobility is changing to a different type of job.

Method. Step one, name the factor and its direction. Step two, explain why people join or stay in work. Step three, apply it to the case, and add the effect on wages.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2021. Singapore is raising its retirement age. Assess whether this is likely to be the most effective way to increase the supply of labour. Nine marks. Use the data. The retirement age is sixty-two now, sixty-three in 2022 and sixty-five by 2030. People aged sixty-two to sixty-four must keep working before their pension, and with life expectancy over eighty-four, many are healthy enough. So the supply of labour shifts right, from S to S one. But weigh it up. The rise is small and gradual. Manufacturing, almost a quarter of G D P, may need younger workers. Finance needs new specialist skills. Judge. It is useful, but probably not the most effective on its own. Combined with training and migration, it would fit Singapore's industries better. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is the school-leaving age. Raising it keeps young people in school, so labour supply falls. Another is mixing up demand and supply. More demand for a firm's products raises the demand for labour, not the supply.

Your turn. Now you try. The country of Norland lets skilled nurses move in from abroad, and five thousand arrive this year. Explain the likely effect on the supply of nurses in Norland. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. This is immigration. Five thousand more nurses can work in Norland, so more nurses are offered at every wage. Supply shifts right. The wage may fall from W e to W one, and more nurses are employed. Any shortage of nurses is eased. Remember: name the factor and its direction, explain why, and apply it to the case.
