Excess demand and excess supply

A school trip has forty seats on the bus, but fifty-five pupils want to go. Fifty-five minus forty: fifteen pupils miss out. More want it than there is. If quantity demanded is bigger, there is excess demand: demand minus supply. If quantity supplied is bigger, there is excess supply: supply minus demand. Always take the smaller from the larger. On a graph, read across at the price. Here quantity demanded is to the right of quantity supplied, so there is excess demand.

Step one, find total demand: read the graph, or add all the figures in the table. Step two, find total supply for the same period. Step three, subtract the smaller from the larger, and name the result with its units.

Here is a real question from June 2022. A concert venue in Malaysia shows the tickets demanded for three concerts. Supply for all three totals nine thousand six hundred. Calculate the excess demand. Two marks. Read the table: four thousand two hundred, six thousand, and three thousand two hundred and fifty tickets. Add them. Four thousand two hundred plus six thousand plus three thousand two hundred and fifty is thirteen thousand four hundred and fifty. Compare. Thirteen thousand four hundred and fifty tickets are demanded, but only nine thousand six hundred are supplied. Demand is bigger, so there is excess demand. Thirteen thousand four hundred and fifty minus nine thousand six hundred is three thousand eight hundred and fifty. The excess demand is three thousand eight hundred and fifty tickets. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is comparing one concert with the total for all three. Add every demand figure first. Another is subtracting the wrong way and getting a negative answer. Take the smaller from the larger.

Now you try. A ferry company offers two thousand five hundred seats over three days. Passengers want seven hundred on Friday, nine hundred on Saturday and six hundred and fifty on Sunday. Calculate the excess demand or excess supply. Pause here and work it out.

Add the demand: seven hundred plus nine hundred plus six hundred and fifty is two thousand two hundred and fifty seats. Supply, two thousand five hundred, is bigger. Two thousand five hundred minus two thousand two hundred and fifty is two hundred and fifty. There is an excess supply of two hundred and fifty seats. Remember: total the demand, total the supply, subtract the smaller from the larger, and name it with units. Now try the practice questions.
