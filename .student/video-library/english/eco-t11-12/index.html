If the price of salt doubled, you would still buy about the same. But if one brand of crisps got dearer, many people would switch to another brand. Price elasticity of demand, or P E D, measures how much quantity demanded responds to a price change. Divide the percentage change in quantity demanded by the percentage change in price. Price up ten per cent, quantity down twenty per cent: P E D is minus two. Ignore the minus sign and read the size. Zero is perfectly inelastic. Between zero and one, inelastic. Exactly one, unitary. Above one, elastic. Infinity, perfectly elastic. On a diagram, elastic demand is a flat curve. A small rise in price brings a big fall in quantity demanded. Inelastic demand is a steep curve. Even a big rise in price brings only a small fall in quantity demanded.

Step one, define P E D. Step two, classify the value. Take minus one point five. Ignore the sign: one point five is above one, so demand is elastic. Step three, apply a reason to the product. Demand is more inelastic when there are few close substitutes, the good is a necessity or a habit, it takes a small share of income, or the time period is short. Step four, link to total revenue: price times quantity. Raise the price from two dollars to two dollars twenty. With inelastic demand, sales fall a little and revenue rises. With elastic demand, sales fall a lot and revenue falls. Firms use this to set prices. Governments use it too: taxing goods with inelastic demand raises more tax revenue.

Here is a real question from November 2023. Marina Café, by the sea, charges two euros for a coffee. Other cafés sell the same brand for eighty cents. Analyse why P E D may be inelastic. Six marks. Start with the meaning. Then say what inelastic means: quantity demanded changes by a smaller percentage than price. Reason one: few close substitutes. Other cafés sell the same coffee, but not by the sea with those views. So customers are unlikely to switch. Use the data. Customers already pay one euro twenty more than elsewhere. That is one hundred and fifty per cent more, and they still come. Reason two: a coffee is a small share of income. A small price rise hardly touches a tourist's budget, so they buy nearly as much. Conclude. A price rise brings a smaller percentage fall in sales, so the café could raise its total revenue. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is saying inelastic means people buy exactly the same. They still buy less, just by a smaller percentage. No change at all is perfectly inelastic. Another is getting the revenue rule backwards. With elastic demand, a price rise cuts revenue.

Now you try. A town's only bus company raises fares by twenty per cent, and journeys fall by five per cent. Calculate P E D, classify it, give a reason, and state the revenue effect. Pause here and work it out.

P E D is minus five divided by twenty, which is minus nought point two five. Ignore the sign: nought point two five is between zero and one, so demand is inelastic. There is only one bus company, so there is no other bus to switch to, and people keep travelling. The fare rise therefore increases total revenue. Remember: define P E D, classify the value, apply a reason to the case, and link it to revenue. Now try the practice questions.
