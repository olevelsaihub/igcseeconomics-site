Your friend's cake stall sells out every weekend. So she opens a second stall across town. That is a firm growing: more outlets, more customers, more sales. Firms can also grow by joining with others. A merger is when two firms agree to join into one. A takeover is when one firm buys control of another, usually by buying over half its shares. Growing needs finance: the money a firm uses to run and expand. It can come from retained profit, bank loans or the owner's savings.

Step one, know the factors that influence growth: access to finance, economies of scale, spreading risk, taking over competitors, and government regulation. Step two, develop how the factor drives growth. Finance pays for expansion. Scale lowers unit cost. More places spread risk. Buying a rival raises market share. Step three, for a twelve-mark Evaluate, weigh benefits against risks: borrowing costs, diseconomies of scale, and weak demand in the new place. Then conclude.

Here is a real question from June 2024. Anastasia runs a martial arts club in District sixteen of Budapest. After a successful few years, she may open a second club. Analyse two possible factors. Six marks. First, the case facts. One club, six classes a week, children and adults, and a successful few years. Factor one, access to finance. The club has done well, so it probably has profit to reinvest, and banks are more willing to lend. That money pays for a building, mats and instructors. Factor two, spreading risk. All her income comes from District sixteen. If a rival opened nearby, revenue could fall sharply. A club in another district spreads that risk. Both factors make a second club attractive for Anastasia. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is mixing up a merger and a takeover. Another is naming a factor without saying how it affects the decision to grow.

Now you try. Priya owns a busy phone repair shop. A rival repair shop on the same street is for sale. Explain one reason why Priya might buy it. Pause here and work it out.

Taking over a competitor. If Priya buys the rival, there is one less shop competing for customers on her street. Her market share rises, and she gains the rival's profits too. Remember: pick the factor the case supports, show how it drives growth, and use the case facts. Now try the practice questions.
