Idea. Think of a loaf of bread. A farmer grows the wheat. A bakery turns it into bread. A shop sells the bread to you. Three different kinds of work. The primary sector takes from nature: farming, fishing, mining. The secondary sector makes or builds things: factories and construction. The tertiary sector provides services, such as teaching, banking and shops. In a typical developed country, most people work in services, perhaps three quarters. Only a few work in the primary sector. This chart is only an example, not real data. In developing countries, most work in the primary sector. Why? As incomes rise, people spend more on services such as health, education and holidays. So service firms grow and hire more people.

Method. Step one, classify. Taking from nature is primary. Making or building is secondary. Providing a service is tertiary. Step two, for a trend, read the data: which sector grows and which shrinks. Step three, build a cause chain, set in the named country.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2023. Secondary jobs have fallen in Germany, the UK and the US, as goods are now made in low-cost Asian countries. Tertiary jobs have risen. Analyse why. Six marks. Start by defining the two sectors. The secondary sector makes goods, such as cars. The tertiary sector provides services, such as banking. Chain one. Goods cost less to make in Asia. Firms in Germany, the UK and the US cannot match those prices, so factories close or cut staff. Secondary jobs fall. Chain two. Incomes in these countries are high and tend to rise. People spend more on eating out, holidays and health care. Service firms expand and hire. Put them together: jobs are pushed out of manufacturing and pulled into services. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is putting house building in the tertiary sector. Building makes something, so it is secondary. Another is saying jobs moved to services because people wanted to. Give the cause: incomes rose, so demand for services rose.

Your turn. Now you try. In the country of Taluma, families now earn higher incomes, and more people work in restaurants and hotels. Explain one reason for the rise in tertiary jobs. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Incomes in Taluma have risen. Families can afford to eat out and go on holiday more. So restaurants and hotels need more staff, and tertiary jobs rise. Remember: classify the sector, read the trend, and chain the cause through to jobs in context. Now try the practice questions.
