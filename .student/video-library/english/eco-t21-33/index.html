Think of a school canteen. Training the cooks and buying a bigger oven lets it serve more lunches every day. Supply-side policy does this for a whole country. Supply-side policy means government measures that raise the economy's productive capacity: how much it is able to produce. On a production possibility curve, the whole curve shifts outwards. Four common measures. Education and training match skills to vacancies. Infrastructure, such as roads and broadband, cuts firms' costs. Lower business tax leaves profit to invest. Lower income tax gives people a reason to work more. There are limits. These policies are costly, may cut tax revenue, take a long time to work, and firms or workers may not respond.

Step one, define supply-side policy. Step two, name the measure and how it works. Step three, link it to productivity, output and jobs. Step four, use the case figures. Infrastructure works in two time frames. In the short run, building creates jobs. In the long run, firms' costs fall and they reach wider markets.

Here is a real question from June 2025. Ghana agreed to build a thirty-one point two kilometre highway costing fifty million dollars, part of a sixty-seven kilometre road linking central Ghana to the north and to neighbouring countries. Analyse how road spending could benefit Ghana. Six marks. Pick out the data first. Each figure can anchor one chain. Chain one. Building the thirty-one point two kilometres creates jobs for builders and suppliers. They spend their wages locally, raising demand. Chain two. The full road links central Ghana to the north. Journeys become faster and cheaper, so firms' costs fall, and northern firms reach bigger markets. Chain three. The road reaches neighbouring countries, so exporting is easier. Overall, productivity and capacity rise, and Ghana can produce more. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is calling interest rate cuts supply-side policy. They are monetary policy, and they boost demand, not capacity. Another is praising training without asking what kind of unemployment it is. Training works best when workers lack the skills employers need.

Now you try. Country T has many unfilled jobs in its tech industry. It spends two hundred million dollars on tech training for unemployed workers. The figure is just an example. Explain how this could reduce unemployment. Pause here and work it out.

The training gives unemployed workers tech skills. Their skills now match the unfilled vacancies, so firms hire them. Structural unemployment falls, and output rises.
