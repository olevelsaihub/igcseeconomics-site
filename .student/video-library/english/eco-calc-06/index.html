A café sells forty smoothies at three dollars each. How much money comes in? Total revenue is all the money a firm receives from selling its output. Multiply price by quantity sold. Three times forty is one hundred and twenty dollars. You can rearrange it. Quantity is revenue divided by price. One hundred and twenty dollars divided by three dollars is forty smoothies. If sales are given week by week, add them first. Forty, fifty-five and forty-five make one hundred and forty. Then one hundred and forty times three is four hundred and twenty dollars.

Steps one and two. Find the selling price and the quantity sold. Ignore any cost per unit or average cost: costs are not revenue. Steps three and four. Multiply price by quantity, write the working, and give the answer with the currency sign.

Here is a real question from June 2023. A firm sells its product at seventy-five euros a unit, with a cost per unit of thirty-five euros. Calculate total revenue when monthly sales are two hundred and fifty units. Two marks. Pick out the price and the quantity. The cost of thirty-five euros is a distractor: costs are not revenue. Seventy-five times two hundred and fifty is eighteen thousand seven hundred and fifty euros. Write the euro sign. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is multiplying by the cost per unit instead of the price. Another is leaving out the currency sign on the final answer.

Now you try. A bakery sells cupcakes at two dollars fifty each. It sells one hundred and twenty on Saturday and eighty on Sunday. Each cupcake costs ninety cents to make. Calculate total revenue for the weekend. Pause here and work it out.

Add the sales first: one hundred and twenty plus eighty is two hundred cupcakes. Ignore the ninety-cent cost. Two dollars fifty times two hundred is five hundred dollars. Check it. Saturday brings three hundred dollars and Sunday two hundred: five hundred in total. Remember: find price and quantity, ignore the costs, multiply, and add the currency sign.
