Your school has a rule: no phones in class. Break it, and there is a penalty. Because of the rule and the penalty, most pupils obey. Governments control pollution in the same way. A negative externality is harm to third parties, like pollution. Three controls tackle it. Regulation is a legal rule. A fine is a money penalty for breaking a rule. A pollution permit is legal permission to release a limited amount of pollution. With permits, the total is capped, here at one hundred tonnes. Firm A pollutes only forty tonnes, so it sells ten tonnes of spare permits. Firm B must buy them. Cleaner firms earn money.

Step one, define the control. Step two, name the externality and who it harms. Step three, explain the mechanism. A ban stops the activity. A fine makes harm costly. Permits cap pollution and reward cleaner firms. Step four, use the case evidence. For Assess, weigh it: rules cost money to monitor and enforce, fines may be too low, and the right limit is hard to set. Then judge.

Here is a real question from June 2024. Kenya has banned single-use plastic bags for over five years. Assess the effectiveness of government regulation when dealing with externalities such as pollution. Nine marks. The externality. Dumped bags cause visual pollution, and livestock that eat them can die. Farmers and the public bear costs they did not choose. The mechanism. Bags are illegal and can be punished, so shops and shoppers switch to alternatives, and far fewer bags are dumped. The evidence. The data says the environment is cleaner, with less danger to livestock. But the problem has not been removed totally. The limits. Some people still use bags illegally. Enforcement costs money, which has an opportunity cost. Success needs public cooperation, and bags are only one source of pollution. Judge. Regulation has been quite effective in Kenya, with a visible clean-up. Long-run success depends on strict enforcement. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is saying a pollution permit lets a firm pollute as much as it likes. The key is the cap, and the trading. Another is saying a fine stops pollution, without explaining why.

Now you try. A city fines drivers two hundred dollars for parking in bus lanes. Explain how the fine may reduce an external cost. Pause here and work it out.

A two hundred dollar fine makes parking in a bus lane costly, so fewer drivers do it. Buses are no longer blocked, so passengers, who are third parties, face fewer delays. Remember: define the control, name the externality, explain how behaviour changes, use the evidence, then weigh and judge. Now try the practice questions.
