In a busy pizza shop, one person makes the dough, another adds the toppings, and a third bakes. Nobody makes a whole pizza alone, yet pizzas come out faster. This is division of labour. It means splitting the production process into small, separate tasks, with each worker doing just one task. It is a form of specialisation. For example, three workers each making whole pizzas make thirty an hour. Split into tasks, the same three make forty-five. Output per worker, called productivity, rises from ten to fifteen.

For the firm, workers get faster and more skilled, no time is lost switching tasks or tools, and less training is needed. But bored workers make mistakes and may leave, and one absence can stop the whole line. For workers, they become expert at their task and may feel more confident. But doing one thing all day is boring, they gain few skills, and the task may be automated. Faster work cuts the cost of each unit. If the three workers cost one hundred and fifty dollars an hour, each pizza costs five dollars at thirty an hour, but three dollars thirty-three at forty-five. So the method is: name the point, develop it with because and so, and use the product and workplace in the case. For Assess and Evaluate, weigh the drawbacks, and other ways to raise productivity, like training or technology.

Here is a real question from June 2023. A firm makes tennis balls in several stages, using division of labour. Explain one advantage of division of labour for the firm. Three marks. Identify the advantage. Each worker does one stage of making the tennis balls, so they become highly skilled at it. Develop it. Because they repeat the same stage all day and never switch jobs, they work faster and make fewer mistakes. Finish in context. The firm makes more tennis balls per worker each hour, so the average cost of each ball falls. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is defining division of labour as dividing workers into groups. It is about splitting production into tasks. Another is giving an advantage for workers when the question asks about the firm, or the other way round.

Now you try. A bakery splits bread-making into mixing, shaping, baking and packing, and each worker does one stage. Explain one disadvantage of division of labour for the workers. Pause here and work it out.

One disadvantage is boring, repetitive work. A worker who only shapes loaves all day finds the job monotonous, and gains few other skills. If shaping is automated, the job could go. Remember: define it in two parts, name one point, develop it with because and so, and use the case. Now try the practice questions.
