Competition between firms: benefits and drawbacks

Idea. Picture two cafés side by side on your street. If one charges too much, you simply walk next door. So each café works hard to win you. This is competition: rivalry between firms selling similar products, each trying to win customers and sales from the others. One firm may cut its price. Another may add extras. When more firms join a market, supply increases. The supply curve shifts right, so price falls and quantity rises. That is one way competition helps buyers. Competition also pushes firms to innovate. Innovation means turning a new idea or invention into a new product, or a better way of producing. The idea must be put to use. Competition has winners and losers. Consumers can gain lower prices, more choice and better quality. The economy gains efficient firms. But firms earn lower profits, quality may be cut, and some firms close.

Method. Step one, define the term if asked. Step two, decide whose view the question takes: consumers, firms or the economy. Step three, pick one effect and develop it: what firms do to win customers, and what that means for the group. Step four, use details from the case. Step five, for a twelve-mark Evaluate: benefits of rivalry, then drawbacks, then a supported conclusion.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2023. Dahab is a seaside town in Egypt with a large number of restaurants. Some specialise, some give discounts for regular visits, others offer live entertainment. Evaluate the likely impact of competition on restaurant customers. Twelve marks. Benefit one. With so many restaurants, a customer can walk next door if one charges too much. So owners keep prices low, and visitors spend less. Benefit two. Restaurants also compete apart from price: specialist food, discounts for regulars, live entertainment. Customers get more choice and better service. Now the drawback. Low prices squeeze profit per meal. Some owners may cut quality or staff, and the weakest may close, so choice shrinks. Judge. Dahab has a steady flow of visitors, and many return, so restaurants can stay profitable with low prices. The impact is likely mainly positive, but drawbacks would grow if visitor numbers fell. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying competition lowers prices without saying why. Explain that firms cut prices to attract their rivals' customers. Another is defining innovation as just a new invention. It must be put to use, in a product or a way of producing.

Your turn. Now you try. Three new phone repair shops open on a street that had only one. State one drawback for the original shop. Then explain one benefit for customers, apart from price. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. For the shop: it must cut prices to keep customers, so its profit falls. For customers: shops now compete on speed and service, so repairs become quicker and better.
