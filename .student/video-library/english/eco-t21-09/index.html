Read the narration

Last year, ten dollars bought five snacks at two dollars each. Now each snack costs two dollars fifty, so the same ten dollars buys only four. That is a fall in purchasing power: how much money can buy. Real wages compare pay with prices. If pay rises three per cent but prices rise ten per cent, real wages fall. Two named costs. Menu costs fall on firms: reprinting menus, price labels and websites. Shoe leather costs fall on buyers: the time and effort spent hunting for lower prices. High inflation also brings uncertainty. Firms and families find it hard to plan, confidence falls, and if prices rise faster than abroad, exports become less competitive. Deflation hurts too, because people delay buying.

Step one, start with prices and purchasing power, using the rate given. Step two, if a named cost is asked, define it in your own words. Step three, develop the knock-on effect: confidence, saving, borrowing, investment. Step four, tie every link to the data.

Here is a real question from November 2023. Inflation in Germany reached a record ten point four per cent, and is expected to stay high for two more years. Analyse the impact on consumer confidence. Six marks. Consumer confidence is how positive people feel about the economy and their own money. Chain one: prices rising ten point four per cent cut purchasing power. Unless wages keep up, living standards fall, so confidence drops. Chain two: worried households become cautious. They borrow less for cars or furniture, and save more as a safety net. Spending falls. Chain three: high inflation for two more years makes planning hard. Families cannot tell what prices or pay will be, so confidence weakens further. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is swapping the two named costs. Menu costs belong to firms. Shoe leather costs belong to buyers. Another is saying inflation always lowers living standards. It depends whether prices rise faster than wages.

Now you try. Inflation in Valdora is fifteen per cent. Restaurants reprint menus every month, and families spend hours comparing shop prices. Name each cost, then explain one effect on workers whose pay rose four per cent. Pause here and work it out.

Reprinting menus is a menu cost for the restaurants. Hours of comparing prices is a shoe leather cost for the families. Pay rose four per cent, but prices rose fifteen. Real wages fall, so these workers can buy less and their living standards drop. Remember: start with purchasing power, define any named cost, develop what people then do, and use the rate given.
