Two pizza shops each have two cooks. Shop A makes forty pizzas an hour. Shop B makes sixty. So each cook in shop A makes twenty an hour, and each cook in shop B makes thirty. This is productivity: how much output is made from a given amount of inputs, or in a given time. Labour productivity is output per worker. Higher productivity means more output from the same resources, so the cost of each unit usually falls. Across a whole economy, the production possibility curve shifts outward.

Step one, define productivity. Step two, name the improvement and the factor it works on. Fertiliser, irrigation, drainage and reclamation improve land. Education and training improve labour. New technology improves capital. Step three, build the chain. The improvement lets the same resources produce more, so output per input rises, so average cost falls. Step four, use the case: name the farmers, firm or country. In long answers, also weigh the cost, the time it takes, and the skills needed.

Here is a real question from November 2025. Extra money for draining farmland in India was recommended. Explain one way better drainage may affect the productivity of Indian farmers. Three marks. Identify. Drainage takes excess water off fields that would otherwise flood or stay waterlogged. Develop. Farmers can now grow crops on land that was too wet to use, and fewer crops rot. Context and effect. Indian farmers get more crops from the same land and work, so their productivity rises. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is mixing up productivity and production. Hiring more workers raises total output, but not output per worker. Another is mixing up irrigation and drainage. Irrigation adds water. Drainage removes it.

Now you try. A clothing factory in Bangladesh trains its sewing machine operators to use new, faster machines. Explain one way this may affect the productivity of its workers. Pause here and work it out.

Training builds human capital: the skills of the workers. Skilled operators sew faster and make fewer mistakes. So each worker at the Bangladesh factory makes more clothes per hour, and labour productivity rises. Remember: define productivity, name the improvement and its factor, chain it to more output from the same inputs, and use the case. Now try the practice questions.
