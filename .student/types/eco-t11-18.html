Knowledge card

Market failure, public goods and free riders

Written
  • Market failure happens when a free market does not allocate resources efficiently, so society does not get the best use of its scarce resources.
  • Causes include externalities, public goods (a missing market), lack of information, lack of competition and factor immobility.
  • A public good is non-excludable (people who do not pay cannot be stopped from benefiting) and non-rival (one person using it does not leave less for others). Examples: street lighting, defence, flood defences.
  • A free rider is someone who gets the benefit of a good but pays nothing towards it.
  • Because of free riders, private firms cannot earn enough revenue from a public good, so they will not supply it. The government provides it instead, paid for by taxes.
  • Lack of competition can mean higher prices and less choice; lack of information can mean consumers pay more than they need to.

Before you begin

  • Scarcity and the allocation of resources (ECO-T11-02).
  • Public and private sectors (ECO-T11-16).

The method

  1. Define market failure (inefficient allocation) or the free rider, whichever the question names.

    Why: The definition anchors the answer.

  2. Identify the cause in the case: public good, lack of information, lack of competition.

    Why: The cause must come from the case data.

  3. For a public good, show it is non-excludable and non-rival, so people can benefit without paying.

    Why: These two features are the core of the free rider chain.

  4. Explain the consequence: no private supply, under-supply, higher prices or wasted spending.

    Why: This shows why the outcome is inefficient.

  5. Link to government action: providing the good through taxes or intervening. For 6-mark items, follow the Analyse card.

    Why: Completes the chain the question asks about.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying a public good is any good provided by the government, without the two features.
  • ✗
    Mixing up non-excludable (cannot stop non-payers) and non-rival (use does not reduce what is left).
  • ✗
    Defining a free rider as someone who gets a good free from the government, rather than someone who benefits without paying.
  • ✗
    Naming market failure without explaining why the allocation is inefficient in the case.
  • ✗
    Not linking the free rider problem to the lack of profit for private firms.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Define the free rider.
  2. Show street lighting is non-excludable, then non-rival.
  3. Explain why people will not pay, so a private firm makes no profit and will not supply it.
  4. Link to a missing market and to government provision through taxes.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A free rider is someone who gets the benefit of a good but pays nothing towards it. Street lighting is a public good, and public goods have two features that create this problem. First, street lighting is non-excludable. Once a street light is switched on, nobody can stop a pedestrian or motorist on that street from seeing by it, whether or not they have paid. Second, it is non-rival: one driver using the light to see the road does not leave less light for the next pedestrian. Because of these features, each person knows they will get the extra safety and visibility anyway, so they have no reason to pay. If a private firm tried to sell street lighting, very few people would pay, so the firm would earn little or no revenue and could not make a profit. Private firms would therefore not supply it, leaving a missing market, which is a form of market failure: people want safe, well-lit streets, but the free market would provide none. The government solves this by providing street lighting itself and funding it through taxes, which everyone has to pay. In this way the safety benefits for pedestrians and motorists, and the more inviting streets after dark, are provided for the whole community.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): free rider defined, non-excludability and non-rivalry both explained with street lighting, the chain to no private supply developed, and the link to government provision funded by taxes.
