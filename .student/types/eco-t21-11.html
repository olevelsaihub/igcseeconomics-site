Knowledge card

Unemployment: meaning, measurement and types

Written
  • Unemployment means people who are willing and able to work, and are looking for a job, but cannot find one.
  • The International Labour Organisation (ILO) method is the standard way to measure unemployment. CPI measures inflation and GDP measures growth.
  • Unemployment rate = number unemployed / labour force x 100. The labour force is everyone in work plus everyone unemployed, not the whole population.
  • Cyclical unemployment: caused by a fall in demand in a downturn or recession (for example an airline cutting jobs in a global recession).
  • Structural unemployment: a whole industry declines or technology changes, so workers' skills no longer match the jobs available (for example a coal mine closing, or machines replacing workers).
  • Seasonal unemployment: work is only needed at certain times of the year (for example hotel or farm jobs out of season, or building work stopping in the monsoon).
  • Frictional unemployment: people out of work for a short time while moving between jobs. Voluntary unemployment: people choose not to take jobs, for example because benefits are generous or taxes are high.

Before you begin

  • What a labour force is: people working plus people looking for work
  • The phases of the economic cycle, especially recession
  • The difference between primary, secondary and tertiary industries
1

The method

  1. Define unemployment: willing and able to work, but without a job.

    Why: Both parts of the definition earn a mark each.

  2. Find the cause in the question or option: falling demand, a skills mismatch or declining industry, time of year, changing jobs, or choice.

    Why: Each type is defined by its cause.

  3. Match the cause to the type: cyclical, structural, seasonal, frictional, voluntary.

    Why: Most multiple-choice items test exactly this matching.

  4. For Explain, say why that cause leaves people without work, then apply it to the sector or data in the question.

    Why: The three marks are reason, development and context.

  5. For a 6-mark Analyse or 9-mark Assess, follow those cards: build chains from the cause to job losses using the data, and for Assess weigh limits such as new jobs created or retraining.

    Why: Higher marks need developed, applied chains and, for Assess, balance.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixing up structural and cyclical: a permanent decline or new technology is structural; a temporary fall in demand in a recession is cyclical.
  • ✗
    Calling job losses from machines 'frictional' or 'seasonal' instead of structural.
  • ✗
    Dividing the number unemployed by the whole population instead of by the labour force.
  • ✗
    Defining unemployment as 'people without a job', leaving out willing and able to work.
  • ✗
    In Explain answers, naming the type correctly but not saying why the cause leaves people jobless in that context.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the data: youth unemployment (ages 16 to 24) in China was 14.9% in December 2023; new graduates far outnumbered available jobs, most of which were lower-skilled.
  2. Identify the reason: a mismatch between the skills workers have and the skills jobs need.
  3. Develop: graduates are trained for higher-skilled work, but most vacancies are lower-skilled, so they do not match.
  4. Apply: this leaves many young Chinese graduates unemployed, helping explain the 14.9% rate.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One reason is a mismatch between workers' skills and the jobs available. In China, many young people have been trained at university for higher-skilled careers, but most of the vacancies are in lower-skilled sectors, so their qualifications do not fit the jobs on offer. As a result, many new graduates cannot find suitable work, which helps explain why unemployment among 16 to 24 year olds reached 14.9% in December 2023.

How the marks are earned

3 marks (AO2): 1 for identifying the reason (skills mismatch), 1 for developing it (graduates trained for higher-skilled work but jobs are mostly lower-skilled), 1 for context (China's young graduates and the 14.9% rate).

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Assess · November 2024 · Paper 2 · Q2(g)

Answer plan

  1. Define structural unemployment as a skills mismatch caused by changes such as new technology.
  2. For: AI can replace traders, salespeople, basic coders and journalists at lower cost.
  3. Data: IBM stopped recruiting for 7,800 jobs; BT expects AI to replace 10,000 jobs by 2030.
  4. Against: AI creates jobs in developing and maintaining systems and in new industries.
  5. Against: higher productivity and growth may create non-routine and creative jobs; losses are gradual.
  6. Judge: likely rise for routine workers; depends on job creation and access to retraining.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Structural unemployment happens when there is a long-term mismatch between the skills workers have and the skills employers want. It is often caused by changes in technology or in the pattern of demand, which make some jobs disappear while new ones need different skills.

AI could raise structural unemployment. AI systems such as ChatGPT can perform tasks normally done by humans, often more cheaply and quickly. The data says jobs at risk include Wall Street traders, salespeople, writers of basic computer code and journalists. If firms can replace these workers with AI, they will employ fewer people in these roles. IBM has already stopped recruiting for 7,800 jobs that could be replaced by AI, and BT predicts AI will replace 10,000 jobs by 2030. The workers who lose these jobs may not have the skills needed for the new jobs that are being created, such as building and running AI systems. Retraining takes time and money, so they may stay unemployed for a long period, which is structural unemployment.

However, the impact may be smaller than feared. AI also creates new jobs: people are needed to develop, maintain and run AI systems, and new industries may grow around the technology. AI can raise productivity, which lowers costs and prices and can lead to economic growth, creating jobs in other areas, especially creative and non-routine roles that AI cannot easily do. In many cases AI may change jobs rather than remove them, with workers using AI to do their work faster. IBM has paused hiring rather than cutting staff, and BT expects its 10,000 reductions only by 2030, so the change is gradual, giving time for workers to retrain or for natural staff turnover, rather than sudden mass redundancies.

Overall, AI is likely to increase structural unemployment for some groups of workers, especially those doing routine tasks, at least in the short to medium term. How large the effect is depends on how quickly AI replaces jobs, how many new jobs are created, and whether governments and firms provide training so that workers can move into the new roles.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines structural unemployment and its cause (AO2); chains AI substitution to job losses and skills mismatch, using IBM's 7,800 jobs and BT's 10,000 by 2030 (AO3); weighs new jobs, productivity-led growth, job change and gradual timing, ending in a judgement conditional on job creation and retraining (AO4).
