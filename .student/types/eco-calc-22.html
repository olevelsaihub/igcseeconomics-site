Knowledge card

Trade and current account balances

Calculation

A balance is exports − imports. Goods give the visible balance, services the invisible balance; add both for the current account. Minus is a deficit.

The calculation

Balance = exports − imports

Current account = visible balance + invisible balance.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Keep the minus sign: it shows a deficit.
  • Write the unit, e.g. $bn.

Try itGoods: exports $56bn, imports $48bn. Services: exports $10bn, imports $15bn. Find the current account balance.

  1. Visible: 56 − 48 = 8
  2. Invisible: 10 − 15 = −5
  3. 8 + (−5) = $3bn surplus

5

The method

  1. Pick the flows

    Goods (visible), services (invisible) or both (current account).

  2. Subtract

    Exports − imports, written out.

  3. Add if needed

    Current account = visible + invisible.

  4. Sign and unit

    Minus is a deficit. Add $bn.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong rows

    Visible is goods, not services.

  • ✗
    Reversed

    Imports − exports flips the sign.

  • ✗
    Added

    Never add exports to imports.

Worked explanation

  1. Visible trade means goods only.
  2. Exports of goods = €150bn; imports of goods = €120bn.
  3. Visible trade balance = €150bn - €120bn = €30bn, a surplus.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Visible trade balance = exports of goods - imports of goods = €150bn - €120bn = €30bn (a visible trade surplus).

How the marks are earned

Two marks. One for €150bn - €120bn. One for €30bn with the currency and units. The correct answer alone earns both.
