Knowledge card

Draw a tariff, subsidy or indirect tax change

Written

A tariff or indirect tax raises suppliers' costs, so supply shifts left. A subsidy cuts costs, so supply shifts right. Demand does not move.

4

The method

  1. Which way?

    Tariff or tax: left. Subsidy: right. Removed: opposite.

  2. Draw S1

    Parallel to S, labelled S1.

  3. New crossing

    Where S1 meets the unchanged D.

  4. Dashed lines

    Across to P1, down to Q1.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Moving demand

    Only supply shifts.

  • ✗
    Removal

    Removing a subsidy shifts supply left.

  • ✗
    No labels

    Label S1, P1 and Q1.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Cause: China introduces a tariff on imported large tyres, a tax that raises suppliers' costs.
  2. Direction: higher costs mean supply shifts left.
  3. Draw S1 to the left of S, parallel to it, labelled S1.
  4. Mark where S1 meets D; read across to P1 above Pe and down to Q1 left of Qe.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

On the diagram I draw a new supply curve S1 to the left of the original supply curve S, parallel to it, and label it S1 (tariff). The new curve crosses the unchanged demand curve D at a higher point. From this point I draw a dashed line across to the price axis and label it P1, which is above Pe, showing the price of large tyres has risen. I draw a dashed line down to the quantity axis and label it Q1, which is to the left of Qe, showing that fewer large tyres are bought.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: supply shifted left and labelled S1. 1 mark: new equilibrium price P1, higher than Pe, labelled. 1 mark: new equilibrium quantity Q1, lower than Qe, labelled.
