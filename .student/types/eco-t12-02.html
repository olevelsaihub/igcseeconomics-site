The method

  1. Classify

    Nature = primary; making = secondary; service = tertiary.

  2. Read the trend

    Which sector grows or shrinks, in which country?

  3. Build a chain

    Incomes rise, so service demand rises, so tertiary jobs grow.

  4. Use the data

    Name the country and quote the figures.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Building as a service

    Construction is secondary.

  • ✗
    A fourth sector

    'Service' is the tertiary sector.

  • ✗
    No reason

    Link the shift to incomes or costs.

  • ✗
    No context

    Use the named country and data.

What a complete response includes

Short items reward accurate sector classification. Longer items reward a cause-and-effect chain for the shift in employment, tied to the countries in the data, and, for Assess, a weighed judgement about the sector's importance.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Pick out the data: secondary jobs falling in Germany, the UK and the US; goods made in low-cost Asian countries; tertiary jobs rising.
  2. Define the two sectors so the reader knows what is moving.
  3. Chain one: lower costs abroad, home manufacturers cannot compete, factories close or cut staff, secondary jobs fall.
  4. Chain two: rising incomes, more demand for services, tertiary firms hire, workers retrain into services.
  5. Link the two chains together to explain the shift.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The secondary sector makes goods from raw materials, for example cars and clothing, while the tertiary sector provides services such as restaurants, banking and entertainment. In Germany, the UK and the US, secondary employment has fallen because goods can now be made more cheaply in low-cost Asian countries, where wages and other costs are lower. Firms in developed countries find it hard to match these lower prices, so some lose sales and either close or cut the number of workers they employ, or move their own factories abroad. With fewer manufacturing jobs available, fewer young people choose to train for them. At the same time, incomes in developed countries are high and have tended to rise. When people are richer, they spend a larger share of their income on services such as eating out, holidays, health care and entertainment. Firms providing these services therefore expand and need more staff, so job opportunities grow in the tertiary sector. Workers who lose manufacturing jobs, and new workers, move into these service jobs. Together, cheaper production abroad pushing jobs out of manufacturing and rising demand for services pulling workers in explain the shift from the secondary to the tertiary sector.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked out of 6 (3 application, 3 analysis). Defining the sectors and naming the causes gives knowledge; using the data (named countries, low-cost Asian producers) gives application; two developed chains, cost competition reducing secondary jobs and rising incomes raising service demand, give the thorough analysis for Level 3.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Assess · June 2023 · Paper 1 · Q3(e)

Answer plan

  1. Define primary, secondary and tertiary sectors; manufacturing is secondary.
  2. For: manufacturing provides products and jobs, and incomes are spent across the economy.
  3. For: its tax revenue funds health care and education (tertiary); services like transport and retail need manufactured goods.
  4. Against: the tertiary sector also earns large incomes and taxes and relies on skills, not only goods.
  5. Against: the secondary sector needs primary raw materials; goods can be imported instead.
  6. Judge: a key role; depends on the stage of development and how easily goods can be imported.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

An economy has three sectors. The primary sector extracts raw materials, such as farming and mining. The secondary sector turns those raw materials into finished goods, for example manufacturing and construction. The tertiary sector provides services, such as health care, education, banking and retail.

The secondary sector plays an important role. Manufacturing provides many of the products people use every day. It also creates jobs, so workers earn incomes which they spend in other businesses, supporting further output and employment. Firms and workers in manufacturing pay taxes, and the data shows the government can use this tax revenue to provide health care and education, which are tertiary services. So a strong secondary sector helps pay for the services that raise living standards. Many services also depend directly on manufactured goods: transport services need vehicles, shops need finished goods to sell, and hospitals need equipment and medicines. Without manufacturing, much of the tertiary sector could not operate. Manufactured goods can also be exported, earning income from abroad.

However, the secondary sector is not the only important sector, and in many economies it is not the largest. Tertiary services such as banking, insurance, health care and education also generate large incomes and tax revenue, which governments can use to fund many things, including support for manufacturing. Services depend on the skills of workers such as doctors, teachers and accountants, not only on manufactured goods. The secondary sector itself depends on the primary sector for raw materials, so all three sectors rely on each other. In addition, a country can import many manufactured goods instead of making them, so its services can still run with a small manufacturing sector.

Overall, the secondary sector plays a key role by producing goods, creating jobs and providing tax revenue that funds tertiary services such as health care and education. How important it is depends on the stage of development of the economy: in developing economies it often drives growth, while in developed economies the tertiary sector usually provides more output and jobs. Its role also depends on how easily the country can import the goods it needs instead.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines the three sectors (AO2); chains manufacturing to products, jobs, incomes and tax revenue that funds health care and education, and links services' reliance on goods (AO3); balances with tertiary income and skills, primary inputs and imports, ending in a judgement depending on stage of development (AO4).
