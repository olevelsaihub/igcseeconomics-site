4

The method

  1. Developed or developing?

    This decides which slice is biggest.

  2. Halve the circle

    Draw a line through the centre first.

  3. Size the slices

    Developed: T over half, P tiny. Developing: P over half.

  4. Label

    Write P, S and T in each slice.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    P under half

    For a developing country, P must pass the half line.

  • ✗
    S biggest when developed

    Services (T) are largest.

  • ✗
    No labels

    Every slice needs P, S or T.

What a complete response includes

Three marks, each for one feature of the slice sizes. Developed: T largest, S second, P smallest. Developing: P largest, P at least half, remainder split between S and T. Clear labels are essential.

Worked explanation

  1. Haiti is a developing economy, so most workers are in farming: P will be the largest slice.
  2. Draw a vertical line through the centre to halve the circle.
  3. Make P cover the whole right half plus a bit more (around 55-60%).
  4. Split the remaining slice into S (smaller, around 10-15%) and T (around 30%).
  5. Label each slice P, S and T.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Inside the circle I draw three slices from the centre. The slice labelled P (primary) covers more than half the circle, about 60%, so it is clearly the largest. The remaining 40% is split into two slices: one labelled T (tertiary), about 30%, and one labelled S (secondary), about 10%. This shows that in a developing economy such as Haiti most people work in farming and other primary activities, with the rest shared between services and manufacturing.

How the marks are earned

One mark for P being the largest slice, one for P covering half or more of the circle, and one for the leftover part being split between S and T. All three need clear P, S, T labels.
