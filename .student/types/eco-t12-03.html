Knowledge card

Draw a sector pie chart for a country

Written
  • A pie chart is a circle split into slices; each slice shows a share of the whole (here, the share of employment or output in each sector).
  • P = primary (farming, mining), S = secondary (manufacturing, building), T = tertiary (services).
  • Developed country (e.g. France, Japan): T is the biggest slice (well over half), S is second, P is the smallest (a thin slice).
  • Developing country (e.g. Haiti, Nepal): P is the biggest slice and is at least half of the circle; the rest is shared between S and T.
  • Sizes only need to be approximate, but the order of sizes and the half-way point must be clear.

Before you begin

  • What the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors are.
  • The difference between developed and developing economies.
  • How to read a pie chart (bigger slice = bigger share).
1

The method

  1. Decide if the named country is developed or developing.

    Why: The ranking of slices depends entirely on this.

  2. Draw a line from the centre straight up and another straight down, splitting the circle in half, then divide the remaining parts.

    Why: A half-way line makes it obvious whether a slice is more or less than 50%.

  3. Developed: make T clearly more than half, S about a quarter, P a small slice of under a tenth.

    Why: Each mark is for one slice being in the right size position.

  4. Developing: make P more than half, then split the rest into S and T.

    Why: Marks go for P being the largest, P being half or more, and the rest shared between S and T.

  5. Write P, S and T clearly inside (or beside) each slice.

    Why: An unlabelled slice cannot earn a mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawing a developing country with P only just the biggest but under half of the circle.
  • ✗
    Making S the largest slice for a developed country; services are largest.
  • ✗
    Leaving slices unlabelled or using words that do not match P, S and T.
  • ✗
    Drawing three equal slices so no size order can be seen.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Now see it used on a real past-paper question

What a complete response includes

Three marks, each for one feature of the slice sizes. Developed: T largest, S second, P smallest. Developing: P largest, P at least half, remainder split between S and T. Clear labels are essential.

Worked explanation

  1. Haiti is a developing economy, so most workers are in farming: P will be the largest slice.
  2. Draw a vertical line through the centre to halve the circle.
  3. Make P cover the whole right half plus a bit more (around 55-60%).
  4. Split the remaining slice into S (smaller, around 10-15%) and T (around 30%).
  5. Label each slice P, S and T.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Inside the circle I draw three slices from the centre. The slice labelled P (primary) covers more than half the circle, about 60%, so it is clearly the largest. The remaining 40% is split into two slices: one labelled T (tertiary), about 30%, and one labelled S (secondary), about 10%. This shows that in a developing economy such as Haiti most people work in farming and other primary activities, with the rest shared between services and manufacturing.

How the marks are earned

One mark for P being the largest slice, one for P covering half or more of the circle, and one for the leftover part being split between S and T. All three need clear P, S, T labels.
