Knowledge card

Equilibrium, excess demand and excess supply

Written
  • Equilibrium price is the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, so the market clears with nothing left over.
  • Excess supply (a surplus) happens when the price is above equilibrium: quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded and goods go unsold.
  • Excess demand (a shortage) happens when the price is below equilibrium: quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied.
  • Size of the excess = the gap between quantity supplied and quantity demanded at that price.
  • Market forces remove a surplus: sellers cut the price, quantity demanded extends and quantity supplied contracts until they are equal.
  • Market forces remove a shortage: the price rises, quantity demanded contracts and quantity supplied extends until they are equal.

Before you begin

  • Demand and supply curves and movements along them (ECO-T11-07).
  • How to read a demand and supply diagram.
1

The method

  1. Define equilibrium as the price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

    Why: It is the target the market moves towards.

  2. At the given price, read off quantity demanded and quantity supplied and name the surplus or shortage with its size.

    Why: Using the figures is the AO3 data mark.

  3. Explain which way the price moves (down for a surplus, up for a shortage) and why.

    Why: This is the first link in the market forces chain.

  4. Explain the extension and contraction along each curve as price changes.

    Why: Shows how the gap closes, not just that it does.

  5. End at the new equilibrium price and quantity from the data. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card.

    Why: A complete sequence earns the top level.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixing up the two: unsold goods mean excess supply, not excess demand.
  • ✗
    Saying the curves shift to remove a surplus, when the price change causes movements along them.
  • ✗
    Stating that the price falls without explaining the changes in quantity demanded and supplied.
  • ✗
    Not quoting the quantities and equilibrium price from the diagram.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Define market forces and excess supply.
  2. Read the diagram at 10 000 Kip: 8 journeys supplied, 4 demanded, a surplus of 4.
  3. Explain why drivers cut the price.
  4. Explain demand extending and supply contracting.
  5. End at 8 000 Kip and 6 journeys.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Market forces are the forces of demand and supply that set the price in a free market. Excess supply exists when, at the current price, the quantity supplied is greater than the quantity demanded. At 10 000 Kip, eight LOCA drivers want to offer journeys but passengers want only four, so there is an excess supply of four journeys and some drivers wait with no customers. Those drivers earn nothing while they wait, so they have a reason to lower their fares to win passengers. As the price falls, two things happen. Taxi journeys become better value, so the quantity demanded extends along the demand curve. At the same time, some drivers decide the lower fare is not worth their time, so the quantity supplied contracts along the supply curve. The price keeps falling until the two quantities are equal. This happens at 8 000 Kip, where six journeys are demanded and six are supplied. At this equilibrium price the excess supply has disappeared and the market clears without any government action.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): excess supply and market forces defined, the gap of four journeys read from the data, the price fall explained, extension of demand and contraction of supply both shown, ending at 8 000 Kip and six journeys.

Calculation check

  • 8-4 = 4
