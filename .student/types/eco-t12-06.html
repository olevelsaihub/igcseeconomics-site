Knowledge card

Revenue, costs and profit: meanings and formulas

Written
  • Total revenue (TR) is all the money a firm receives from selling its products: TR = price x quantity sold.
  • Fixed costs do not change when output changes (rent, insurance). Variable costs change with output (raw materials, pay per unit).
  • Total costs (TC) = total fixed costs + total variable costs.
  • Average cost (AC) = total cost / quantity produced. It is the cost of making one unit.
  • Profit = total revenue - total costs. If TC is bigger than TR, the firm makes a loss.
  • Profit rises if revenue rises faster than costs, or costs fall; it falls if costs rise (e.g. dearer materials) and prices cannot rise to cover them.

Before you begin

  • Basic arithmetic: add, subtract, multiply, divide.
  • The idea that firms aim to make a profit.
1

The method

  1. Write the formula first: TR = P x Q; TC = TFC + TVC; AC = TC / Q; profit = TR - TC.

    Why: Many 1-mark parts are just the formula, and it anchors any definition.

  2. For a 2-mark meaning, give both parts: what is being measured and where it comes from (e.g. money received + from selling all output).

    Why: Each part earns one mark.

  3. In multiple choice, check which items the formula includes: average cost uses total cost, not only fixed or variable cost, and never revenue.

    Why: The wrong options are usually partial versions of the right formula.

  4. For profit effects, trace the change: what happens to costs, what happens to revenue, so what happens to TR - TC.

    Why: Analysis credit comes from linking the cost or revenue change to profit in context.

  5. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card and use the case's figures and actions.

    Why: Level 3 needs developed chains using the data.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Confusing revenue (money coming in) with profit (money left after costs).
  • ✗
    Choosing fixed cost / output as average cost; average cost uses total cost.
  • ✗
    Giving only half a definition, e.g. 'money a firm gets' without 'from selling its products'.
  • ✗
    In profit analysis, saying costs rise and stopping, without linking to the fall in profit.
  • ✗
    Mixing up fixed and variable costs (labour paid per unit is variable).

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Worked explanation

  1. Recall the formula: total revenue = price x quantity sold.
  2. Part 1: say what it is (the money received by a firm).
  3. Part 2: say where it comes from (selling all of its output).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Total revenue is the total money a firm takes in from selling all of its goods or services in a period; it is worked out as price multiplied by the quantity sold.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for saying it is money received by the firm, and 1 mark for linking it to the sales of all its products (or to price x quantity).
