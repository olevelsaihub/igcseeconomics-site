Knowledge card

Reading a production possibility curve and economic growth

Written
  • A production possibility curve (PPC, also called a PPF) shows the most of two goods an economy can make when it uses all its resources fully and efficiently.
  • A point on the curve means resources are fully employed. A point inside the curve means some resources are unemployed. A point outside the curve cannot be reached with today's resources.
  • Moving along the curve means making more of one good and less of the other. The amount of the other good given up is the opportunity cost.
  • An outward shift means positive economic growth: the economy can now make more of both goods. Causes include better technology, more investment in capital, more or better trained workers and higher productivity or efficiency.
  • An inward shift means negative growth. Causes include resources running out (such as oil or coal), skilled workers emigrating, or natural disasters.
  • Capital goods (machines, tools, buildings) are used to make other goods; consumer goods are bought by households to use. More capital goods today usually means more capacity in future.

Before you begin

  • Opportunity cost and scarcity (ECO-T11-02).
  • The four factors of production: land, labour, capital and enterprise.
  • How to read values from the axes of a graph.
1

The method

  1. Say what the PPC shows: maximum output of two goods using all available resources.

    Why: It sets up the meaning before you read the diagram.

  2. Classify what the question shows: a point (on, inside, outside), a movement along, or a shift (outward or inward).

    Why: Each one has a different meaning and earns different marks.

  3. Quote the figures from the diagram and work out the change in each good.

    Why: Using the data is how you reach the top level in Analyse questions.

  4. For a shift, give a cause (technology, investment, labour force, resource depletion, emigration) and explain how it changes productive capacity.

    Why: The cause-and-effect chain is the analysis.

  5. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: build two or three developed chains using the data and end with the impact on the economy.

    Why: Analyse marks reward linked reasoning, not a list.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying a point inside the curve is impossible to reach; it is reachable but wastes resources.
  • ✗
    Describing a shift of the whole curve as a movement along it.
  • ✗
    Giving a cause that would shrink the economy, such as higher unemployment, for an outward shift.
  • ✗
    Describing a move from A to B without stating how many units of the other good are given up.
  • ✗
    Forgetting that producing more capital goods raises future productive capacity.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Read point A: 350 million capital goods and 160 million consumer goods.
  2. Read point B: 450 million capital goods and 130 million consumer goods.
  3. Work out the changes: capital goods up 100 million, consumer goods down 30 million.
  4. Explain the trade-off (opportunity cost) because both points are on the curve.
  5. Explain why extra capital goods raise capacity and shift the PPC outwards in future.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A production possibility curve (PPC) shows the largest output of two types of goods an economy can make when it uses every resource fully. Points A and B both lie on the curve, so at each one the economy is fully using its resources. At A it makes 350 million units of capital goods and 160 million units of consumer goods. At B it makes 450 million units of capital goods, which is 100 million more. Because resources are scarce, the extra workers, land and machines needed for these capital goods have to be moved away from making consumer goods, so consumer goods fall to 130 million units, a drop of 30 million. These 30 million consumer goods are the opportunity cost of the extra capital goods. In the short run households have fewer goods to enjoy, so living standards may dip. However, capital goods are machines, tools and buildings that are used to make other products. With 100 million more of them, the economy's productive capacity rises, so in future the whole PPC can shift outwards. This is positive economic growth: later the economy could produce more capital goods and more consumer goods together than it can at either A or B today.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): accurate meaning of the PPC, the figures used to show the 100 million gain and 30 million loss, the opportunity cost named, and a developed chain from more capital goods to higher capacity and an outward shift.

Calculation check

  • 450-350 = 100
  • 160-130 = 30
