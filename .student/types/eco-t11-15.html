Knowledge card

Income elasticity of demand and types of goods

Written
  • Income elasticity of demand (YED) measures how much quantity demanded responds to a change in income. YED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in income.
  • Normal good: positive YED. Demand rises when incomes rise and falls when incomes fall.
  • Luxury good: YED greater than 1. Demand rises by a bigger percentage than income, such as luxury holidays or streaming subscriptions.
  • Inferior good: negative YED. Demand falls when incomes rise and rises when incomes fall, such as cheap bus travel.
  • Firms use YED to plan output, staff, prices and marketing as incomes change; governments use it to predict spending and tax revenue.
  • YED is only an estimate: tastes, prices and competitors can change at the same time.

Before you begin

  • Income as a factor shifting demand (ECO-T11-08).
  • Percentages and dividing one percentage change by another.
  • PED (ECO-T11-12), which uses a similar formula.
1

The method

  1. Define YED: how strongly quantity demanded reacts when income changes.

    Why: This opens every longer answer.

  2. Calculate YED if data is given, or read the value supplied.

    Why: The value decides the type of good.

  3. Classify by sign and size: negative = inferior, positive = normal, above 1 = luxury.

    Why: Classification is what most short items test.

  4. Explain what rising or falling incomes do to demand for this good in the case.

    Why: This applies the idea to the context.

  5. Explain how the firm or government can use this (staffing, routes, stock, marketing). For 12-mark items follow the Evaluate card and weigh limits.

    Why: Significance and limits earn the higher levels.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Calling a good with negative YED a normal good because its demand changes.
  • ✗
    Mixing up YED (income) with PED (price).
  • ✗
    Saying all normal goods have YED above 1; only luxuries do.
  • ✗
    Classifying the good but not explaining what it means for the firm in the case.
  • ✗
    Ignoring the minus sign when calculating, which changes the type of good.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Define YED and calculate it: −15% ÷ 10% = −1.5.
  2. Negative, so bus travel is an inferior good; size above 1, so demand is very responsive.
  3. Explain what rising and falling incomes do to Megabus's demand.
  4. Explain how Megabus can use this: routes, buses, drivers, costs in three countries.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Income elasticity of demand (YED) measures how much the quantity demanded of a good responds to a change in consumers' income. It is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income. Using Megabus's research, YED = −15% ÷ 10% = −1.5. Because YED is negative, bus travel is an inferior good: as people become richer they buy less of it, probably switching to cars, trains or flights, and when incomes fall they buy more. The size, 1.5, is greater than 1, so demand is very responsive to income: a 10% rise in income causes a larger 15% fall in demand. This knowledge is useful to Megabus in several ways. If incomes are forecast to rise in one of its markets, for example the US, it knows demand for its buses will fall sharply there, so it can cut routes and the number of buses it runs, avoiding the cost of fuel and drivers' wages for empty seats. If incomes are expected to fall, for example during a recession in the UK, it can plan to add services and hire drivers in advance so it can serve the extra passengers and gain revenue. Because Megabus operates in Canada, the US and the UK, where incomes may change at different times, it can move buses and staff to where demand is growing. Knowing YED therefore helps Megabus control its costs and plan capacity, which protects its profit.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): YED defined and calculated correctly (−1.5), bus travel classified as inferior with the meaning explained, and developed uses for Megabus (cutting or adding services, planning staff and costs) across its three countries.

Calculation check

  • -15 ÷ 10 = -1.5

Evaluate · November 2020 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define YED and its formula; explain normal, luxury (YED above one) and inferior goods.
  2. Data: Amazon has 11.5% of Japan's streaming market, above Netflix but below dTV, U-NEXT and Hulu; annual membership 3 900 yen ($36.75), cheaper than rivals.
  3. Chain: streaming as a normal or luxury good -> rising incomes -> market grows -> Amazon's subscribers and revenue rise; YED helps forecasting, investment and marketing.
  4. Against: cheap subscription already affordable so YED may be low; richer consumers may trade up to dearer rivals; YED is only an estimate.
  5. Against: content, PED and rivals' prices may matter more.
  6. Judgement: YED is important for forecasting but only one factor; depends on switching behaviour and size of the income rise.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Income elasticity of demand (YED) measures how much the quantity demanded of a good responds to a change in income. It is the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income. Normal goods have a positive YED, luxury goods have a YED above one, and inferior goods have a negative YED.

Streaming services are likely to be normal goods, and perhaps luxury goods, because people tend to spend more on entertainment when they have more money. If YED is above one, a rise in incomes in Japan causes a larger percentage rise in demand for streaming. Amazon holds 11.5% of the Japanese market, so if the whole market grows quickly, Amazon's subscribers and revenue grow too, even if its share stays the same. Knowing its YED helps Amazon plan: it can forecast subscriber numbers, decide how much to invest in new films and shows, and plan its marketing. YED is therefore important for predicting revenue as Japanese incomes rise.

However, the importance of YED is limited by several factors. Amazon's annual membership costs 3 900 yen ($36.75), which is cheaper than its competitors. For many households this is already affordable, so a rise in income may have little effect on whether they subscribe, meaning YED for such a cheap product may be low. More worrying, as incomes rise consumers may switch from Amazon to dearer providers, such as dTV, U-NEXT or Hulu, which already have larger shares. Compared with its rivals, Amazon's cheap service could then behave almost like an inferior good, so its own demand might grow slowly or even fall. YED is also only an estimate based on past data, and tastes change. Other factors may matter more for Amazon's success: the range and quality of its films and shows, price elasticity of demand, and how rivals such as Netflix set their prices.

Overall, YED is important for Amazon Prime Video because it shows whether the streaming market will grow as incomes rise, and a growing market lifts Amazon's sales. But its importance depends on whether consumers stay with Amazon or trade up to dearer competitors as they become richer, and on how large the income increase is. Amazon's content and pricing are likely to matter at least as much as YED, so YED should be used alongside other information when planning.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines and explains YED types, applies the 11.5% share and 3 900 yen ($36.75) price in chains on market growth, forecasting and switching to dearer rivals; evaluates low YED for an affordable product, estimation limits and other factors; judgement depends on consumer switching and size of income rise.
