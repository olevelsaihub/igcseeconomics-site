Knowledge card

Poverty and inequality: meanings and why governments act

Written
  • Absolute poverty: a household's income is too low to pay for the basic needs for survival, such as food, clean water and shelter.
  • Relative poverty: a person's standard of living is below the typical (average) standard in their own society. In the EU it is often measured as income below 60% of median income.
  • Income inequality: income is spread unevenly, so there is a gap between what different groups of earners receive.
  • Low GDP per capita (low output per person) makes poverty more likely.
  • Governments want to reduce poverty and inequality so that basic needs are met, living standards rise, and for fairness (ethical reasons).
  • Do not confuse these terms with income distribution (the range of incomes) or the poverty trap (when benefits discourage people from working).

Before you begin

  • What income and standard of living mean
  • The idea of an average for a society
  • What GDP per capita measures

The method

  1. Decide which idea is asked: absolute poverty, relative poverty, or inequality.

    Why: Each has a different definition and the marks depend on the right one.

  2. For a definition, give both parts: absolute = income too low + basic needs for survival; relative = living standard below + the typical standard in that society.

    Why: Two-mark definitions give one mark for each part.

  3. For inequality, say income is unevenly shared + between groups or across society.

    Why: Both the 'uneven' idea and the 'who' are credited.

  4. For a reason to act, name it (meet basic needs, raise living standards, fairness), develop it, and use the case.

    Why: Explain answers give marks for the reason, development and context.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Swapping absolute and relative poverty.
  • ✗
    Defining relative poverty without saying what it is compared with (the typical standard in that society).
  • ✗
    Writing 'being poor' or 'having little money' without mentioning basic needs for survival.
  • ✗
    Giving a reason to reduce poverty but not developing or applying it in a 3-mark Explain.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
What is meant by4Two-part definition: absolute poverty needs both income and basic survival needs; relative poverty needs comparison with typical living standards in that society.
State1One short reason, such as making sure everyone's basic needs are covered, lifting living standards, or fairness.
multiple-choice4Match the wording: 'compared with the average person' is relative poverty; 'cannot meet basic needs' is absolute poverty.
Explain1Give one reason, explain how reducing poverty achieves it, and link to the case, for example the money given to low-income households.

What a complete response includes

Definitions earn one mark for each of their two parts (the condition and the comparison or basic needs). Explain answers reward one reason, a clear development and a link to the case.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the case: the Scottish Government is giving £37.2m to low-income households to tackle poverty and inequality.
  2. Pick one reason: to make sure basic needs are met.
  3. Develop it: low-income households may not afford food, heating or housing.
  4. Apply it: the £37.2m helps these households pay for essentials, so fewer people live in poverty.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One reason is to make sure that people can meet their basic needs. Households on low incomes may not have enough money for essentials such as food, heating and a safe home. By giving £37.2m to low-income households, the Scottish Government helps more families afford these basics, so fewer people in Scotland are left living in poverty.

How the marks are earned

3 marks (AO2): 1 for the reason (meeting basic needs), 1 for developing it (low incomes cannot cover essentials), 1 for context (the £37.2m paid to Scottish low-income households reducing poverty).
