Scarcity, choice and opportunity cost

Written

Wants are unlimited but resources are scarce, so every consumer, firm and government must choose. The opportunity cost of a choice is the single next best alternative given up.

Find scarcity, the choice and the opportunity cost in Lena's decision.

  1. Scarcity: limited money
  2. The choice made

Scarcity: limited money

Why
Lena wants more than $600 can buy.
Write it as
Lena has limited money, so cannot buy everything.

The choice made

Why
Scarcity forces her to pick one.
Write it as
Lena chooses the laptop.

Opportunity cost

Why
The phone is her next best alternative.
Write it as
The opportunity cost is the phone, as she cannot afford both.

Exam tipNot the trip: only the next best alternative counts. Name one item.

4

The method

  1. Start from scarcity

    Limited money or resources, but more than one want.

  2. Who chose what?

    Name the agent and their choice, from the question.

  3. Name one alternative

    Give one realistic next best alternative.

  4. Say why it is lost

    The budget cannot afford both.

  5. Match the command word

    Define: one sentence. Explain: point, two linked steps, context.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    A list of options

    Give one next best alternative, not everything else.

  • ✗
    The money paid

    The price is not the opportunity cost.

  • ✗
    Wants as needs

    Education, phones and holidays are wants.

  • ✗
    No reason given

    Say limited money means both cannot be had.

  • ✗
    Consumer or producer?

    A consumer buys for own use; a producer makes and supplies.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Read the context: Casper has decided to buy a new television.
  2. Think of a realistic item Casper might also have wanted: a laptop.
  3. Link to scarcity: his money is limited, so he cannot buy both.
  4. Write one point, then two linked expansions, using Casper's name.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One possible opportunity cost for Casper is a new laptop that Casper could have bought instead. Casper has only a limited amount of money, so spending it on the new television means he cannot afford the laptop as well. The laptop was his next best choice, so the use he would have got from it is what he sacrifices by choosing the television.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for identifying a specific alternative (the laptop). 1 mark for developing why it is lost (limited money, cannot afford both). 1 mark for context (Casper and the television).
