Knowledge card

Opportunity cost from a PPC diagram

Calculation

Moving along a production possibility curve (PPC) to make more of one good means making less of the other. The opportunity cost is the amount of the other good given up: old amount minus new amount, in units of that good.

  • Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
  • A production possibility curve (PPC) shows the most of two goods an economy can make with all its resources fully used.
  • Moving along the PPC to make more of one good means making less of the other good.
  • Opportunity cost on a PPC = old amount of the other good - new amount of the other good.
  • The answer is measured in units of the good given up, not in money.

The calculation

Opportunity cost = old amount of other good − new amount of other good

The other good is the one that falls when you make more of the good named.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find OC:OC = Old − New

To find New:New = Old − OC

To find Old:Old = New + OC

  • The answer is in units of a good, never in $.
  • Name the good given up, for example "units of capital goods".
  • Show the subtraction with numbers, not just the formula.

Try itOn an invented PPC, at 200 units of consumer goods the economy makes 720 units of capital goods. At 300 units of consumer goods it makes 620. Find the opportunity cost of the extra 100 units of consumer goods.

  1. Old amount of capital goods = 720.
  2. New amount of capital goods = 620.
  3. Opportunity cost = 720 − 620 = 100.
  4. Answer: 100 units of capital goods.

The method

  1. Find the start

    Read the current output of the good named, then read across to the other good.

  2. Find the new point

    Add the extra units, then read the other good's amount at that point.

  3. Subtract

    Write old − new for the other good, with the numbers.

  4. Name the good

    Finish with the amount and the good given up, such as "100 units of capital goods".

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong good subtracted

    Subtract the amounts of the good given up, not the good that increased.

  • ✗
    Bare number

    Say which good is sacrificed, or a mark can be lost.

  • ✗
    Formula only

    "OC = old − new" with no numbers scores nothing.

  • ✗
    Wrong starting axis

    Check which good the question names before you read the graph.

Worked explanation

  1. The question starts at 250 units of consumer goods. On the PPC, 250 consumer goods matches 500 units of capital goods.
  2. Making an extra 40 units of consumer goods moves output to 250 + 40 = 290 units of consumer goods.
  3. At 290 consumer goods, the PPC shows 400 units of capital goods.
  4. Capital goods given up = 500 - 400 = 100.
  5. Name the good given up in the final answer.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

At 250 units of consumer goods the economy makes 500 units of capital goods. At 290 units of consumer goods it makes 400 units of capital goods. Opportunity cost = 500 - 400 = 100 units of capital goods.

How the marks are earned

Two marks. The first is for showing the subtraction of the two capital goods readings (500 - 400). The second is for the right answer: 100 units of capital goods given up. A correct answer with the good named earns both marks.

Calculation check

  • 250+40 = 290
  • 500-400 = 100
