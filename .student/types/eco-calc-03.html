Calculating price elasticity of demand (PED)

Calculation

PED shows how much quantity demanded reacts to a price change. It is usually negative; ignoring the sign, above 1 is price elastic.

The calculation

PED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price

To find %ΔQd:%ΔQd = PED × %ΔP

To find %ΔP:%ΔP = %ΔQd ÷ PED

  • Quantity on top, price below.
  • Keep the minus sign.

5

The method

  1. Sign each change

    + for a rise, − for a fall.

  2. Quantity over price

    %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP.

  3. Divide or multiply

    Given PED? Multiply it by %ΔP.

  4. Round and sign

    Decimal places asked, keep the minus.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Upside down

    Price never goes on top.

  • ✗
    Sign dropped

    Lose the minus, lose the mark.

  • ✗
    % on PED

    PED has no unit.

What a complete response includes

One mark for the quantity change over the price change written with numbers; one mark for the final value with the minus sign and the requested rounding. Working without the minus sign keeps only the first mark.

Worked explanation

  1. Price increased by 1.6%, so the price change is +1.6.
  2. Quantity demanded fell by 2.1%, so the quantity change is -2.1.
  3. PED = -2.1 / 1.6 = -1.3125.
  4. Round to two decimal places: -1.31. Ignoring the sign it is above 1, so demand is price elastic.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

PED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in price = -2.1% / 1.6% = -1.3125 = -1.31 (to 2 d.p.).

How the marks are earned

Two marks. One mark for writing -2.1 over 1.6. The second mark is for -1.31, with the minus sign and rounded to two decimal places. The correct answer alone scores both.

Calculation check

  • -2.1 ÷ 1.6 = -1.3125
  • round(-2.1 ÷ 1.6, 2) = -1.31
