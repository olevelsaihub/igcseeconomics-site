Knowledge card

The four factors of production

Written
  • Production means turning resources (inputs) into goods or services that people want. A producer is anyone (a firm or a person) who does this.
  • A factor of production is a resource used to make goods and services. There are four: land, labour, capital and enterprise.
  • Land = all natural resources, for example water, oil, coal, wood, forests, fields, fish and minerals.
  • Labour = human effort, physical or mental, that people put into production, for example a farm worker, a teacher or a mechanic.
  • Capital = man-made items used to make other goods and services, for example machines, tools, computers, vehicles and factory buildings.
  • Enterprise = the entrepreneur: the person who has the business idea, organises the other three factors and takes the risk of losing money.
  • Profit, wages, rent and taxes are NOT factors of production; they are payments or rewards linked to the factors.

Before you begin

  • The difference between goods (things you can touch) and services (things done for you).
  • That firms combine inputs to make outputs.
1

The method

  1. Decide what the item is: something from nature, a person working, a man-made tool, or the risk-taking organiser.

    Why: Each of these maps to exactly one factor, so this one decision gives you the answer.

  2. Name the factor using the exam word: land, labour, capital or enterprise.

    Why: Marks are given for the correct factor name, not a description such as 'stuff from the ground'.

  3. For a 2-mark Describe, say what the item does in production, then say 'therefore it is classed as ...'.

    Why: One mark is for the reason it helps produce something, the second for linking it to the right factor.

  4. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: go through all four factors and tie each one to a real step in making the product in the case.

    Why: Top-level answers use the case details (what is picked, what machine is used, who checks quality) rather than general lists.

  5. Finish an Analyse answer by saying why the product needs all four together.

    Why: It shows the factors are combined, which is what the enterprise does, and completes the chain of reasoning.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Choosing profit or wages as a factor of production, when they are rewards paid to factors.
  • ✗
    Calling money 'capital' in the economics sense; capital here means man-made equipment used in production.
  • ✗
    Classifying a worker who repairs machines as capital because he works with machines; the person is labour.
  • ✗
    In a 6-mark Analyse, listing the four factors with textbook examples instead of the steps described in the case.
  • ✗
    Describing enterprise as 'the business' rather than the person who organises and takes the risk.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Short items reward the right factor or a clear definition. Describe: one mark for the role in production, one for naming the factor. Analyse: each factor matched to a real stage of the case, plus why all are combined.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the case: sea water, machines that filter out salt, production workers who take samples.
  2. Match each detail to a factor: sea water = land, filter machines = capital, workers taking samples = labour.
  3. Enterprise is not named in the case, so explain its role: the organiser who brings the others together and takes the risk.
  4. Give each factor a 'because' or 'so that' link to the production of drinking water.
  5. Close with why all four are needed together.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Production of drinking water in the Canary Islands needs land, labour, capital and enterprise. The raw material is sea water, which is a natural resource and so counts as land; without it there would be nothing to turn into drinking water. The salt is taken out by filtering the sea water through machines. These machines are man-made aids to production, so they are capital, and they allow large amounts of water to be made safe far faster than could be done by hand. People are still needed: production workers take samples to check that the salt and other impurities have gone. This human effort is labour, and it protects quality so that the water is safe for consumers to drink. Finally, someone has to organise the whole process: buy or rent the site, invest in the machines, hire the workers and take the risk that the water business may not make a profit. This is enterprise. Each factor depends on the others: the machines are useless without sea water, the water cannot be checked without workers, and none of them would be combined without an entrepreneur. So all four factors are needed for drinking water to reach consumers efficiently.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked out of 6 (3 for applying knowledge, 3 for analysing the case). Naming and defining the factors builds knowledge; matching sea water, filter machines and sampling workers to land, capital and labour shows application; explaining enterprise and why all four combine gives the developed analysis needed for Level 3 (5-6).
