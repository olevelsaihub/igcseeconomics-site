Knowledge card

Why countries protect trade: dumping, infant industries, jobs, safety

Written
  • Protection (protectionism): government measures that restrict imports to shield domestic producers or consumers.
  • Dumping: a firm or country selling exports abroad at a price below its cost of production.
  • Firms dump to get rid of surplus output or to drive foreign rivals out and win market share; protection stops this unfair competition.
  • Infant industries: new industries that have not yet grown big enough to compete; protection gives them time to grow and gain economies of scale.
  • Other reasons: to protect domestic jobs, to keep out unsafe or substandard goods, to raise tax revenue from tariffs, to reduce a current account deficit (when imports exceed exports) and to retaliate against another country's barriers.
  • Protection reduces free trade; it does not promote it.

Before you begin

  • What imports and exports are.
  • Cost of production and market share.
  • Economies of scale.
  • The current account in simple terms.

The method

  1. Name the motive: stopping dumping, helping an infant industry, saving jobs, keeping out unsafe goods, raising revenue or cutting a deficit.

    Why: The first mark is for a valid reason.

  2. Explain the threat imports pose (below-cost goods, new firms crushed, unsafe products, job losses).

    Why: The development mark explains the problem being guarded against.

  3. Show what happens if nothing is done, or how restricting imports removes the threat.

    Why: This completes the reasoning chain.

  4. Use the case: the product, country or event described.

    Why: One of the three Explain marks is for context.

  5. For a definition, give both parts: dumping is selling abroad and below cost; infant industries are new and not yet established.

    Why: Each part earns one of the two marks.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Defining dumping as just selling cheaply, without the below-cost and abroad ideas.
  • ✗
    Describing how a tariff works instead of why a country wants protection.
  • ✗
    Saying protection is used to promote free trade or to allow dumping.
  • ✗
    Explaining safety in general terms without linking it to the product in the case.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Motive: consumer safety.
  2. Data: in December 2018 UK consumers were warned about imported plastic toys containing banned chemicals, many sold online.
  3. Reason: foreign producers may not meet UK safety standards.
  4. Develop: unsafe toys could be bought without parents knowing.
  5. Consequence in context: children could be harmed, so restricting such imports protects UK consumers.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Some imported goods are made in countries with weaker safety rules than the UK, so they may not meet UK safety standards. For example, in 2018 imported plastic toys sold through online marketplaces were found to contain banned chemicals. Parents buying these toys could not tell they were unsafe, and children playing with them could become seriously ill, so the government needs to stop or restrict such imports to protect UK consumers.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: a valid reason (imports may not meet UK safety standards). 1 mark: development (unsafe products reach buyers who cannot tell). 1 mark: context (the toys with banned chemicals and the harm to children).
