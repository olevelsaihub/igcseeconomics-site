Why countries protect trade: dumping, infant industries, jobs, safety

Written

Protection restricts imports. Countries use it to stop dumping (exports sold abroad below cost), shield infant industries, save jobs, keep out unsafe goods, raise revenue or cut a deficit.

4

The method

  1. Name the motive

    Dumping, infant industry, jobs, safety, revenue or deficit.

  2. The threat

    What the imports do to firms or buyers.

  3. The fix

    How restricting imports removes the threat.

  4. Use the case

    The product or country named.

  5. Definitions

    Give both parts.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Just cheap

    Dumping is abroad and below cost.

  • ✗
    How, not why

    Explain the motive, not how a tariff works.

  • ✗
    Free trade

    Protection reduces free trade.

  • ✗
    Generic safety

    Link it to the product in the case.

Worked explanation

  1. Motive: consumer safety.
  2. Data: in December 2018 UK consumers were warned about imported plastic toys containing banned chemicals, many sold online.
  3. Reason: foreign producers may not meet UK safety standards.
  4. Develop: unsafe toys could be bought without parents knowing.
  5. Consequence in context: children could be harmed, so restricting such imports protects UK consumers.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Some imported goods are made in countries with weaker safety rules than the UK, so they may not meet UK safety standards. For example, in 2018 imported plastic toys sold through online marketplaces were found to contain banned chemicals. Parents buying these toys could not tell they were unsafe, and children playing with them could become seriously ill, so the government needs to stop or restrict such imports to protect UK consumers.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: a valid reason (imports may not meet UK safety standards). 1 mark: development (unsafe products reach buyers who cannot tell). 1 mark: context (the toys with banned chemicals and the harm to children).
