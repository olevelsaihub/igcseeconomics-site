Knowledge card

Why consumers may not maximise their benefit

Written
  • Economists assume consumers aim to maximise their benefit: to get the most satisfaction they can from their limited income.
  • Reason 1: consumers are not always good at working out the benefit of a product. They may lack information or be persuaded by advertising.
  • Reason 2: habits are hard to give up, for example sugary drinks or reaching for a mobile phone, even when people know the harm.
  • Reason 3: consumers copy the behaviour of others, such as friends or family, to fit in with a group.
  • When this happens, consumers spend money or time on something that gives them less benefit than another choice would.

Before you begin

  • Consumers have limited income and must choose (ECO-T11-02).
  • The idea that a price rise usually reduces quantity demanded.
1

The method

  1. State the assumption: consumers try to get the greatest benefit from their limited income.

    Why: This is the starting point that the reasons then challenge.

  2. Pick a reason that fits the case: poor calculation of benefit, habit, or copying others.

    Why: Each recognised reason is creditworthy; matching it to the case adds application.

  3. Develop how that reason leads to a choice that gives less benefit, using details such as prices, adverts or friends.

    Why: The development and context marks come from this chain.

  4. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: two or three reasons, each developed with the data, then a short conclusion.

    Why: Levels reward developed, data-based chains.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying consumers aim to maximise profit; that is the assumption about firms.
  • ✗
    Naming a reason without explaining how it leads to a worse choice.
  • ✗
    Ignoring case details such as a tax, adverts or the behaviour of friends.
  • ✗
    Arguing that consumers are always rational, which is the opposite of the point being tested.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Credit goes for a recognised reason (poor calculation, habit, copying), a developed chain to a poorer choice, and use of the case, such as adverts, a tax or peer behaviour.

Worked explanation

  1. State the benefit-maximising assumption.
  2. Reason 1: poor judgement of benefit, using the rise in obesity and more adverts.
  3. Reason 2: habit, using the 0.50 zloty ($0.11) per litre tax that has not stopped buying.
  4. Reason 3: copying others.
  5. Conclude that consumers get less than the maximum benefit from their income.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Economists usually assume that consumers spend their limited income in the way that gives them the greatest benefit. Buying sugary drinks such as Coca Cola and Fanta may not do this. First, consumers are often poor at judging the true benefit of a product. Obesity has been rising in Poland, but each drink tastes pleasant at the moment it is bought, while the harm to health builds up slowly and is hard to notice. The increase in adverts for soft drinks gives consumers an attractive image and little information about the health risks, so they overestimate the benefit. Second, drinking sugary drinks can become a habit. People used to the sweet taste find it hard to stop, so they keep buying even though the sugar tax has added 0.50 zloty ($0.11) per litre. This extra cost is only a small part of most people's income, so it is not enough to break the habit. Third, consumers may copy the people around them. If friends and family drink these products, a person may buy them to fit in rather than choosing a healthier drink that would give more benefit. Because of poor judgement, habit and copying others, consumers keep spending money on drinks that damage their health, so they do not get the maximum benefit from their income.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): the assumption is stated, three recognised reasons are each developed into a chain, and the case is used throughout (obesity, adverts, the 0.50 zloty tax, still buying).
