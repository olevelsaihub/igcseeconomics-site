The method

  1. Define it

    Compare government revenue with spending.

  2. Pick a consequence

    Surplus: repay debt, cut tax, spend more. Deficit: borrow, interest.

  3. Chain it

    Say who is affected: households, firms, services.

  4. Use the figures

    Quote the size or % of GDP given.

  5. Assess

    Weigh the other side, then judge.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Current account

    Exports and imports are a different balance.

  • ✗
    Reversed

    Surplus: revenue is bigger.

  • ✗
    No use given

    Say how the surplus is used.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the data: Nauru, population 10,824, had a fiscal surplus of 16.1% of GDP in 2019.
  2. Define a fiscal surplus.
  3. Chain 1: repay debt, so less interest in future.
  4. Chain 2: cut taxes, raising disposable income and spending.
  5. Chain 3: spend on healthcare or education, raising productivity and growth.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A fiscal surplus happens when a government's revenue, mostly from taxes, is greater than its spending over a year. Nauru's surplus was 16.1% of GDP in 2019, which is very large, so its government has a lot of spare money to use.

One benefit is that the government can pay back money it has borrowed in the past. Repaying debt means it pays less interest each year, so in future more of its revenue can go on services rather than on interest charges, making its finances safer.

A second benefit is that the surplus could be handed back to the 10,824 residents through lower taxes. Lower income tax would raise people's disposable income, so households could spend more and enjoy a higher standard of living, and local firms would see more demand.

A third benefit is that the government could spend the surplus on healthcare, or on education and training. Better-trained and healthier workers are more productive, which can raise Nauru's output and economic growth in the long run. Because the surplus is so large compared with GDP, Nauru may be able to do more than one of these at the same time.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked, 3 AO2 + 3 AO3. The correct definition and three developed uses (repay debt and save interest, cut taxes, fund services) give AO2; using 16.1% of GDP and the 10,824 population throughout gives AO3, reaching Level 3 (5-6).

Assess · June 2023 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(g)

Answer plan

  1. Define a fiscal surplus (tax revenue greater than government spending).
  2. Data: San Francisco expects a $108m surplus, its first since 1998, after heavy spending on homelessness and mental health; the Mayor wants more for police and public safety.
  3. Chain: spend surplus on safety and services -> more attractive city -> more business and jobs; or cut taxes -> more disposable income and local spending.
  4. Chain: repay debt -> lower interest payments -> more funds later; reserve for a downturn.
  5. Against: taxes may have been too high; surplus may reflect service cuts hurting low-income families and raising inequality; may not last.
  6. Judgement: beneficial overall; depends on how it is spent and whether it came from good management rather than cuts.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A fiscal surplus occurs when a government's tax revenue is greater than its spending over a year. San Francisco expects a surplus of $108m, its first since 1998.

A surplus brings several benefits. First, the city can choose how to use the $108m. It could spend more on key priorities such as homelessness and mental health, or on police and public safety, as the Mayor has called for. Better public safety makes the city more attractive to residents, tourists and firms, so local businesses gain customers and may invest, creating jobs. Second, the city could use the surplus to cut local taxes. Lower taxes raise households' disposable income, so they spend more in local shops, which boosts the local economy. Third, if the city has debts, the surplus can repay them. This reduces future interest payments, so less money goes on interest and more is available for public services later. A surplus also gives the city a reserve for hard times, such as a recession, when tax revenue falls. The Mayor says the surplus came from smart budgeting decisions, which suggests the city's finances are well managed and can raise confidence.

However, a surplus is not always a benefit. It means the city is taking more from taxpayers than it spends, so tax rates may have been too high, reducing residents' spending power. A surplus could also come from cuts to public services; this would harm low-income families who rely on them and could increase inequality. Keeping money unspent while problems such as homelessness remain may mean residents' needs are not being met. The benefit also depends on whether the surplus continues, since the first surplus in many years may not last.

Overall, a fiscal surplus of $108m is likely to benefit San Francisco, because it gives the city the choice to improve public services, cut taxes or repay debt. How large the benefit is depends on how the money is spent and on whether the surplus came from good management rather than cuts to services that low-income families rely on.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines a fiscal surplus and applies $108m, the first since 1998, and the Mayor's priorities in developed chains on services, tax cuts, debt repayment and reserves; balances with over-taxation, service cuts, inequality and sustainability; judgement depends on how the money is used and the surplus's cause.
