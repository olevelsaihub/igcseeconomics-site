Draw a minimum wage on a labour market diagram

Written

A minimum wage W1 above We is a horizontal line. Where it meets demand gives Qd, fewer jobs; where it meets supply gives Qs, more workers. The gap is a surplus of labour.

4

The method

  1. Find We and Qe

    Where D meets S.

  2. Draw W1

    A horizontal line above We, labelled.

  3. Read Qd

    Down from where W1 meets D.

  4. Read Qs

    Down from where W1 meets S.

  5. Check

    Qd is left of Qe; Qs is right of it.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Below We

    A binding minimum wage is above We.

  • ✗
    Shifted curve

    Draw a line; do not move D or S.

  • ✗
    Qd and Qs swapped

    Demand gives the smaller quantity.

  • ✗
    No labels

    Label W1, Qd and Qs.

Worked explanation

  1. Locate We and Qe on the given diagram.
  2. Draw a horizontal line at W1 above We, across both curves.
  3. Drop a dotted line from where W1 cuts the demand curve to the x-axis: Qd.
  4. Drop a dotted line from where W1 cuts the supply curve to the x-axis: Qs.
  5. Check Qd < Qe < Qs.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

I draw a horizontal line across the diagram at a wage rate above We and label it W1 (minimum wage). Where W1 meets the demand for labour curve, I draw a dotted line down to the quantity axis and label it Qd; this is to the left of Qe, because firms want fewer workers at the higher wage. Where W1 meets the supply of labour curve, I draw a dotted line down and label it Qs; this is to the right of Qe, because more people want to work at the higher wage. The gap between Qd and Qs is a surplus of labour.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the W1 line drawn above We and labelled; one for Qd, the smaller quantity where W1 meets demand, labelled; one for Qs, the larger quantity where W1 meets supply, labelled.
