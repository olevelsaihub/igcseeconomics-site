Knowledge card

Draw the effect of an import quota

Written
  • Quota: a legal limit on the quantity of a good that can be imported (or caught, for fish).
  • On the diagram a quota is shown as a vertical supply line at the permitted quantity. A vertical supply curve is called perfectly inelastic: the quantity supplied cannot change whatever the price.
  • A new quota set below the free-market quantity (Qe) gives a vertical line to the left of Qe: price rises and quantity falls.
  • A tighter quota (smaller than the old one) moves the vertical line further left; a bigger quota moves it right, lowering price and raising quantity.
  • The new price is where the vertical quota line meets the demand curve D, which does not move.
  • Price is on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis.

Before you begin

  • How to read a supply and demand diagram.
  • Equilibrium price and quantity.
  • What a quota is.
  • What perfectly inelastic means (a vertical line).
1

The method

  1. Read where the quota quantity lies: below Qe (or below the old quota) means left; a larger allowance means right.

    Why: The curve mark needs the vertical line on the correct side.

  2. Draw a vertical supply line at that quantity and label it, for example S1 (quota).

    Why: The examiner looks for a perfectly inelastic supply curve, labelled.

  3. Find where the vertical line crosses the demand curve D.

    Why: That point gives the new equilibrium.

  4. Draw a dashed line across to the price axis and label the new price P1.

    Why: One mark is for the new price, labelled.

  5. Label the new quantity Q1 where the vertical line meets the quantity axis.

    Why: One mark is for the new quantity, labelled.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawing a parallel shift of the sloping supply curve instead of a vertical quota line.
  • ✗
    Putting the vertical line on the wrong side when the quota is increased rather than introduced or tightened.
  • ✗
    Shifting the demand curve.
  • ✗
    Not labelling the new curve, price or quantity.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Cause: China introduces a quota on crude oil imports, limiting the quantity allowed in.
  2. The quota is below the current quantity Qe, so the vertical line goes to the left of Qe.
  3. Draw and label the vertical line S1 (quota).
  4. Where it meets D, read across to P1 above Pe; its foot on the axis is Q1, left of Qe.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

I draw a vertical supply line to the left of Qe and label it S1 (quota), showing that only a fixed quantity of crude oil can be supplied whatever the price. This line crosses the demand curve D above the original equilibrium. I draw a dashed line from this point across to the price axis and label it P1, which is higher than Pe. Where the vertical line meets the quantity axis I label Q1, which is lower than Qe. So the quota raises the price of crude oil in China and reduces the quantity bought and sold.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: vertical supply line drawn to the left of Qe and labelled. 1 mark: new price P1, higher than Pe, labelled. 1 mark: new quantity Q1, lower than Qe, labelled.
