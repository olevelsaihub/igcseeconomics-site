Knowledge card

Draw the effect of an import quota

Written

An import quota is a legal limit on the quantity imported. Draw it as a vertical supply line at that quantity: where it meets demand gives the new, higher price.

1

What goes on your drawing?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 parts to find

Free trade gives 60 000 tonnes. Kova sets a quota of 40 000 tonnes. Find the parts that earn marks.

  1. Vertical supply line
  2. Left of Qe
  3. Price rises to P1
  4. Quantity falls to Q1

Vertical supply line

Why
Only 40 000 tonnes can come in, whatever the price.

Exam tipNot a parallel shift. A quota line is vertical: perfectly inelastic.

Left of Qe

Why
40 000 is less than the 60 000 traded before.

Price rises to P1

Draw
Where the line meets D, dash across to the price axis.

Quantity falls to Q1

Draw
The foot of the vertical line on the quantity axis.
0 of 4 found

3 parts to find

The quota rises from 40 000 to 50 000 tonnes. Find what changes.

  1. Line moves right
  2. Demand stays put
  3. Price falls to P2

Line moves right

Why
More sugar is allowed in.

Demand stays put

Why
A quota limits supply, not what buyers want.

Price falls to P2

Why
The new line meets D lower down.

4

The method

  1. Which side?

    Quota below Qe or the old quota: left. Bigger quota: right.

  2. Draw it vertical

    At the quota quantity, labelled S1 (quota).

  3. Meet demand

    Find where it crosses D.

  4. Price

    Dash across to the price axis: P1.

  5. Quantity

    Label the foot of the line Q1.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Parallel shift

    A quota is a vertical line.

  • ✗
    Wrong side

    A raised quota moves the line right.

  • ✗
    Moving demand

    D stays where it is.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Cause: China introduces a quota on crude oil imports, limiting the quantity allowed in.
  2. The quota is below the current quantity Qe, so the vertical line goes to the left of Qe.
  3. Draw and label the vertical line S1 (quota).
  4. Where it meets D, read across to P1 above Pe; its foot on the axis is Q1, left of Qe.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

I draw a vertical supply line to the left of Qe and label it S1 (quota), showing that only a fixed quantity of crude oil can be supplied whatever the price. This line crosses the demand curve D above the original equilibrium. I draw a dashed line from this point across to the price axis and label it P1, which is higher than Pe. Where the vertical line meets the quantity axis I label Q1, which is lower than Qe. So the quota raises the price of crude oil in China and reduces the quantity bought and sold.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: vertical supply line drawn to the left of Qe and labelled. 1 mark: new price P1, higher than Pe, labelled. 1 mark: new quantity Q1, lower than Qe, labelled.
