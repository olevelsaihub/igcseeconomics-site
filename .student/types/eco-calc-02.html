Knowledge card

Excess demand and excess supply

Calculation
  • Quantity demanded (Qd) is how much buyers want to buy at a given price.
  • Quantity supplied (Qs) is how much sellers offer for sale at a given price.
  • Excess demand (a shortage) happens when Qd is greater than Qs: excess demand = Qd - Qs.
  • Excess supply (a surplus) happens when Qs is greater than Qd: excess supply = Qs - Qd.
  • If demand is given for several months or places, add them all before comparing with the total supplied.
  • Give the answer in units of the good (tickets, seats, meals), not money.

Before you begin

  • Meaning of demand and supply
  • Reading a supply and demand diagram
  • Adding several numbers accurately
1

The method

  1. Find total quantity demanded: read it off the graph at the price, or add all the demand figures in the table.

    Why: You must compare like with like over the same period.

  2. Find total quantity supplied for the same period (use the total if the question gives one).

    Why: Monthly supply must be matched with the same months of demand.

  3. Take the smaller from the larger and write the full calculation, including the addition.

    Why: Shown working earns the first mark.

  4. State the gap with its unit and say whether it is excess demand or excess supply.

    Why: Earns the answer mark and shows you know which side is bigger.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Comparing one month's demand with the three-month supply total.
  • ✗
    Subtracting the wrong way round and giving a negative answer.
  • ✗
    Reading quantity demanded from the supply curve on a graph (mixing up the D and S lines).
  • ✗
    Adding errors when totalling three large numbers.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Calculate10Write (sum of demand) - supply, or supply - (sum of demand), then the gap in units. Check which is bigger before subtracting.

What a complete response includes

First mark for the totals and the subtraction written out; second mark for the correct size of the shortage or surplus. A correct final figure alone earns both marks.

Worked explanation

  1. Add the tickets demanded for the three concerts: 4,200 + 6,000 + 3,250 = 13,450.
  2. The tickets supplied for all three concerts total 9,600.
  3. Demand is bigger than supply, so there is excess demand.
  4. Excess demand = 13,450 - 9,600 = 3,850 tickets.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Total quantity demanded = 4,200 + 6,000 + 3,250 = 13,450 tickets. Excess demand = 13,450 - 9,600 = 3,850 tickets.

How the marks are earned

Two marks. One is for the working that adds the three demand figures and takes away the 9,600 supplied. The other is for the correct excess demand of 3,850 tickets. The right answer on its own also scores both.

Calculation check

  • 4200+6000+3250 = 13450
  • (4200+6000+3250)-9600 = 3850
