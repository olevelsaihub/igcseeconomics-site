Knowledge card

Why governments regulate competition and mergers

Written
  • Governments regulate competition to promote competition, limit monopoly power, protect consumers' interests, and control mergers and takeovers.
  • A competition regulator (e.g. the CMA in the UK) can block a merger or takeover that would give one firm too much of a market.
  • A firm with a very large market share may charge higher prices, offer less choice, give poorer service and have less reason to innovate.
  • Lowering barriers to entry (e.g. making it easier for new taxi firms to start) increases competition, choice and keeps prices down.
  • Consumer protection laws can cap charges or ban unfair practices so that consumers are not exploited.

Before you begin

  • What monopoly, merger and takeover mean.
  • What market share and barriers to entry are.
  • Why competition usually benefits consumers.
1

The method

  1. State the aim: promote competition, limit monopoly power, protect consumers, or control mergers and takeovers.

    Why: The first mark is for a relevant reason for intervention.

  2. Explain the harm being avoided: a dominant firm can raise prices, cut quality or innovate less.

    Why: The development mark comes from showing why the aim matters.

  3. Tie it to the case: the firm's market share, the takeover price, the charge cap, the market (taxis, gaming, cable TV).

    Why: Context is the third mark in Explain and needed in Analyse.

  4. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: build more than one chain (prices, choice, service, innovation) from the data.

    Why: Level 3 needs developed chains using the data.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying the government 'does not want firms to grow' without explaining the harm to consumers.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to use the market share or case details.
  • ✗
    Confusing competition regulation with regulation of pollution (externalities).
  • ✗
    Giving the benefit to the firm rather than to consumers or the market.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Case: the CMA stopped Microsoft buying Activision; Microsoft already had about 60-70% of online video gaming.
  2. Reason: to stop monopoly power growing and keep competition.
  3. Develop: with even more of the market, Microsoft would face little pressure from rivals.
  4. Effect in context: gamers could face higher prices and slower innovation.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The CMA may have blocked the deal to prevent Microsoft gaining even more monopoly power (identify). Microsoft already controlled about 60-70% of the online video gaming market, so buying Activision would increase its market share even further, leaving rivals with very little of the market (develop). With few rivals to fear, Microsoft would have less pressure to keep prices low or to develop new games and services, so UK gamers could pay more and see less innovation (context).

How the marks are earned

1 mark for identifying the reason (prevent monopoly power or protect competition), 1 for developing it (removing a rival leaves little competition), and 1 for context and effect using Microsoft's 60-70% share and the impact on gamers.
