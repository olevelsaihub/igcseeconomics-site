Globalisation: what it means and what drives it

Globalisation is the world's economies becoming more connected and dependent on each other. Lower trade barriers, cheaper transport and communication, and MNCs drive it.

The method

  1. Meaning

    Economies more connected and dependent.

  2. Pick the driver

    Barriers, transport, communication, MNCs.

  3. Show the cost falls

    Doing business abroad gets cheaper.

  4. Carry the chain on

    More trade, so greater globalisation.

  5. Use the case

    Quote a figure or example.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Only "more trade"

    Say connected and dependent.

  • ✗
    Wrong-way drivers

    Higher tariffs slow it down.

  • ✗
    Benefits, not causes

    Answer why it grew.

  • ✗
    Chain stops early

    End at greater globalisation.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Read the driver: lower transport costs.
  2. Pull out the data: about 90% of goods trade goes by sea in containers, the largest ship carries over 20,000 containers, and shipping a t-shirt from Vietnam costs only $0.05.
  3. Reason: very large ships carry huge numbers of goods on one journey, so the cost per item falls.
  4. Develop: cheaper movement of goods means firms can buy from low-cost countries far away and still make a profit.
  5. Finish the chain: more goods cross borders, so countries become more connected, which is globalisation.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Container ships have become so large that one ship can carry more than 20,000 containers, so the cost of moving each item is tiny, for example only $0.05 to ship a t-shirt from Vietnam. Because transport now adds almost nothing to the price, a retailer in Europe can buy clothes from a low-cost producer on the other side of the world and still sell them cheaply. Many more goods are therefore traded between countries, which makes economies more connected and dependent on each other, so globalisation increases.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: a valid reason (big ships carry many goods at once, so the cost per item is low). 1 mark: development (firms can source from low-cost countries far away, so trade rises). 1 mark: context (the $0.05 t-shirt from Vietnam or the 20,000-container ships).
