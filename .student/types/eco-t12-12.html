Knowledge card

Why and how firms grow: mergers and takeovers

Written
  • Finance is the money a firm uses to run and expand, e.g. savings, retained profit or bank loans.
  • A takeover is when one firm buys control of another, usually by buying more than half of its shares.
  • A merger is when two firms agree to join together into one firm.
  • Factors that influence growth: access to finance, the chance of economies of scale, the desire to spread risk (selling in more markets or places), the wish to take over competitors, and government regulation.
  • Spreading risk: if sales fall in one market or location, income from the others keeps the firm going.
  • Buying a competitor can increase market share and control, reduce rivalry and give the buyer a share of the rival's profits.
  • Growth also brings risks: borrowing costs, diseconomies of scale, and new markets that may not have enough demand.

Before you begin

  • What profit and revenue are.
  • What economies of scale and market share mean.
  • That firms can borrow from banks.
1

The method

  1. Define the term if asked: takeover (one firm buys control of another), merger (two firms join), finance (money for the business).

    Why: Define items are one mark each for the core idea.

  2. Pick the growth factor that fits the case: finance, economies of scale, spreading risk, removing a competitor, or regulation.

    Why: Marks need a factor that the case supports.

  3. Develop how it drives or allows growth: e.g. a profitable past gives money to fund a second site.

    Why: Explain and Analyse credit is for the linked development.

  4. Use case facts: number of years trading, market shares, countries, markets entered.

    Why: Application to the data is needed for context marks and higher levels.

  5. For a 12-mark Evaluate, follow the Evaluate card: benefits of expansion, then risks (cost, diseconomies, weaker demand in the new place), then a supported conclusion.

    Why: Balanced judgement is required for the top level.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixing up merger (agreed joining) and takeover (one firm buys control).
  • ✗
    Naming a factor (e.g. finance) without showing how it affects the growth decision.
  • ✗
    Writing about why firms stay small when the question asks why they grow.
  • ✗
    In a 'two factors' Analyse, developing only one factor.
  • ✗
    In Evaluate, giving only benefits of expansion without risks or a conclusion.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Note the case facts: a martial arts club in District 16 of Budapest, six classes a week, successful for a few years, thinking of a second club elsewhere in the city.
  2. Factor 1: access to finance. Past success gives profit or a good record with banks, so money for a second site is available.
  3. Factor 2: spreading risk. A second district means income does not depend on one area.
  4. Develop each factor to show how it pushes the decision to grow.
  5. Name the growth factors as a group first to show knowledge.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Firms grow for several reasons, including access to finance, the desire to spread risk, the chance of economies of scale and taking over competitors. Two factors are especially relevant to Anastasia. The first is access to finance. Her club has done well for several years, so it has probably made profits that she could reinvest, and a good trading record makes banks more willing to lend to her. With this money she can pay for a second building, mats and equipment, and hire instructors, so finance makes the expansion possible. The second factor is the wish to spread risk. At present all her income comes from one club in District 16. If a rival club opened nearby, or families in that area cut back on spending, her revenue could fall sharply. Opening in a different district of the city would give her a separate group of customers, so a fall in demand in one area could be covered by income from the other, making the business more secure. Both factors make opening a second club an attractive choice for Anastasia.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked out of 6 (3 application, 3 analysis). Naming the growth factors shows knowledge; using the successful history and District 16 location applies it; two developed chains, finance paying for the second site and risk spread across districts, give Level 3.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Evaluate · June 2025 · Paper 1 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define expansion (organic growth); Řešení, successful since 2007 with a small, busy team and excellent reputation.
  2. For: 12% of businesses in Prague vs 3.5% in Brno -> far more potential clients; higher prices in Prague -> higher revenue and profit.
  3. For: spreads risk across two cities; economies of scale in advertising and administration; reputation helps raise finance.
  4. Against: busy team means hiring costs or lower quality in Brno; more competition in Prague; higher costs there; managing offices over 200 km apart -> diseconomies of scale; time to break even.
  5. Judgement: likely beneficial; depends on recruiting qualified staff, strength of competition and whether costs outweigh higher prices.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Expansion means a firm growing in size. Here it would be internal (organic) growth: opening a new office rather than merging with or taking over another firm. Řešení has been a successful accountancy firm in Brno since 2007, and Daniella is considering opening an office in Prague.

The main benefit is a much larger market. About 12% of Czechia's businesses are located in Prague, compared with only 3.5% in Brno. Since Řešení's customers are businesses, Prague has more than three times as many potential clients. Winning even a small share of them would raise revenue. In addition, prices in Prague are higher on average, so Řešení can charge a higher price for its services. More customers and higher prices together mean higher revenue and, if costs are controlled, higher profit.

Second, the firm already has an excellent reputation and its accountants are usually very busy. This shows strong demand that the firm cannot fully meet at present. A second office would let it grow and use its reputation to win new clients.

Third, operating in two cities spreads risk: if demand in Brno falls, the Prague office still earns revenue. A larger firm may also gain economies of scale, such as spreading advertising, software and administration costs over more clients, which lowers average costs. Its successful track record may also make it easier to borrow to finance the expansion.

However, there are risks. The small team of accountants is already very busy, so Daniella would need to recruit more qualified accountants, which raises costs. Moving existing staff to Prague could reduce service quality for customers in Brno and damage the firm's reputation. Prague has more competition from other accountancy firms, so winning clients may be slow, and higher prices there probably also mean higher rents and wages, which reduce the profit gain. Managing two offices over 200 km apart could cause diseconomies of scale, such as poor communication and coordination, which raise average costs. Opening a new office also takes time and money, so profits may take a while to appear.

In conclusion, expanding to Prague is likely to benefit Řešení because the market is much larger and prices are higher. However, the benefit depends on whether Daniella can recruit enough qualified accountants without harming quality in Brno, on how strong the competition in Prague is, and on whether higher costs there outweigh the higher prices. Starting with a small office would reduce the risk.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines organic growth, applies the 12% versus 3.5% business shares, higher Prague prices, the busy team and the 200 km distance in coherent chains (revenue, risk-spreading, economies and diseconomies of scale), weighs competition and costs, and gives a supported conclusion with clear conditions.

Evaluate · January 2023 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Q4(c)

  1. Define expansion (internal growth); NT Massage is small, family-run, with long-standing local customers; Mei wants a chain of shops in busier areas.
  2. For: more customers from the 7.5 million population plus tourists and business people -> higher revenue; higher prices possible in new shops -> higher profit.
  3. For: several locations spread risk; economies of scale (shared advertising in hotels, bulk buying); success may help raise finance.
  4. Against: heavy competition from many similar businesses; training and new shops cost time and money; diseconomies of scale from managing many shops; loyal customers may dislike losing the traditional service.
  5. Judgement: expand gradually if demand from tourists and business people is strong; depends on competition, finance and keeping service quality.
