The method

  1. Pick a reason

    Market size, niche, finance, owner's aims, flexibility or personal service.

  2. Develop it

    Say why it stops growth or helps the firm.

  3. Apply

    Use the owner and business in the case.

  4. Assess (9)

    Weigh small-firm benefits against what growth would bring, then judge.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Spot the key case fact: Heather's restaurant is in a small town in Portugal.
  2. Choose the reason that fits: the size of the market.
  3. Develop: a small town has a limited number of customers, and there may already be other restaurants.
  4. Link: a second restaurant would split the same customers, so it might not earn enough to be worth opening.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One reason is the small size of the market. Heather's restaurant is in a small town, so there are only a limited number of local people who eat out. A second restaurant in the same town would mostly share these same customers rather than attract new ones, so its sales might be too low to cover the extra costs of premises and staff, which is why Heather may decide to stay with one successful restaurant.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for identifying the reason (small market size), 1 for developing it (limited customers, a second restaurant would split them), and 1 for applying it to Heather's restaurant in a small town.

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Assess · January 2020 · Paper 1 · Q2(h)

Answer plan

  1. Define small and large firms and economies of scale.
  2. Data: handmade candles with soy wax and essential oils instead of paraffin; Elissa's aim is to help the environment; a larger firm offers facilities in return for a share of profits.
  3. Chain for small: niche product and personal service -> customer loyalty and higher prices; keeps control, identity and all profit; growth may end handmade production.
  4. Chain for large: more output meets demand and replaces more paraffin candles -> bigger environmental impact; bulk buying lowers average costs; stronger against new rivals.
  5. Judgement: staying small is better for now; depends on demand growth and whether quality and handmade identity can be kept when larger.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A small firm has few employees and a small share of its market. A large firm produces a much higher output and often benefits from economies of scale, which are falls in average cost as output grows.

Wonderful Wax may gain more from staying small. Its candles are handmade with soy wax and pure essential oils, so they are a niche product aimed at customers who care about the environment. A small firm can give a personal service and keep close control of quality, so customers trust the product and may pay higher prices, giving a good profit margin. If the firm used a larger firm's facilities to raise production, the candles might no longer be handmade, so Wonderful Wax could lose the feature that makes it different and its brand could suffer. Staying small also lets Elissa keep full control of the business and its aim of helping the environment. Under the offer she would have to share the profits, and the larger firm might want to change the methods to cut costs.

However, becoming larger also has benefits. A small firm may be unable to meet demand, so it loses sales and customers. With the larger firm's facilities, Wonderful Wax could make and sell many more environmentally friendly candles, replacing more candles made from harmful paraffin wax, which would serve Elissa's own aim. A larger scale would also bring economies of scale, such as buying soy wax in bulk, lowering average costs and perhaps prices, so more customers could afford the candles. A larger firm would also be better placed if competitors start making eco-friendly candles.

Overall, Wonderful Wax is likely to benefit more from staying small at present, because its handmade, niche identity is its main advantage and Elissa keeps control and all of the profit. However, this depends on demand: if demand grows beyond what a small firm can produce, and if the candles can stay handmade and environmentally friendly in the larger facilities, growing with the partner could achieve more of Elissa's goal of helping the environment.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines small and large firms and economies of scale, applies handmade soy-wax candles, Elissa's environmental aim and the profit-sharing offer in developed chains on niche pricing, control and identity; balances with lost sales, wider environmental impact, scale economies and competition; judgement depends on demand and keeping production handmade.
