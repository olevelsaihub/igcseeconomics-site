Exchange rates: meaning and what moves a currency

Written

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another. More demand for a currency makes it appreciate; more supply makes it depreciate.

The method

  1. Define if asked

    The price of one currency in terms of another.

  2. Demand or supply?

    Who buys the currency, and who sells it?

  3. Give the reason

    For example, a better return draws savers in.

  4. State the result

    Appreciates or depreciates.

  5. Apply

    Name the currency and the rate given.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Interest backwards

    Higher rates usually raise the currency.

  • ✗
    Imports as demand

    Buying imports adds to supply.

    Stopping early

    End at the change in value.

Worked explanation

  1. Data: in November 2018 South Africa raised interest rates by 0.25 percentage points to 6.75%.
  2. Demand side: foreign savers earn more in South African banks, so they buy rand to deposit; this is hot money.
  3. Supply side: South African savers have less reason to move money abroad, so fewer rand are sold.
  4. Result: higher demand and lower supply make the rand appreciate.
  5. Extra chain: speculators expecting a rise buy rand, adding to demand.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

When South Africa raised its interest rate to 6.75%, saving money in South African banks became more rewarding compared with saving in countries where rates are lower. Foreign savers and investors want this better return, so they move money into South Africa. To do this they must buy South African rand, so demand for the rand increases. Money that moves between countries in search of the highest interest rate is called hot money.

At the same time, South African savers have less reason to send their savings abroad, because they can now earn 6.75% at home. Fewer rand are exchanged for foreign currencies, so the supply of rand on the currency market falls.

With demand for the rand rising and supply falling, the price of the rand in terms of other currencies rises, so the rand appreciates. Currency speculators who expect this rise may also buy rand early to make a profit, which increases demand further and strengthens the appreciation. However, because the rise was only 0.25 percentage points, the size of the appreciation may be small.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked, 3 AO2 and 3 AO3. Level 3 needs a clear chain from higher interest rates to more saving in South Africa, higher demand (and lower supply) of the rand, and appreciation, using the 6.75% rate and the 0.25-point rise.
