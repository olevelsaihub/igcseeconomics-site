Knowledge card

The World Trade Organization: what it does

Written
  • The World Trade Organization (WTO) is an international organisation of member countries whose purpose is to promote freer trade between them.
  • Role 1: it hosts trade negotiations where members agree to cut tariffs, quotas and other trade barriers.
  • Role 2: it sets rules for international trade and monitors whether members follow them.
  • Role 3: it settles trade disputes, acting as a go-between (mediator) and ruling when one member complains about another's barriers.
  • Role 4: it persuades countries to reduce protectionism and encourages more countries to join.
  • The WTO does NOT lend money, write off debts, pay subsidies, regulate multinationals or fix exchange rates.
  • Less protectionism leads to more trade, which tends to bring lower prices and more choice for consumers.

Before you begin

  • What free trade and protectionism are.
  • What tariffs and quotas are.
  • The benefits of trade for consumers.
1

The method

  1. Name one WTO role: negotiations, rules, settling disputes, or pushing for lower barriers.

    Why: The first mark is for a correct role.

  2. Say how the WTO carries it out (bringing members together, ruling on complaints, monitoring rules).

    Why: This is the development of the role.

  3. Show the result: barriers come down or conflicts are resolved, so more trade happens.

    Why: The chain must end at the effect on trade.

  4. Apply it to the countries or dispute in the case, such as a threatened tariff.

    Why: Context is the third Explain mark and AO3 in Analyse.

  5. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: develop two or three roles, each linked to the growth of trade.

    Why: Level 3 needs thorough analysis with several developed roles.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Confusing the WTO with bodies that lend money or support currencies.
  • ✗
    Saying the WTO raises trade barriers or pays subsidies.
  • ✗
    Naming a role without explaining how it increases trade.
  • ✗
    Not referring to the countries or dispute given in the question.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Describe1A role plus how it is done, e.g. settles disputes by acting as a mediator between the countries.

Worked explanation

  1. Data: in 2017 the US government wanted a 300% tariff on aircraft imported from Bombardier, a Canadian firm.
  2. Role: the WTO sets trade rules and settles disputes.
  3. How: Canada can complain to the WTO, which judges whether the tariff breaks its rules.
  4. Effect: the tariff is removed or not imposed, so trade continues freely.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One way is through the WTO's rules and its role in settling trade disputes. If the US places a 300% tariff on Bombardier aircraft from Canada, Canada can take a complaint to the WTO, which acts as a referee and decides whether the tariff breaks the trade rules both countries have agreed to. If it does, the US can be required to remove or reduce the tariff, so Canadian aircraft can continue to be sold in the US and trade between the two countries stays free.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: a WTO role (setting trade rules and settling disputes). 1 mark: development (the WTO judges the complaint and can require the tariff to be removed). 1 mark: context (the 300% tariff on Canadian Bombardier aircraft).
