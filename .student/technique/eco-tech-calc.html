Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Economics · 4EC1

Calculate: show the working, then the answer with units (2 marks)

Pearson's meaning: use maths skills to reach an answer from the given data. Calculators may be used and working should be shown. Common 4EC1 calculations: percentages, percentage change, totals such as total revenue, trade balances, currency conversion and opportunity cost from a PPC.

Command word: CalculateUsually 2 marksAbout 2 minutes

What Pearson asks

Pearson's meaning: use maths skills to reach an answer from the given data. Calculators may be used and working should be shown. Common 4EC1 calculations: percentages, percentage change, totals such as total revenue, trade balances, currency conversion and opportunity cost from a PPC.

How the marks are earned

Two AO2 marks: one for the correct working with the right numbers put in, and one for the correct final answer. A fully correct answer with its unit earns both marks even with no working. Leaving off the unit or symbol (%, $, bn, 'units of capital goods') loses a mark. Writing only a general formula, with no numbers, earns nothing.

Method

  1. Find the exact numbers you need in the data and note their units (£bn, %, units).

    Why: Using the wrong figure loses both marks.

  2. Write the calculation with the real numbers in it, e.g. 45 * 1.15.

    Why: The working mark is for the numbers in place, not for a word formula.

  3. Work it out on the calculator and round as the question asks (often two decimal places).

    Why: Wrong rounding can lose the answer mark.

  4. Write the final answer with its unit or symbol, e.g. €51.75bn or 12.79%.

    Why: A missing unit loses a mark.

  5. Check it makes sense (is a percentage below 100? is the deficit negative?).

    Why: A quick check catches upside-down fractions.

Answer frame

Working: (numbers from the data with the operation) = (answer). Answer: (number to the required decimal places) (unit).

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Giving the answer without its unit or currency symbol.
  • Writing only the formula in words, such as 'exports times exchange rate', with no numbers.
  • Rounding to the wrong number of decimal places or rounding too early.
  • Turning a fraction upside down, for example labour force divided by unemployed.
  • For opportunity cost, giving the gained good instead of the amount of the other good given up.

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Each £1 buys €1.15, so multiply the pound value by 1.15. Working: 45 * 1.15 = 51.75 Answer: €51.75bn

Why it earns the marks

1 mark for the working 45 * 1.15 and 1 mark for the answer €51.75bn with both the euro sign and 'bn'. Without the unit only 1 mark would be given.

Question types that use this card

  • Finding the average (mean) of data
  • Fiscal balance: budget surplus or deficit
  • Total revenue: price times quantity
  • Wage income from an hourly rate
  • Excess demand and excess supply
  • Sorting costs into fixed and variable
  • Total cost from fixed and variable costs
  • Total revenue from direct or indirect taxes
  • Average cost per unit
  • Calculating price elasticity of supply (PES)
  • Converting currencies with an exchange rate
  • Opportunity cost from a PPC diagram
  • Percentage share of a total, including unemployment rate
  • Profit or loss from revenue and costs
  • Trade and current account balances
  • Calculating income elasticity of demand (YED)
  • Calculating price elasticity of demand (PED)
  • GDP per capita
  • Applying a percentage: interest, tax and tariffs
  • Percentage change, including inflation from a price index
