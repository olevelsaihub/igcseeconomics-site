Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Economics · 4EC1

Define and What is meant by: give precise definitions (1-2 marks)

Define: give the meaning of a term from the specification (usually 1 mark). What is meant by: define the term, and the definition must have two separate parts (2 marks). No example or context is needed.

Command words: Define, What is meant byUsually 1 (Define) or 2 (What is meant by) marksAbout 1-2 minutes

What Pearson asks

Define: give the meaning of a term from the specification (usually 1 mark). What is meant by: define the term, and the definition must have two separate parts (2 marks). No example or context is needed.

How the marks are earned

All AO1 (knowledge). For 'What is meant by', one mark goes to each of the two parts of the definition, such as what it is plus how it is measured, or what it is plus its cause or result. For 'Define', one accurate idea earns the mark. Other correct wordings are accepted.

Method

  1. Learn a two-part definition for every term in the specification.

    Why: Pearson's meaning of 'What is meant by' needs two separate parts.

  2. Part 1: say what the thing is, without using the word itself.

    Why: Repeating the term ('growth is when the economy grows') earns nothing.

  3. Part 2: add the second element: a time period, a cause, who it affects, or how it is measured.

    Why: This is the second mark.

  4. Stop after one or two sentences.

    Why: Examples and context earn no extra marks here.

Answer frame

(Term) is ... (what it is) ... (second part: over a period of time / caused by ... / relative to ... ).

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Using the term to define itself.
  • Giving only one part for a 2-mark 'What is meant by' question.
  • Giving an example instead of a definition.
  • Mixing up close terms, such as economic growth and economic development, or demand-pull and cost-push inflation.

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Economic growth is an increase in the real output of goods and services produced in an economy (real GDP), measured across a period of time such as a year.

Why it earns the marks

1 mark for the idea of an increase in the economy's output or GDP, and 1 mark for the second part: it is measured across a period of time.
