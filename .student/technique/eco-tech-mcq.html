Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Economics · 4EC1

Multiple choice: choose the one correct answer (1 mark)

Pearson's meaning: pick the right option from those offered. These test recall of specification content, or need a short calculation to reach the answer. In 4EC1 there are four options (A to D) and one is correct.

Command word: multiple-choiceUsually 1 markAbout 1 minute

What Pearson asks

Pearson's meaning: pick the right option from those offered. These test recall of specification content, or need a short calculation to reach the answer. In 4EC1 there are four options (A to D) and one is correct.

How the marks are earned

One mark (AO1 for recall questions, AO2 for applying or calculating) for the single correct letter. The wrong options are built from common mistakes, such as reversing a subtraction, adding instead of subtracting, or describing a related but different idea.

Method

  1. Read the stem and underline the key term or the numbers.

    Why: The stem tells you exactly what is being tested.

  2. Before looking at the options, say or work out the answer yourself.

    Why: You are less likely to be pulled in by a tempting wrong option.

  3. For a calculation, write the working on the paper and note the sign (surplus or deficit, rise or fall).

    Why: Wrong options often have the right number with the wrong sign or word.

  4. Cross out options you know are wrong, then choose from what is left.

    Why: Removing distractors raises your chance even when unsure.

  5. Mark only one box, and never leave it blank.

    Why: There is no penalty for a wrong answer.

Answer frame

My own answer first: ... . Option that matches: (letter). Options ruled out: ... because ... .

Common errors

Inferred, not measured: Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

  • Choosing an option with the right number but the wrong word (surplus instead of deficit).
  • Doing the subtraction the wrong way round, or adding when you should subtract.
  • Picking a related idea that is close but not the one asked (for example menu costs instead of shoe leather costs).
  • Changing a correct first answer without a clear reason.

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Trade balance = exports - imports = 19.9 - 44.2 = -24.3. The answer is negative, so the UK spends more on imports from Spain than it earns from exports to Spain: a deficit. Answer: C, a trade deficit of £24.3bn.

Why it earns the marks

1 mark for C. A has the right size but the wrong word (surplus); B and D add the two values instead of subtracting.
