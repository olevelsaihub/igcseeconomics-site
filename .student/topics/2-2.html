The global economy · Type 2

Globalisation: what it means and what drives it

What globalisation means (growing integration and interdependence of national economies) and why it has increased: fewer tariffs and quotas, cheaper transport, cheaper communication and the growing importance of MNCs.

The World Trade Organization: what it does

What the WTO does: hosts trade negotiations, sets and enforces trade rules, settles disputes, and pushes members to lower barriers, and how this promotes trade.

The global economy · Type 4

Why countries protect trade: dumping, infant industries, jobs, safety

Why countries restrict imports: to stop dumping, protect jobs and infant industries, raise tariff revenue, keep out unsafe products, reduce a current account deficit, or retaliate.

Currency market diagram (price of the currency against quantity) showing how exports, imports, interest rates or speculation shift demand or supply, with the new equilibrium exchange rate and quantity.

The global economy · Type 7

Exchange rates: meaning and what moves a currency

The exchange rate as the price of one currency in another, and how interest rates, speculators, trade flows and government currency dealing shift the demand for or supply of a currency.

The global economy · Type 8

Free trade: meaning, gains, losses and trade patterns

Trade without barriers and its effects (lower prices and more choice, cheaper inputs, wider markets, against foreign competition hurting domestic firms and jobs), the effects of cutting or removing tariffs generally, and the typical trade patterns of developed and developing countries.

The global economy · Type 9

Multinationals and FDI: why firms invest abroad, gains and costs

What an MNC and FDI are, why firms set up abroad (scale economies, resources, cheap inputs, lower transport/communication costs, new customers), and the advantages and disadvantages for host countries.

The global economy · Type 10

Tariffs, quotas and subsidies: how they protect, gains and costs

What tariffs, quotas and subsidies are, how each protects domestic producers, and the gains and costs of each (revenue, jobs, competitiveness, higher prices, retaliation, fiscal cost).

The global economy · Type 11

Trading blocs: what they are and their effects

What a trading bloc is (countries cutting barriers between themselves), named examples, and the benefits and drawbacks of membership for countries and firms, including tougher internal competition.

The global economy · Type 12

Who gains and who loses from globalisation

The gains and costs of globalisation for different groups: living standards, choice, prices, communication, closure of traditional industries, environmental harm and dependence on world markets.

Rising and falling currencies: effects on trade and the current account

Market-driven (appreciation/depreciation) versus government-set (revaluation/devaluation) changes in a currency, reading a change from quoted rates, and the effects on export and import prices, demand for exports and imports, and the current account.
