Knowledge card

Scarcity, choice and opportunity cost

Written

Wants are unlimited but resources are scarce, so every consumer, firm and government must choose. The opportunity cost of a choice is the single next best alternative given up.

  • Scarcity: people's wants never run out, but resources (land, labour, capital, enterprise) are limited, so not every want can be met.
  • Needs are things we must have to survive, such as food, water and shelter. Wants are things we would like on top of that, such as holidays, phones or education.
  • Because of scarcity every person, firm and government must choose. A society must decide what to produce, how to produce it and for whom.
  • Opportunity cost is the next best alternative that is given up when a choice is made. It is one single option, not a list and not the money price.
  • Economic agents: a consumer buys goods and services for their own use; a producer is a person, firm or country that makes and supplies goods and services; a government is the group with authority to run a country.
  • Every agent faces opportunity cost: a family choosing between two purchases, a firm choosing between two investments, a government choosing between a hospital and a school.

Before you begin

  • Goods are physical items and services are things done for people.
  • Resources such as land, workers and machines are used to make goods and services.

Scarcity: limited money

Why it matters
Lena wants more things than $600 can buy.
Write it as
Lena has only a limited amount of money, so she cannot buy everything she wants.

The choice made

Why it matters
Scarcity forces Lena to pick one item.
Write it as
Lena chooses to spend her money on the laptop.

Opportunity cost

Why it matters
The phone is her next best alternative, so it is what she gives up.
Write it as
The opportunity cost is the phone, because she cannot afford both.

Exam tipThe weekend trip is not the opportunity cost. Only the next best alternative counts, so name one item, not a list.

4

The method

  1. Start from scarcity

    The agent has limited money or resources but wants more than one thing.

  2. Who chose what?

    Name the consumer, firm or government and what they chose, using the question's names.

  3. Name one alternative

    Give one specific, realistic next best alternative they could have had instead.

  4. Say why it is lost

    Explain that the budget cannot afford both, so the alternative is given up.

  5. Match the command word

    Define: one clear sentence. Explain: a point, two linked steps, and the context.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    A list of options

    Opportunity cost is one next best alternative, not everything else.

  • ✗
    The money paid

    The price of the item is not its opportunity cost.

  • ✗
    Wants as needs

    Education, phones and holidays are wants. Needs are survival basics.

  • ✗
    No reason given

    Say that limited money means both cannot be had, or a mark is lost.

  • ✗
    Consumer or producer?

    A consumer buys for own use; a producer makes and supplies goods.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
multiple-choice5Check which option is essential for survival (a need) and which is extra (a want). Opportunity cost is always the next best alternative given up.
Define7One accurate sentence on the agent's role: buying for own use, making and supplying, or running the country; or wants as desire for goods and services.
Explain4Name one alternative given up, say both cannot be afforded, and use the person, firm or government from the question.
Describe1Give the reason (resources are finite) and develop it: demand for resources is greater than the resources available.
What is meant by1Two parts: the next best alternative, and that it is given up when a choice is made.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Read the context: Casper has decided to buy a new television.
  2. Think of a realistic item Casper might also have wanted: a laptop.
  3. Link to scarcity: his money is limited, so he cannot buy both.
  4. Write one point, then two linked expansions, using Casper's name.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One possible opportunity cost for Casper is a new laptop that Casper could have bought instead. Casper has only a limited amount of money, so spending it on the new television means he cannot afford the laptop as well. The laptop was his next best choice, so the use he would have got from it is what he sacrifices by choosing the television.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for identifying a specific alternative (the laptop). 1 mark for developing why it is lost (limited money, cannot afford both). 1 mark for context (Casper and the television).
