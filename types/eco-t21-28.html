The method

  1. Define

    The central bank changes rates or the money supply.

  2. Direction

    Raise to cut inflation; cut to raise output and jobs.

  3. Chain

    Borrowing and saving, spending, demand, prices or output.

  4. Currency route

    Higher rates lift the currency.

  5. Weigh

    Time lags, confidence, size of change.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Government sets rates

    The central bank does.

  • ✗
    Tax as monetary

    Tax is fiscal policy.

  • ✗
    Wrong direction

    Rates go up to cut inflation.

  • ✗
    Half definition

    Cost of borrowing and reward for saving.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Read the data: the Federal Reserve raised rates seven times in 2022 to 4.5%, highest in 15 years, forecast 5.25% by end-2023.
  2. Define monetary policy and say rates are raised to fight inflation.
  3. Chain 1: dearer borrowing cuts consumer spending and investment, lowering demand and demand-pull inflation.
  4. Chain 2: better reward for saving reduces spending further.
  5. Chain 3: stronger dollar makes imports cheaper, easing cost-push inflation.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Monetary policy means the central bank, here the US Federal Reserve, changing interest rates or the amount of money in circulation in order to influence total demand in the economy. To reduce inflation the Federal Reserve raises interest rates, as it did seven times in 2022 to reach 4.5%, with a further rise to 5.25% expected.

Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive. Households take out fewer loans and mortgages, and those with variable-rate debts have less left to spend, so consumer spending falls. Firms also face dearer loans, so they postpone investment in new machinery and buildings. With less consumption and investment, total demand in the US falls, which eases demand-pull pressure on prices and slows inflation.

Higher rates also increase the reward for saving. With rates at their highest for 15 years, more Americans may choose to put money into savings accounts rather than spend it, which cuts demand further.

Finally, higher US interest rates attract savings from abroad, raising demand for the dollar so its exchange rate rises. A stronger dollar makes imported goods and raw materials cheaper, lowering firms' costs and the price of imports, which helps reduce cost-push inflation as well.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked, 3 AO2 + 3 AO3. The correct definition and three developed chains (borrowing, saving, exchange rate) to lower inflation give AO2; using the 4.5%, the 15-year high and the 5.25% forecast gives AO3, reaching Level 3 (5-6).

Grade 9 long answers

Top-level 9-mark Justify and 12-mark Evaluate answers for this type. Written by olevels.com; not official mark schemes.

Assess · November 2025 · Paper 2 · Q2(g)

Answer plan

  1. Define monetary policy and output; the base rate was cut from 5.5% to 5.25% in August 2024.
  2. For: cheaper loans raise investment and household spending, so firms produce more.
  3. For: a weaker currency boosts exports; the first cut since March 2020 may lift confidence.
  4. Against: the cut is small and conditions are 'extremely tight'; confidence and investment are weak.
  5. Against: time lags and possible inflation reduce the gain in real output.
  6. Judge: partly effective; depends on further cuts and a recovery in confidence, possibly alongside fiscal policy.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Monetary policy means the central bank changing interest rates and the money supply to influence total demand in the economy. Output is the total amount of goods and services produced, measured by GDP. Cutting interest rates is expansionary monetary policy.

In August 2024 New Zealand's central bank cut the official base rate from 5.5% to 5.25%. Lower rates make borrowing cheaper, so firms may take loans to buy machinery and expand, which raises investment. Households with loans pay less interest, so they have more disposable income, and saving earns less, so they may spend more. Higher spending raises demand for goods and services, so firms increase production and may hire more workers, which could slow the rise in unemployment. Lower rates can also reduce the value of the New Zealand dollar, making exports cheaper abroad and raising output in exporting firms. As this was the first cut since March 2020, it also signals that the bank wants to support the economy, which may help confidence.

However, the cut is small, and the economist says monetary conditions are 'extremely tight', so borrowing is still expensive. Consumer confidence is weak and investment activity is falling. If households fear losing their jobs, they may save rather than spend, and firms may not invest if they expect low sales, whatever the interest rate. There is also a time lag: a change in rates can take many months to affect spending. Finally, if rates are cut a lot, spending could rise faster than supply and push up inflation, which reduces real incomes.

Overall, monetary policy is likely to be only partly effective in raising New Zealand's output in the short term. One small cut will not do much on its own. Its success depends on whether the central bank keeps lowering rates and, above all, on whether confidence recovers so that firms and households actually want to borrow and spend. Using it together with fiscal policy may give a stronger boost.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines monetary policy and output (AO2); full chains from the 5.5% to 5.25% cut through borrowing, spending, investment, exports and jobs (AO3); balances small size, tight conditions, weak confidence, time lags and inflation, ending in a judgement conditional on further cuts and recovering confidence (AO4).

Evaluate · June 2021 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define monetary policy, the PBOC's role and inflation; inflation reached 2.8% in July 2019.
  2. For: higher interest rates cut borrowing and spending, raise saving and may strengthen the yuan, lowering demand-pull inflation.
  3. Data: food prices up 9.1%, meat up 27% from animal disease, fresh fruit up 39.1% from severe weather.
  4. Against: these are supply shocks (cost-push), which rate rises cannot fix; they would slow growth and jobs.
  5. Against: time lags and higher firm costs; supply-side policies may tackle the cause better.
  6. Judge: effective for demand-pull inflation, limited here; depends on the cause of inflation.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Monetary policy means the central bank changing interest rates and the money supply to influence demand. In China this is the job of the People's Bank of China (PBOC), which aims to keep prices stable and to promote growth. Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level. In July 2019 China's inflation rate reached 2.8%, and it had been above 2% since March 2019.

To control inflation, the PBOC could raise interest rates. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive, so households buy fewer goods on credit and firms delay investment. Saving also becomes more rewarding, so people spend less now. Lower spending reduces total demand, which takes pressure off prices, so inflation should slow. Higher rates may also attract money from abroad and raise the value of the yuan, making imports cheaper. If inflation is caused by too much demand, this can work well, and a central bank can change rates quickly without needing new laws.

However, the data suggests much of China's inflation is not caused by demand. Food prices rose by 9.1% in a year, with meat prices up 27% because of infectious animal diseases and fresh fruit prices up 39.1% because severe weather hit supply. These are supply shocks: less meat and fruit is available, so prices rise. This is cost-push inflation, and higher interest rates do not create more meat or fruit. To bring prices down, the PBOC would have to cut demand across the whole economy, which would slow growth and could raise unemployment, conflicting with its aim of promoting growth. Higher rates also raise firms' borrowing costs, which some may pass on as higher prices. In addition, rate changes work with a time lag, so by the time they take effect the food supply may have recovered anyway.

There are other options. Supply-side measures, such as better control of animal diseases, deal more directly with the cause of the price rises.

In conclusion, monetary policy is effective against demand-pull inflation but is likely to be of limited use for China at this time, because the rise is driven mainly by food supply problems. Its effectiveness depends on the cause of inflation. If price rises spread beyond food into wages and other goods, a modest rate rise could stop inflation becoming established; for the food shock itself, supply-side policies are better.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines monetary policy, the PBOC role and inflation (AO2); develops the interest rate to spending, demand and exchange rate chain, then uses 9.1%, 27% and 39.1% to identify cost-push causes (AO3); weighs growth conflict, lags and alternatives, with a judgement depending on the type of inflation (AO4).

Assess · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Q3(e)

  1. Define monetary policy and expansionary policy: lower interest rates to raise demand.
  2. Data: China's central bank eased policy and cut interest rates to 4.35% because growth was slowing.
  3. Chain: cheaper loans and lower repayments raise consumer spending and firm investment, so output grows.
  4. Against: growth also depends on business and consumer confidence, and rate cuts work with a time lag.
  5. Against: if supply cannot keep up, extra demand causes inflation rather than growth.
  6. Judge: likely to help if confidence is reasonable and spare capacity exists; may need other policies too.

Evaluate · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

  1. Define monetary policy and output; GDP fell by 9.3% in 2020, the biggest fall for nearly 100 years.
  2. For: 11 rate cuts down to 4.25% make loans cheaper, raising spending and investment and so output.
  3. For: lower repayments give households more disposable income; weaker currency may help exports.
  4. Against: confidence may be too weak after such a large fall, and rate cuts take time to work.
  5. Against: inflation at 3.3% was above the 3% target, limiting further cuts; the bank held rates to assess.
  6. Judge: helpful but not enough alone; depends on confidence and combining with fiscal and supply-side policy.

Assess · June 2025 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q3(e)

  1. Define monetary policy; the ECB rate was 4.5% in December 2023 with unemployment at 7.2%.
  2. For: cutting rates makes borrowing cheaper, raising consumer spending and investment, so firms hire more workers.
  3. For: a weaker currency could make French exports more competitive and protect jobs.
  4. Against: inflation of 5.8% limits room to cut; weak Germany and slow China growth reduce demand for exports.
  5. Against: time lags, and unemployment may be structural, which lower rates cannot fix.
