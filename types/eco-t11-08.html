Knowledge card

What shifts the demand curve

Written

A change in anything other than the own price shifts demand: right if buyers want more at every price, left if less. With supply unchanged, a right shift raises price and quantity.

4

The method

  1. Name the factor

    Income, tastes, substitute, complement, adverts or population.

  2. Give the direction

    Right (more) or left (less).

  3. Say why

    Why buyers want more or less at every price.

  4. Equilibrium

    Right shift: price and quantity rise. Left: both fall.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Own price

    This moves along D; it does not shift it.

  • ✗
    Supply factors

    Costs, taxes and subsidies shift supply.

  • ✗
    Complements

    A cheaper complement raises demand.

  • ✗
    No effect given

    Finish with price and quantity.

Worked explanation

  1. Define what a demand curve shows.
  2. Identify the factor: the substitute (Amazon Prime) has gone and the remaining one (Sky) is much dearer.
  3. Explain why more fans choose Tennis TV at every price: demand shifts right.
  4. Show the effect: price Pe to P1, quantity Qe to Q1, and link to Tennis TV raising its price.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A demand curve shows the quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able to buy at each price. Changes in factors other than the good's own price shift the whole curve. One of these factors is the price of substitutes. Until the end of 2023, UK tennis fans could stream live tennis through Amazon Prime or Tennis TV. When Amazon Prime ended its service, Sky took over but at a much higher price, so the main substitute for Tennis TV became far more expensive. Tennis TV is now the only alternative to Sky for streaming most live tennis. Fans who want value for money are likely to switch to Tennis TV rather than pay Sky's higher price, so at every price more Tennis TV subscriptions are demanded. The demand curve shifts to the right, from D to D1. With supply unchanged, there is now excess demand at the old price Pe, so the equilibrium price rises from Pe to P1 and the equilibrium quantity of subscriptions rises from Qe to Q1. This fits the data, because Tennis TV raised its subscription price on 28 December 2023, just as the extra demand was arriving.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): demand curve explained, the correct factor (dearer and fewer substitutes) applied to the case, a right shift with a developed reason, and the rise in price and quantity linked to Tennis TV's price increase.
