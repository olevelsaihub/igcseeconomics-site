Knowledge card

What shifts the demand curve

Written
  • A shift in demand means consumers want more (shift right) or less (shift left) at every price. It is caused by things other than the good's own price.
  • Main factors: advertising, consumers' income, tastes and fashion (for example celebrities, health studies, reviews), the price of substitutes, the price of complements, and demographics (population size and age).
  • A substitute is a good people can buy instead of another one to meet the same want. If a substitute becomes dearer or disappears, demand for the other good shifts right.
  • A complement is used together with another good, such as motorcycles and helmets. If the complement becomes cheaper, demand for the other good shifts right.
  • With supply unchanged, demand shifting right raises equilibrium price and quantity; shifting left lowers both.
  • Costs of production, subsidies and indirect taxes shift supply, not demand.

The method

  1. Identify the non-price factor in the case.

    Why: Naming the right factor is the core of every answer form here.

  2. State the direction: demand shifts right (increase) or left (decrease).

    Why: Direction is what multiple-choice and State items test.

  3. Explain why consumers now want more or less at every price, using case details.

    Why: This reasoning is the development and context.

  4. Give the effect on equilibrium: price and quantity both rise (right shift) or both fall (left shift), with a diagram if useful.

    Why: The final link to price and quantity completes the Analyse chain.

  5. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card and use a labelled diagram (D to D1, Pe to P1, Qe to Q1).

    Why: A diagram supports the chain and shows understanding quickly.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Calling a change in the good's own price a shift of demand.
  • ✗
    Choosing supply-side factors such as costs or subsidies as causes of a demand shift.
  • ✗
    Getting complements the wrong way round: a cheaper complement increases demand.
  • ✗
    Stating the shift but not its effect on equilibrium price and quantity.
  • ✗
    Not using the case details to explain why buyers change their behaviour.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

multiple-choice6Rule out cost, subsidy and tax options (they shift supply) and the good's own price (a movement).

What a complete response includes

Credit goes for the right factor and direction, a reasoned chain about consumer behaviour from the case, and the resulting change in equilibrium price and quantity, often shown on a diagram.

Worked explanation

  1. Define what a demand curve shows.
  2. Identify the factor: the substitute (Amazon Prime) has gone and the remaining one (Sky) is much dearer.
  3. Explain why more fans choose Tennis TV at every price: demand shifts right.
  4. Show the effect: price Pe to P1, quantity Qe to Q1, and link to Tennis TV raising its price.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A demand curve shows the quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able to buy at each price. Changes in factors other than the good's own price shift the whole curve. One of these factors is the price of substitutes. Until the end of 2023, UK tennis fans could stream live tennis through Amazon Prime or Tennis TV. When Amazon Prime ended its service, Sky took over but at a much higher price, so the main substitute for Tennis TV became far more expensive. Tennis TV is now the only alternative to Sky for streaming most live tennis. Fans who want value for money are likely to switch to Tennis TV rather than pay Sky's higher price, so at every price more Tennis TV subscriptions are demanded. The demand curve shifts to the right, from D to D1. With supply unchanged, there is now excess demand at the old price Pe, so the equilibrium price rises from Pe to P1 and the equilibrium quantity of subscriptions rises from Qe to Q1. This fits the data, because Tennis TV raised its subscription price on 28 December 2023, just as the extra demand was arriving.

How the marks are earned

Level 3 (5-6): demand curve explained, the correct factor (dearer and fewer substitutes) applied to the case, a right shift with a developed reason, and the rise in price and quantity linked to Tennis TV's price increase.
