Written-answer check

Worked explanation

  1. Read the data: South Korea's unemployment for 15-29 year olds rose to 11.6%; reports criticise too many rules; experts suggest cutting employment rules in finance, transport and tourism.
  2. Define deregulation as a supply-side policy.
  3. Chain: lower hiring costs in services lead to more hiring; easier entry in finance leads to new firms and jobs.
  4. Weigh: firms may not hire, lower pay may reduce labour supply, lost protections, other causes and policies.
  5. Judge, stating what success depends on.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Deregulation means removing or reducing government rules and controls on firms and markets. It is a supply-side policy because it aims to make markets more competitive and to lower firms' costs.

In South Korea, international reports say there are too many rules, and experts suggest cutting employment regulations in services such as finance, transport and tourism. Employment rules add to the cost and risk of taking on a worker. If they are relaxed, it becomes cheaper for tourism and transport firms to hire, so they are likely to take on more staff, including young people. This could help bring down the 11.6% unemployment rate for 15-29 year olds. Removing rules that limit entry into finance could also allow new firms into the market; these new firms need staff, so jobs are created, and the extra competition may lower prices and raise demand further.

However, deregulation may not create many jobs. Firms might simply keep the lower costs as extra profit instead of hiring. If deregulation includes lowering the minimum wage, some people may decide work is not worth it, so fewer people would offer to work. Rules also exist to protect workers, consumers and the environment; removing them could mean less job security, unsafe conditions or more pollution, which lowers living standards. In addition, youth unemployment may be caused by other things, such as a skills mismatch or weak demand, which deregulation does not fix, so measures such as training or lower income tax could be more effective.

Overall, deregulation could reduce youth unemployment in South Korea's service sectors if rules really are the main barrier to hiring, but the effect is uncertain and it carries risks for workers. It is likely to work best as one part of a wider set of supply-side policies rather than on its own.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked, 3 AO2 + 3 AO3 + 3 AO4. Defining deregulation and chaining from lower costs and easier entry to jobs give AO2; using 11.6%, the named sectors and the expert advice gives AO3; firms keeping profits, labour supply, lost protections, alternatives and a conditional judgement give Level 3 (7-9).

Grade 9 long answers

Assess · November 2020 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(g)

Answer plan

  1. Define deregulation as a supply-side policy removing rules to raise competition.
  2. Data: unemployment for people aged 15-29 rose to 11.6% in May 2018; South Korea criticised for too many rules.
  3. For: fewer employment rules in finance, transport and tourism cut hiring costs, so firms employ more young workers.
  4. For: lower costs raise competitiveness and allow new firms to enter, creating jobs.
  5. Against: firms may keep savings as profit; worker protections lost; weak demand or skills mismatch limit hiring.
  6. Judge: some effect; depends on demand and the cause of youth unemployment; combine with training.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Deregulation means removing or reducing government rules that control how markets and firms operate. It is a supply-side policy, aimed at making markets more competitive and efficient so that the economy can produce more.

Deregulation could help reduce unemployment in South Korea. In May 2018 the unemployment rate for people aged 15–29 rose to 11.6%, and reports have criticised South Korea for having too many rules and regulations. Employment regulations add to firms' costs, for example through paperwork or rules that make hiring and firing difficult. If these rules are reduced in service sectors such as finance, transport and tourism, the cost and risk of employing workers fall. Firms are then more willing to hire, especially young workers with little experience. Lower costs can also make South Korean firms more competitive against foreign firms, so they win more sales and need more workers. Fewer rules on starting a business may encourage new firms to enter these sectors, creating further jobs.

However, deregulation may not be effective. Firms may use lower costs to increase profits rather than to hire more staff. Many regulations exist to protect workers, for example on hours, safety and job security; removing them could lead to worse conditions and less secure jobs, so any new jobs may be low quality. If demand in the economy is weak, firms will not hire more workers just because rules are simpler, since they have no extra sales to meet. Youth unemployment may also be caused by a mismatch between young people's skills and the jobs available, which deregulation does not fix. Other supply-side policies, such as education and training, may be more effective for young workers.

Overall, deregulation could help reduce unemployment in South Korea, particularly in finance, transport and tourism, by lowering the cost of employing people. However, its effectiveness depends on whether firms use the savings to hire, on the level of demand in the economy, and on whether youth unemployment is caused by regulation or by a lack of skills. It is likely to work best combined with training for young people.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines deregulation as a supply-side policy (AO2); chains fewer employment rules to lower hiring costs, competitiveness and new entrants in finance, transport and tourism, applied to 11.6% youth unemployment (AO3); weighs profits not jobs, worker protection, weak demand and skills mismatch, ending in a conditional judgement (AO4).
