Drawing a labelled concave PPC for two goods, then showing a move along it or a shift to a new curve.

Knowledge card

Draw a PPC showing a movement or shift

Written

A PPC is a curve bowed outwards from the origin with one good on each axis. Show a change in production as a movement from A to B along the curve, and economic growth as a shift to a new outer curve.

1

What goes on your drawing?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 parts to find

The firm makes more tables and fewer chairs. Find the parts that earn marks on the drawing.

  1. Axes: the two goods
  2. Concave, labelled curve
  3. B: more tables
  4. Dashed lines

Axes: the two goods

Why
Each axis shows the quantity of one good.
Watch out
Not price and quantity: this is not a demand and supply diagram.

Concave, labelled curve

Why
The curve bows away from the origin and touches both axes.
Label
Write PPC (or PPF) on the curve.

B: more tables

Why
B is to the right of A, so more tables are made.
And
B is lower than A, so fewer chairs are made.

Exam tipCheck the direction: the good that increases must be further along its axis at B.

Dashed lines

Why
They show the change in each good on the axes.
Label
Mark the readings, such as C1, C2 and T1, T2.
0 of 4 found

3 parts to find

The country's resources increase. Find the parts that show economic growth.

  1. PPC1: the old curve
  2. PPC2: further out
  3. Arrow shows the shift

PPC1: the old curve

Why
This is what the country could make before growth.
Label
Label it PPC1.

PPC2: further out

Why
Growth means more of both goods can be made.
Label
Label it PPC2, outside PPC1.

Exam tipLabel both curves. An unlabelled second curve does not show which way the economy moved.

Arrow shows the shift

Why
The arrow shows the curve moving away from the origin.

4

The method

  1. Label both axes

    One good on each axis, such as Chairs and Tables.

  2. Draw the curve

    Smooth, bowed outwards, meeting both axes. Label it PPC.

  3. Movement: A to B

    Mark A on the curve, then B further along towards the good that increases.

  4. Dashed lines and arrow

    Dashed lines from A and B to both axes; arrow A to B.

  5. Growth: PPC2

    Draw a second curve further out and label the curves PPC1 and PPC2.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong axis labels

    Use the two goods, never price and quantity.

  • ✗
    Wrong shape

    Bow the curve outwards, not towards the origin.

  • ✗
    B the wrong way

    Check the good that should rise really is higher at B.

  • ✗
    Shift for a movement

    If only the mix changes, stay on one curve.

  • ✗
    Unlabelled curves

    For growth, label PPC1 and PPC2.

What a complete response includes

Three marks: correctly labelled axes with a PPC, then one mark for each correct change (fall in one good, rise in the other), or for correctly placed inner and outer curves when showing growth.

Worked explanation

  1. Put Diaries on the vertical axis and Calendars on the horizontal axis.
  2. Draw a curve bowed outwards from the vertical axis to the horizontal axis and label it PPF.
  3. Mark point A high on the curve and point B lower and further right.
  4. Draw dashed lines from A and B to both axes and an arrow from A to B.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

I draw a vertical axis labelled 'Diaries' and a horizontal axis labelled 'Calendars', both starting at 0. I draw a smooth curve that starts on the Diaries axis, bows outwards away from the origin and ends on the Calendars axis, and I label it PPF. I mark point A on the curve, with dashed lines to D1 on the Diaries axis and C1 on the Calendars axis. Further down and to the right on the same curve I mark point B, with dashed lines to D2 and C2. C2 is to the right of C1, showing more calendars produced, and D2 is below D1, showing fewer diaries produced. An arrow along the curve points from A to B.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: PPF drawn with both axes labelled with the goods. 1 mark: diaries shown falling (D1 to D2). 1 mark: calendars shown rising (C1 to C2).
