Knowledge card

Draw elastic and inelastic demand or supply curves

Written
  • An elastic curve is drawn fairly flat (shallow): a small change in price causes a large change in quantity.
  • An inelastic curve is drawn steep: a large change in price causes only a small change in quantity.
  • Demand curves slope downwards from left to right; supply curves slope upwards from left to right.
  • Show the price change as P1 to P2 on the price axis and the quantity change as Q1 to Q2 on the quantity axis, using dashed lines from the curve.
  • Elastic: the gap Q1 to Q2 must look clearly larger than the gap P1 to P2. Inelastic: the gap P1 to P2 must look clearly larger than Q1 to Q2.

Before you begin

  • PED (ECO-T11-12) and PES (ECO-T11-13) categories.
  • Shapes of demand and supply curves (ECO-T11-07).
1

The method

  1. Check whether it is demand (slopes down) or supply (slopes up), and elastic (flat) or inelastic (steep).

    Why: Both the direction and the steepness must be right.

  2. Draw the curve with that slope and label it D or S.

    Why: One mark is for the correctly sloped, labelled curve.

  3. Pick two points on the curve and draw dashed lines from each to the price axis, labelled P1 and P2.

    Why: One mark is for showing the price change on the axis.

  4. Draw dashed lines from the same points to the quantity axis, labelled Q1 and Q2.

    Why: One mark is for the quantity change on the axis.

  5. Check proportions: for elastic, Q1 to Q2 is wider than P1 to P2; for inelastic, the reverse.

    Why: The relative sizes are what prove the elasticity.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawing elastic as steep and inelastic as flat.
  • ✗
    Drawing a supply curve sloping downwards.
  • ✗
    Showing changes on only one axis.
  • ✗
    Making the price and quantity gaps about the same size, so the elasticity is unclear.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to label the curve.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Demand slopes down; elastic means draw it fairly flat.
  2. Label the curve D.
  3. Choose two points close together in height but far apart sideways.
  4. Mark P1 and P2 close together on the price axis; Q1 and Q2 far apart on the quantity axis.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

On the axes I draw a fairly flat demand curve sloping gently downwards from left to right and label it D. I choose two points on the curve. From the higher point I draw a dashed line across to the price axis, labelled P1, and down to the quantity axis, labelled Q1. From the lower point I draw dashed lines to P2, just below P1 on the price axis, and to Q2, well to the right of Q1 on the quantity axis. The small gap between P1 and P2 and the large gap between Q1 and Q2 show that a small fall in price leads to a much larger rise in quantity demanded, so demand is price elastic.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: elastic (shallow) demand curve drawn and labelled D. 1 mark: smaller price change P1 to P2 on the price axis. 1 mark: larger quantity change Q1 to Q2 on the quantity axis.
