Knowledge card

Draw elastic and inelastic demand or supply curves

Written

Elastic curves are drawn flat: a small price change, a big quantity change. Inelastic curves are steep. Demand slopes down, supply slopes up.

4

The method

  1. Demand or supply?

    Demand slopes down; supply slopes up.

  2. Elastic or inelastic?

    Elastic: flat. Inelastic: steep.

  3. Draw and label

    Draw the curve and label it D or S.

  4. Both axes

    Dashed lines to P1, P2 and Q1, Q2.

  5. Check the gaps

    Elastic: Q gap wider. Inelastic: P gap wider.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Slopes swapped

    Elastic is flat, not steep.

  • ✗
    Supply sloping down

    Supply always slopes up.

  • ✗
    Equal gaps

    Make one gap clearly bigger.

  • ✗
    No label

    Write D or S on the curve.

Worked explanation

  1. Demand slopes down; elastic means draw it fairly flat.
  2. Label the curve D.
  3. Choose two points close together in height but far apart sideways.
  4. Mark P1 and P2 close together on the price axis; Q1 and Q2 far apart on the quantity axis.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

On the axes I draw a fairly flat demand curve sloping gently downwards from left to right and label it D. I choose two points on the curve. From the higher point I draw a dashed line across to the price axis, labelled P1, and down to the quantity axis, labelled Q1. From the lower point I draw dashed lines to P2, just below P1 on the price axis, and to Q2, well to the right of Q1 on the quantity axis. The small gap between P1 and P2 and the large gap between Q1 and Q2 show that a small fall in price leads to a much larger rise in quantity demanded, so demand is price elastic.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: elastic (shallow) demand curve drawn and labelled D. 1 mark: smaller price change P1 to P2 on the price axis. 1 mark: larger quantity change Q1 to Q2 on the quantity axis.
