Knowledge card

Demand, supply and movements along the curves

Written
  • Demand is the quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to buy at a given price over a period of time.
  • Supply is the quantity of a good or service that producers are willing and able to sell at a given price over a period of time.
  • The demand curve slopes down: when the price falls, quantity demanded rises (an extension); when the price rises, quantity demanded falls (a contraction).
  • The supply curve slopes up: a higher price makes selling more profitable, so quantity supplied rises; a lower price means quantity supplied falls.
  • A change in the good's own price causes a movement along the curve. It never shifts the curve.
  • To draw a demand curve from data, plot each price against its quantity, join the points with a downward-sloping line and label it D.

Before you begin

  • How to read values from a graph's axes.
  • Consumers and producers as economic agents (ECO-T11-02).
1

The method

  1. For a definition, include 'willing and able' and 'at a given price'.

    Why: These are the two creditworthy parts of a 2-mark definition.

  2. Read the graph: note the starting price and quantity, then the new price and quantity.

    Why: Quoting the figures gives the context mark.

  3. Say whether quantity rises or falls and that this is a movement along the curve, not a shift.

    Why: This is the effect the marks are looking for.

  4. Give the reason: cheaper compared with substitutes for demand, more profitable to produce for supply.

    Why: The reason develops the effect into a full Explain answer.

  5. For a 3-mark Explain, follow the Explain card: effect, then two linked expansions using the figures.

    Why: Keeps the answer focused on one effect.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying a change in the good's own price shifts the curve.
  • ✗
    Defining demand as 'what people want' without 'able to buy' and the link to price.
  • ✗
    Describing the effect without quoting the prices and quantities from the graph.
  • ✗
    Plotting points at the wrong price or quantity when drawing from data.
  • ✗
    Mixing up which way supply moves when the price falls.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What is meant by4Quantity consumers (or producers) are willing and able to buy (or sell), plus 'at a given price'.
Explain3State the change in quantity, quote the prices and quantities from the graph, and give the reason for the movement along the curve.
Draw1Plot each price and quantity pair accurately, join them in a downward line, and label the curve D.
multiple-choice1A price change moves along the curve; when price falls, quantity supplied falls and quantity demanded rises.

What a complete response includes

Definitions need willing-and-able plus price. Explain answers earn marks for the direction of change, the figures from the graph and a reason, recognising a movement along the curve. Drawing marks are for correct plotted points.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the graph: at $4.50, 150 units; at $5.00, 200 units; at $4.00, 100 units.
  2. Choose a price rise from $4.50 to $5.00.
  3. Work out the change: 50 more units supplied.
  4. Say it is an extension along the supply curve and give the profit reason.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

If the price of wheat rises from $4.50 to $5.00, the quantity of wheat supplied rises from 150 units to 200 units, which is 50 units more. This is an extension along the existing supply curve, not a shift of the curve, because only the price of wheat itself has changed. Farmers offer more wheat because each unit now sells for more, so growing and selling wheat becomes more profitable and they are willing to put more on the market.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the effect (quantity supplied rises when the price rises). 1 mark for using the graph figures ($4.50 to $5.00, 150 to 200 units). 1 mark for the reason (more profitable, movement along the curve).

Calculation check

  • 200-150 = 50
