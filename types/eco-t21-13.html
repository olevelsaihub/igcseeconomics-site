Knowledge card

The current account: exports, imports, deficit and surplus

Written
  • The balance of payments records all money flows between one country and the rest of the world. The current account is the part that records trade in goods (visibles) and services (invisibles), plus income flows such as wages, profits and interest earned abroad, and transfers such as money sent home by workers.
  • Exports are goods or services sold to other countries, so money flows in. Imports are goods or services bought from other countries, so money flows out.
  • Visible trade is trade in goods (physical items such as cars or oil). Invisible trade is trade in services (such as tourism, banking or transport).
  • Tourism is a service: foreign tourists spending in a country are an invisible export for that country; its own residents holidaying abroad are an invisible import.
  • Current account surplus: the value of exports is greater than the value of imports.
  • Current account deficit: the value of imports is greater than the value of exports.

Before you begin

  • The difference between goods and services
  • That countries trade with each other and pay in money
1

The method

  1. Decide whether the item is a good (visible) or a service (invisible).

    Why: This sorts half of every classification question.

  2. Follow the money from the named country's point of view: money coming in is an export; money going out is an import.

    Why: The direction is judged from the country whose account is named, not the other country.

  3. Combine the two answers, for example 'invisible import'.

    Why: Multiple-choice options always pair visible/invisible with export/import.

  4. For a definition, give two parts: what is traded (goods or services, or their value) and the direction or comparison (to or from another country; exports versus imports).

    Why: Each part earns one of the two marks.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Classifying tourism as visible trade; it is a service, so invisible.
  • ✗
    Judging direction from the wrong country: Kenyans holidaying in South Africa is an import for Kenya, not an export.
  • ✗
    Reversing deficit and surplus.
  • ✗
    Defining exports as 'goods sent abroad' and forgetting services, or leaving out the other country.
  • ✗
    Defining the current account as the government's budget; it records international trade, not tax and spending.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

One-mark items reward correct visible/invisible and export/import classification. Two-mark definitions reward naming goods or services (or their value) plus the cross-border direction or the export-import comparison.

Worked explanation

  1. Recall that the balance of payments has several parts; the current account is the trade part.
  2. Mark 1: say it is a record of transactions or of the value of exports and imports.
  3. Mark 2: say these are international, between this country and other countries.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The current account is the part of the balance of payments that records the value of a country's exports and imports of goods and services, that is, its trade transactions with other countries.

How the marks are earned

2 marks (AO1): one for the idea of a record of the value of exports and imports, one for making clear these are transactions with other countries (international trade).
