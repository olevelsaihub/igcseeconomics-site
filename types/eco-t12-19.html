Knowledge card

Draw a shift in a labour market diagram

Written

On a labour market diagram, a change in how many people can work shifts the supply of labour. Fewer workers: supply shifts left, wage up. More workers: supply shifts right, wage down.

4

The method

  1. Which curve?

    Who can work moves supply; firms' need for workers moves demand.

  2. Which way?

    More workers: right. Fewer: left.

  3. Draw S1

    Parallel to the old curve, labelled.

  4. New crossing

    Dotted lines from where S1 meets D to both axes.

  5. Label

    W1 on the wage axis, Q1 on the quantity axis.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    School-leaving age

    Raising it means fewer young workers: supply left.

  • ✗
    Shifting demand

    Only if firms' need for workers changes.

  • ✗
    Missing labels

    S1, W1 and Q1 each earn a mark.

Worked explanation

  1. Ask: does raising the school-leaving age change how many people can work? Yes, young people must stay in education longer.
  2. So fewer people are available: supply of labour shifts left.
  3. Draw S1 to the left of S, parallel to it.
  4. The new crossing with D is higher up and further left.
  5. Label W1 above We on the wage axis and Q1 to the left of Qe on the quantity axis.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

I draw a new supply of labour curve, S1, to the left of the original supply curve S, because a higher school-leaving age means fewer young people are available to work. S1 cuts the unchanged demand for labour curve at a new equilibrium. From this point I draw dotted lines to both axes: the new equilibrium wage rate W1 is higher than We, and the new equilibrium quantity of workers employed Q1 is lower than Qe.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the supply of labour shifting left and labelled S1; 1 mark for the higher equilibrium wage labelled W1; 1 mark for the lower equilibrium quantity of workers labelled Q1.
