Knowledge card

Draw a shift in a labour market diagram

Written
  • A labour market diagram has wage rate on the vertical (y) axis and quantity of workers employed on the horizontal (x) axis.
  • The demand for labour curve slopes down; the supply of labour curve slopes up. Where they cross is the equilibrium wage (We) and quantity of workers (Qe).
  • More people available to work (higher retirement age, lower school-leaving age, immigration) shifts supply of labour to the RIGHT: wage falls, employment rises.
  • Fewer people available (higher school-leaving age, lower retirement age, emigration) shifts supply of labour to the LEFT: wage rises, employment falls.
  • Label the new curve (e.g. S1), the new wage (W1) and the new quantity (Q1) on the axes.

Before you begin

  • How to read a supply and demand diagram.
  • The factors that affect the supply of labour (retirement age, school-leaving age, migration).
  • What equilibrium means.
1

The method

  1. Decide which curve moves: changes in how many people can work move supply; changes in firms' need for workers move demand.

    Why: Shifting the wrong curve loses the curve mark and usually the other two.

  2. Decide the direction: more workers available = supply right; fewer = supply left.

    Why: The direction fixes whether wage rises or falls.

  3. Draw the new curve parallel to the old one and label it S1 (or D1).

    Why: One mark is for the correctly shifted, labelled curve.

  4. Find where the new curve crosses the other curve, and draw dotted lines to both axes.

    Why: This locates the new equilibrium.

  5. Label the new wage W1 on the wage axis and the new quantity Q1 on the quantity axis.

    Why: One mark each for the correct, labelled new wage and quantity.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Shifting supply right for a higher school-leaving age; fewer young people can work, so supply moves left.
  • ✗
    Shifting the demand curve when the change is about who is available to work.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to label the new curve, wage or quantity on the axes.
  • ✗
    Marking the wage and quantity changes in the wrong directions for the shift drawn.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Ask: does raising the school-leaving age change how many people can work? Yes, young people must stay in education longer.
  2. So fewer people are available: supply of labour shifts left.
  3. Draw S1 to the left of S, parallel to it.
  4. The new crossing with D is higher up and further left.
  5. Label W1 above We on the wage axis and Q1 to the left of Qe on the quantity axis.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

I draw a new supply of labour curve, S1, to the left of the original supply curve S, because a higher school-leaving age means fewer young people are available to work. S1 cuts the unchanged demand for labour curve at a new equilibrium. From this point I draw dotted lines to both axes: the new equilibrium wage rate W1 is higher than We, and the new equilibrium quantity of workers employed Q1 is lower than Qe.

How the marks are earned

1 mark for the supply of labour shifting left and labelled S1; 1 mark for the higher equilibrium wage labelled W1; 1 mark for the lower equilibrium quantity of workers labelled Q1.
