Knowledge card

Draw a minimum wage on a labour market diagram

Written
  • The labour market diagram has wage rate on the y-axis and quantity of workers (labour) on the x-axis, with a downward-sloping demand for labour curve and an upward-sloping supply of labour curve.
  • They cross at the equilibrium wage We and quantity Qe.
  • A minimum wage W1 set above We is drawn as a horizontal line across the diagram at W1.
  • At W1, firms want fewer workers: read down from where W1 meets the demand curve to find the new quantity demanded (Qd), which is less than Qe.
  • At W1, more people want to work: read down from where W1 meets the supply curve to find the new quantity supplied (Qs), which is more than Qe.
  • The gap between Qd and Qs is a surplus of labour (unemployment).

Before you begin

  • How to read a supply and demand diagram.
  • What a minimum wage is.
  • What equilibrium wage and quantity mean.
1

The method

  1. Draw a horizontal line at a wage clearly above We and label it W1 (minimum wage).

    Why: One mark is for the new wage line, labelled, above equilibrium.

  2. Find where W1 meets the demand for labour curve and draw a dotted line down to the x-axis; label it Qd.

    Why: One mark is for the lower quantity of labour demanded, labelled.

  3. Find where W1 meets the supply of labour curve and draw a dotted line down; label it Qs.

    Why: One mark is for the higher quantity of labour supplied, labelled.

  4. Check Qd is left of Qe and Qs is right of Qe; you may mark the gap as excess supply.

    Why: This confirms the correct reading and shows the unemployment created.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawing the minimum wage below We; a binding minimum wage must be above equilibrium.
  • ✗
    Shifting the demand or supply curve instead of drawing a horizontal wage line.
  • ✗
    Swapping Qd and Qs: demand gives the smaller quantity, supply the larger.
  • ✗
    Not labelling W1, Qd or Qs.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Locate We and Qe on the given diagram.
  2. Draw a horizontal line at W1 above We, across both curves.
  3. Drop a dotted line from where W1 cuts the demand curve to the x-axis: Qd.
  4. Drop a dotted line from where W1 cuts the supply curve to the x-axis: Qs.
  5. Check Qd < Qe < Qs.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

I draw a horizontal line across the diagram at a wage rate above We and label it W1 (minimum wage). Where W1 meets the demand for labour curve, I draw a dotted line down to the quantity axis and label it Qd; this is to the left of Qe, because firms want fewer workers at the higher wage. Where W1 meets the supply of labour curve, I draw a dotted line down and label it Qs; this is to the right of Qe, because more people want to work at the higher wage. The gap between Qd and Qs is a surplus of labour.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the W1 line drawn above We and labelled; one for Qd, the smaller quantity where W1 meets demand, labelled; one for Qs, the larger quantity where W1 meets supply, labelled.
