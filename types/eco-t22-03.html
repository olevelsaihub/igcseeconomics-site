The method

  1. Whose view?

    The firm's motives, or the host country's gains and costs.

  2. Motive: link to profit

    Lower costs or more revenue.

  3. Effect: follow it through

    Jobs lead to income and tax.

  4. Use the case

    Firm, country and figures.

  5. Assess or Evaluate

    Both sides; judge it depends on the MNC.

Do not lose marks here

    Exporting as FDI

    Wrong viewpoint

    Host gains do not answer "why invest?".

    Weigh costs too.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Viewpoint: the firm's motives, not benefits to Bangladesh.
  2. Data: Mondelez, a food and drink MNC, opened its first Bangladesh factories in 2018 after 15 years of exporting there.
  3. Motive 1: producing inside Bangladesh removes long-distance shipping, cutting transport costs.
  4. Motive 2: labour may be cheaper, cutting production costs.
  5. Develop: lower costs allow lower prices or higher profit and a competitive edge.
  6. Motive 3: producing locally avoids import tariffs and exchange rate risk.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Mondelez had been exporting to Bangladesh for 15 years, so it already knew there was strong demand for its food and drink products there. By opening factories inside Bangladesh it no longer has to ship finished goods over long distances, so its transport costs fall.

Wages in Bangladesh are likely to be lower than in many countries where Mondelez already produces. Lower labour costs reduce the cost of making each product. Mondelez can then either keep its prices the same and earn more profit on each item, or cut its prices and win customers from rival firms, giving it a competitive advantage in the Bangladeshi market.

Producing locally also means Mondelez's goods are no longer imports, so it avoids any tariffs or other trade barriers Bangladesh puts on imported food. It also pays its costs and earns its revenue in the same currency, so a change in the exchange rate cannot suddenly make its products more expensive in Bangladesh. All these reasons make investing in Bangladesh more profitable than continuing to export.

How the marks are earned

Levels-marked, 3 AO2 and 3 AO3. Level 3 needs several clearly developed motives (lower transport costs, cheaper labour, competitive advantage, avoiding trade barriers and currency risk), each linked to Mondelez's profit and to the 15 years of exporting in the case.

Assess · January 2020 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q2(g)

Answer plan

  1. Define MNC; data: 24 new Japanese MNCs since 2016, 269 in total, including Honda and Nippon Koei.
  2. For: Nippon Koei invested $59.19m and hired 2,500 workers -> incomes, spending, multiplier, lower unemployment.
  3. For: local suppliers gain orders; training and technology raise skills; more tax revenue for public services.
  4. Against: MNCs came for low wages (China's costs four times higher) -> low pay, poor conditions; profits sent to Japan; local firms squeezed; steel pollution.
  5. Judgement: real benefits in jobs and tax; depends on wages, training and government regulation.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A multinational corporation (MNC) is a firm that produces in more than one country. Investment by MNCs is a form of foreign direct investment (FDI). Since 2016, 24 more Japanese MNCs have invested in Bangladesh, taking the total to 269, including Honda and the steel firm Nippon Koei.

The clearest benefit is jobs. Nippon Koei has invested $59.19m and recruited 2,500 employees for its steel factories. These workers now earn incomes, so unemployment falls and their spending rises. Their spending creates income for shops and other local firms, a multiplier effect that adds to GDP. Local firms may also supply the MNCs with raw materials, parts or services, so demand spreads through the economy. Higher incomes and profits increase tax revenue, which the government could spend on schools, health care and infrastructure. MNCs such as Honda may also train local workers and bring new technology, raising the skills and productivity of the labour force in the long run. Factory output may also be exported, earning foreign currency.

However, the benefits may be smaller than they first appear. The data says the main reasons for locating in Bangladesh are low wages and low production costs; costs in China are four times higher. This suggests MNCs come mainly for cheap labour, so wages may stay low and working conditions may be poor, limiting the rise in living standards. Senior jobs may go to managers brought from Japan, so local workers stay in low-skilled roles. Profits may be sent back to Japan instead of being reinvested in Bangladesh. Large MNCs may also take market share from local firms, and heavy industry such as steel can cause pollution that the government then has to clean up.

Overall, MNCs bring real benefits to Bangladesh, mainly jobs, incomes and tax revenue, as the 2,500 jobs at Nippon Koei show. How large the benefits are depends on whether MNCs train local workers and pay fair wages, and on whether the government regulates pollution and collects tax on their profits.

Why it reaches the top level: Defines MNC and FDI, applies the 24 new MNCs, $59.19m and 2,500 jobs in chains (incomes, multiplier, tax, skills), uses the low-cost motive in the data for a balanced counter-case (low wages, profit repatriation, pollution) and ends with a judgement that depends on MNC behaviour and regulation.

Evaluate · November 2023 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

Answer plan

  1. Define FDI; data: FDI up 17.8% to $22.1bn in 2022; top investors Netherlands, US, Switzerland, Belgium.
  2. For: manufacturing FDI up 36.9% and retail up 32.6% -> jobs, incomes, multiplier; local suppliers gain.
  3. For: technology and training raise productivity; transport FDI up 20.1% improves infrastructure; tax revenue and exports; less reliance on oil and gas.
  4. Against: low production costs may mean low wages; foreign managers; profits sent abroad; mining FDI up 11.7% may damage the environment; firms may leave.
  5. Judgement: mainly an advantage as growth is led by manufacturing and retail; depends on MNC behaviour and environmental regulation.
Grade 9 model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Foreign direct investment (FDI) is investment by a firm or government from one country into productive assets, such as factories or shops, in another country. In 2022, FDI into Kazakhstan increased by 17.8% to reach $22.1bn, with the Netherlands, the US, Switzerland and Belgium as the top investors.

The first advantage is jobs and incomes. FDI in manufacturing grew by 36.9% and in retail by 32.6%. New factories and shops need local workers, so unemployment falls and incomes rise. Workers spend their wages, which creates demand for other local firms, a multiplier effect that raises GDP and living standards. Local firms can also supply the foreign firms with raw materials, parts and services.

Second, FDI brings capital, technology and skills. Foreign firms often bring modern machinery and management methods and train local workers. This raises labour productivity and the long-run productive capacity of the economy. Growth in manufacturing is especially valuable because it helps Kazakhstan reduce its reliance on oil and gas. FDI in transport, which grew by 20.1%, can also improve infrastructure that all firms use.

Third, foreign firms pay taxes, which can fund public services, and goods made in Kazakhstan can be exported, improving the current account of the balance of payments.

However, there are drawbacks. Investors are attracted partly by low costs of production, which may mean low wages and poor working conditions. Foreign firms may bring their own managers, so local people get only low-skilled jobs. Profits may flow back to the investing countries instead of staying in Kazakhstan. FDI in mining grew by 11.7%, and extracting oil and gas can damage the environment, leaving clean-up costs for the government. Some firms may leave if costs rise or incentives end, so the jobs may not last. Large foreign retailers could also push local shops out of business.

In conclusion, the increase in FDI is mainly an advantage for Kazakhstan, especially because the fastest growth is in manufacturing and retail rather than mining, which points to more jobs and a more balanced economy. However, the size of the benefit depends on how foreign firms behave: whether they train local workers, pay their taxes and reinvest profits in Kazakhstan, and on whether the government regulates environmental damage.

Why it reaches the top level: Accurate definition of FDI; uses the 17.8%, $22.1bn and each sector growth rate in coherent chains (jobs, multiplier, technology, tax, exports); balanced drawbacks (low wages, repatriated profits, mining pollution, footloose firms); supported conclusion with a 'depends on' condition.

Evaluate · November 2021 · Paper 2 · Q4(c)

  1. Define FDI; data: new law in January 2019 simplified FDI rules; India ranked in the top 10 economies benefitting from FDI.
  2. For: capital investment in chemicals, software, telecoms, automobiles, construction, power and pharmaceuticals -> jobs, incomes, lower unemployment.
  3. For: skilled, English-speaking, low-cost workforce gains training and new technology; local suppliers gain; more tax and exports, market of over one billion people.
  4. Against: low wages and poor conditions; foreign managers take top jobs; local firms lose market share; chemicals and construction may pollute; profits sent abroad and tax avoided; MNCs may leave.
  5. Judgement: benefits likely large for India; depends on how MNCs behave and on regulation of tax and pollution.

Assess · January 2023 · Paper 2 · Regional (R) · Q3(e)

  1. Define MNC; data: 2,000 large firms may owe as much as GBP 34.8bn in tax for 2019 to 2020, up from GBP 29.9bn in 2018 to 2019.
  2. Drawback: MNCs move profits to lower-tax countries -> lost tax revenue -> less spending on education and health care or higher taxes for others.
  3. Drawback: MNCs may damage the environment and squeeze out smaller UK firms.
  4. Other side: MNCs create jobs, buy from local suppliers, and many pay their taxes; penalties may recover some of the money.
  5. Judgement: tax avoidance is a serious and growing drawback; depends on the individual MNC and on how well the government enforces tax rules.

Assess · November 2025 · Paper 2 · Q3(e)

  1. Define MNC; data: Levi Strauss & Co has 118 factories across 16 countries.
  2. For: jobs and incomes; worker wellbeing programme (health education, family welfare, financial empowerment) -> healthier, more productive workforce.
  3. For: water-saving processes saved nearly 13 billion litres over nine years and the net-zero target by 2050 reduce environmental harm; technology and tax revenue for host countries.
  4. Against: profits may flow back to the home country; local firms may struggle to compete; conditions may still be poor despite programmes; MNCs may influence governments.
  5. Judgement: advantages are real where MNCs act responsibly like this; depends on whether such behaviour is typical and on host-country regulation.
