Knowledge card

Globalisation: what it means and what drives it

Written
  • Globalisation: the world's economies becoming more connected (integrated) and more dependent on each other (interdependent).
  • It shows up as more international trade, more cross-border investment and more firms operating in many countries.
  • Driver 1: fewer trade barriers. A tariff is a tax on imports and a quota is a limit on the amount imported; cutting them makes trading abroad cheaper and easier.
  • Driver 2: cheaper transport, for example huge container ships and faster air freight, lowers the cost of moving each item between countries.
  • Driver 3: cheaper communication, for example the internet, email, video calls and online marketplaces, lets firms find and serve customers and suppliers far away.
  • Driver 4: the growing importance of multinational corporations (MNCs), firms that produce or sell in more than one country.
  • Anything that makes trade dearer or harder (more tariffs, higher transport or communication costs, weaker MNCs) slows globalisation.

Before you begin

  • What imports and exports are.
  • What a tariff and a quota are.
  • What a firm's costs are and why lower costs encourage firms to do more.
  • What a multinational corporation is.
1

The method

  1. If asked for the meaning, say economies are becoming more connected and more dependent on each other around the world.

    Why: The two marks go for the linking idea and for the world's economies.

  2. Pick out the driver named in the question (transport, communication, trade barriers or MNCs).

    Why: The answer must be about that driver, not globalisation in general.

  3. Show how the driver cuts the cost or difficulty of doing business across borders.

    Why: This is the first link in the chain the examiner rewards.

  4. Carry the chain on: lower cost leads to more trade, more sourcing from abroad or more firms selling overseas, which is greater globalisation.

    Why: The final link back to globalisation completes the explanation.

  5. Use a figure or example from the case, such as the cost of shipping one item or a named online marketplace.

    Why: One mark in an Explain, and much of the AO3 credit in an Analyse, is for context.

  6. For a 6-mark Analyse, follow the Analyse card: write two or three developed chains, each ending at more global integration, using the data.

    Why: Level 3 needs clear, well-organised chains applied to the data.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Defining globalisation only as 'more trade' without the idea of economies becoming connected or dependent on each other.
  • ✗
    Choosing an option such as higher tariffs or higher transport costs as a cause of globalisation; these would reduce it.
  • ✗
    Explaining the benefits of globalisation when the question asks why it has increased.
  • ✗
    Stopping at 'transport is cheaper' without the chain to more trade and greater globalisation.
  • ✗
    Ignoring the case data, losing the context mark in a 3-mark Explain.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write

Describe1State the effect on globalisation (it rises) and add one reason why, such as firms can trade more easily.

What a complete response includes

Marks reward the connection-and-interdependence idea, a valid driver, a chain from that driver to cheaper or easier cross-border business and then to more trade and integration, and use of the case information.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the driver: lower transport costs.
  2. Pull out the data: about 90% of goods trade goes by sea in containers, the largest ship carries over 20,000 containers, and shipping a t-shirt from Vietnam costs only $0.05.
  3. Reason: very large ships carry huge numbers of goods on one journey, so the cost per item falls.
  4. Develop: cheaper movement of goods means firms can buy from low-cost countries far away and still make a profit.
  5. Finish the chain: more goods cross borders, so countries become more connected, which is globalisation.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Container ships have become so large that one ship can carry more than 20,000 containers, so the cost of moving each item is tiny, for example only $0.05 to ship a t-shirt from Vietnam. Because transport now adds almost nothing to the price, a retailer in Europe can buy clothes from a low-cost producer on the other side of the world and still sell them cheaply. Many more goods are therefore traded between countries, which makes economies more connected and dependent on each other, so globalisation increases.

How the marks are earned

1 mark: a valid reason (big ships carry many goods at once, so the cost per item is low). 1 mark: development (firms can source from low-cost countries far away, so trade rises). 1 mark: context (the $0.05 t-shirt from Vietnam or the 20,000-container ships).
